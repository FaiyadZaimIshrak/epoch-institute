# Empire profile changes

Fact-check and polish of all 94 empire profiles: factual corrections, added specifics, British spelling and style.

## Abbasid Caliphate

- FACT: "Turkic ghilman army created by al-Mu'tasim in 833" -> "built up by al-Mu'tasim" (he began recruiting Turkic slave soldiers as a prince, well before his accession in 833).
- FACT: Barmakids "served under al-Mansur and Harun al-Rashid" as viziers -> served the early caliphs and peaked as viziers under Harun until their fall in 803 (their vizierate proper belongs to Harun's reign).
- FACT: Al-Khwarizmi's "invention of algebra" -> the treatise that gave algebra its name, dated c. 820 (exact date uncertain).
- FACT: "Hulagu's forces breached the walls on 10 February 1258" -> "took the city in February 1258" (walls were breached earlier in the month; the surrender/entry date is debated).
- FACT: Baghdad "the largest city in the world" -> "one of the largest" (contested claim); Ctesiphon "near" -> "not far from" (c. 30 km away); "zero political authority" -> "little or no".
- FACT: Abu Muslim "a Persian commander" -> "of obscure but probably Persian origin" (his origins are disputed).
- ADDED: move of capital to Samarra (836); assassination of al-Mutawakkil starting the Anarchy; the mihna; Seljuks replacing Buyids in 1055; fall of the Barmakids (803).
- STYLE: removed the quoted Britannica citation (no references allowed); British spelling (marginalised); italicised Arabic terms; removed repetition in summary. No years removed except the exact day "10 February".

## Achaemenid Empire

- FACT: "permanent loss of Egypt in 404 BCE" -> loss of Egypt for six decades, reconquered by Artaxerxes III in 343 BCE (Egypt was recovered before Alexander; the original also contradicted its own "61 years").
- FACT: Median inheritance "modern Iran, Azerbaijan, and much of Anatolia" -> "the Iranian plateau to eastern Anatolia as far as the Halys" (Media never held much of Anatolia; Lydia held the west).
- FACT: "encompassing an estimated 44 percent of the world's population" -> "a large proportion ... (modern estimates vary widely)" (popular figure without sound basis).
- FACT: "great revolts of 522–520 BCE" -> "revolts that followed his accession in 522 BCE" (main revolts 522–521; chronology varies).
- FACT: Lydia campaign "Battle of Thymbra in 546 BCE" -> "traditionally at the Battle of Thymbra ... around 547–546 BCE" (date and battle name uncertain).
- FACT: Persepolis burned "early 330" -> "330 BCE" (burning usually dated to spring/May 330); Artaxerxes III "assassination" specified as poisoning.
- ADDED: Salamis (480 BCE); Cunaxa; silver siglos; Nile–Red Sea canal under Darius; Artaxerxes III's reconquest of Egypt (343 BCE).
- STYLE: hedged Herodotean claims (twenty satrapies, Immortals at exactly ten thousand); softened "first truly multicultural" and "earliest declaration of religious tolerance"; removed duplicated empire name in summary; "229-year empire" rephrased as "more than two centuries". Removed the date "early" qualifier only; no years removed.

## Adal Sultanate

- FACT: Ahmad's figurehead sultans "first Abu Bakr ibn Muhammad, then his brother Umar Din" -> Ahmad opposed and killed Abu Bakr (c. 1525) and installed Umar Din as figurehead (Abu Bakr was a rival, not a puppet).
- FACT: Monasteries destroyed "at Debre Damo, Lalibela, and Lake Tana" -> "Debre Libanos and Atronsa Maryam and the island monasteries of Lake Tana" (Ahmad failed to take Debre Damo; Lalibela's churches survived).
- FACT: Decline listed "execution of Sultan Muhammad V in 1577" and separately "death of Muhammad ibn Nasir at the Battle of Webi River" -> one event: Sarsa Dengel defeated and executed Sultan Muhammad ibn Nasir near the Webi in 1576; withdrawal to Aussa in 1577.
- FACT: Sabr ad-Din reconstituted the state "at Zeila" -> seat at Dakkar with Zeila as port; capital "shifted from Zeila to Dakar" -> at Dakkar until moved to Harar in 1520 by Abu Bakr; Harar capital "from the mid-fifteenth century" -> "early sixteenth century".
- FACT: Sa'ad ad-Din II "captured and executed by Dawit I around 1410" -> defeated and killed near Zeila by Ethiopian forces, date given variously c. 1403–1415 (disputed).
- FACT: "Within weeks, three-quarters of Ethiopia had been liberated" -> highlands recovered "within a few years".
- FACT: Ethiopian–Adal War "1527–1543" -> 1529–1543 in impact (campaign years 1527–1543 kept in socioeconomy for raids); specific troop numbers at Shimbra Kure (5,000–6,000 vs 15,000–20,000) removed as uncertain -> "a much larger Ethiopian force". Harar "one of the holiest cities in Islam" -> "a renowned centre of Islamic learning".
- ADDED: Lebna Dengel named (death 1540); Zara Yaqob at Gomit; nickname Gragn; Ottoman musketeers from c. 1541; Nur as Ahmad's nephew; Aussa (1577).
- STYLE: unified spelling Gelawdewos; British spelling; trimmed repetition. Removed the troop figures noted above; no years removed.

- FACT: Isfahan liberated "after eight years of occupation" -> "seven years" (Afghans held it 1722–1729).

- FACT: Indian campaign order "crossing the Khyber in December 1738, sweeping through Peshawar and Lahore, crossing the Indus" -> Khyber (late 1738), Peshawar, Indus, Lahore, Karnal, Delhi (March 1739).

- ADDED: Damghan and Murche-Khort (1729); Kars (1745); zamburaks; Fathabad as assassination site; Teimuraz II; Najaf conference (1743); Shahrokh blinded 1750.
- STYLE: removed unattributed quotation about exhausted land; toned down superlatives; British spelling. Removed "within three hours"/"six times" figures as noted; no years removed.

## Akkadian Empire

- FACT: Sargon "washing his weapons in the sea" placed at the Mediterranean -> refers to the Lower Sea (Persian Gulf) after conquering Sumer; western campaigns described as reaching the Cedar Forest and Silver Mountains towards the Mediterranean.
- FACT: "installed Akkadian officials across more than 65 cities and established postal ... systems" -> officials placed in the conquered cities (the figure and a postal system are not securely attested); "postal system" also removed from governance.
- FACT: "revolts that plagued the later reigns of Sargon, Rimush, and Manishtushu" -> Sargon, Rimush and Naram-Sin (the Great Revolt was under Naram-Sin; Manishtushu's reign is less associated with revolt).
- FACT: "city of Akkad itself was destroyed and never reoccupied" -> "declined into obscurity" (Akkad is still mentioned in later texts; its destruction is not established).
- FACT: Akkadian as lingua franca "for nearly two millennia" used "between Babylon and Assyria" -> in its Babylonian form for much of the second millennium BCE; Macedonian empire removed from list of heirs.
- FACT: Anatolia campaigns -> attributed to later legend; Enheduanna "the first named author" hedged ("often described as"); "merchant guilds" removed (anachronistic).
- ADDED: Lower Sea episode; ships of Dilmun/Magan/Meluhha at Akkad's quay; Naram-Sin as Sargon's grandson, "King of the Four Quarters", divine determinative; Great Revolt; Stele of the Vultures; Ur III revival of divine kingship; Shar-kali-sharri's wars with Gutians and Amorites.
- STYLE: hedged absolute claims ("first true empire" -> "widely regarded as"); toned down summary ("Romans to Mughals"); British spelling. The 4.2 ka drought date span "2200–2150 BCE" simplified to "around 2200 BCE"; no other years removed.

## Aksumite Empire

- FACT: Kaleb's Yemen campaign extended Aksumite control "to its maximum extent of approximately 2.5 million square kilometres" -> figure removed (no reliable basis).
- FACT: Persians in Yemen "defeated the Aksumite garrison at Sana'a" (c. 575–578 / 570–578, inconsistent) -> overthrew "the Abyssinian rulers of Yemen, the successors of Abraha", dated "in the 570s" consistently (Yemen had become effectively independent under Abraha before the Persian conquest).
- FACT: Frumentius "oversaw the construction of 44 churches" -> removed (unsupported); added his title Abba Salama.
- FACT: Zagwe succession "around 940 CE" stated as fact -> traditional account, date uncertain (tenth–twelfth century); Kebra Nagast "intimately connected to Aksumite royal ideology" -> codified in the fourteenth century, traced through the Aksumite kings.

- FACT: Zoskales described as king of Aksum and "Greek-educated" -> "local ruler ... versed in Greek letters"; coin minting "ceased in the early seventh century" hedged to "around the early seventh century"; soil erosion "from around 650 CE onward" -> hedged environmental theory (date removed). Year 940 removed as noted.
- ADDED: Najran persecution under Dhu Nuwas; expedition c. 525; Abraha's independence; Meroë; teff; Theodosius I; Alexandrian appointment of bishops; Aksum's sheltering of early Muslim emigrants (c. 615).
- STYLE: removed the named historian citation (Munro-Hay quote); "Africa's most consequential" -> "one of Africa's most consequential"; British spelling (Graeco-Roman).

- FACT: Yusuf II "(1214–1224)" -> (r. 1213–1224); Las Navas coalition "Castile, Aragon, Navarre, and Portugal" -> Castile, Aragon and Navarre with Portuguese and other crusading contingents (the King of Portugal was not present); "militarily passive" al-Nasir removed.
- FACT: Jews and Christians had "coexisted relatively peacefully under the Almoravids" -> qualifier removed (the Almoravids also deported Christians); trade partners "Italian, Byzantine" -> Genoese, Pisan.
- ADDED: Ibn Mardanish and completion of al-Andalus conquest (1172); Almohad entry into Iberia from 1146; Giralda begun 1184; talaba; Abu Zakariya; Abu Dabbus.
- STYLE: fixed typo "reach its zenith"; British spelling; trimmed repetition. "1159–1160" simplified to "by 1160"; no other years removed.

- FACT: Almoravid dinars "circulating from England to Egypt" / influencing "currency stability from England to Egypt" -> widely trusted in the western Mediterranean and imitated in Christian Iberia as the morabetino (origin of the maravedí); the England claim is unsupported.

- ADDED: Toledo (1085); Awdaghust; taifa annexation dates (Granada 1090, Seville 1091, Badajoz 1094, Zaragoza 1110); Reverter; burning of al-Ghazali's works; Qubba Ba'adiyyin, Tlemcen and Qarawiyyin; Tashfin ibn Ali's death (1145).
