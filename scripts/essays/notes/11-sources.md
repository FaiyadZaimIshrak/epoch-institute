## s11-lines What Set the Ranks Apart
1. venice intro (Serrata 1297, Golden Book). hawaii mobility ("Rank in Hawaii was genealogy, so no one could make
   himself a chief"). mali-empire mobility ("a person belonged to a lineage, free, nyamakala or enslaved, and married
   within it"). joseon intro (household registers; "Status decided dress, marriage ..."). tokugawa-japan intro
   (registers on temple certificates; "Status decided where people lived, what they wore ... whom they married").
   Entry serrata-del-maggior-consiglio exists.
2. old-babylonia intro (three classes, awīlum, muškēnum, slave; "punishes by class"). kievan-rus intro (Russkaya
   Pravda "ranked everyone else by what it allowed and what it charged"). russian-empire intro (Code of Laws of 1832,
   four estates; first two "paid no poll tax and gave no recruits"). ancien-regime-france intro (privilege not wealth
   alone; no taille, the chief direct tax; only nobles could wear a sword). ayutthaya intro (sakdina numbers 5, 20,
   600, 10,000; "What the numbers measured in practice was manpower").
3. song-china intro ("rank came above all from office, and office came, more than ever before, from the civil service
   examinations"). ottoman-empire intro (askeri paid no taxes; reaya "the flock" paid for them). mughal-empire intro
   (mansabdars "paid with assignments of land revenue that he moved at will"). athens intro (Solon's property classes
   "graded by the yield of a man's land"). dutch-republic intro ("social status was largely determined by income").
   han-china intro (merchants last; "barred from office and from wearing silk yet could grow richer than nobles").
4. abbasid-caliphate intro (dhimmis paid a poll tax; both above the enslaved). delhi-sultanate intro (jizya; "relied on
   Hindu chiefs and village officials to collect the revenue"). venice intro (Jews denied citizenship, Ghetto from
   1516). dutch-republic intro (only those who conformed to the Reformed Church could hold office). yuan-china intro
   ("The order followed how early each people had submitted"). ptolemaic-egypt intro ("ranked by class and by
   language"). qing-china intro (the quoted division). achaemenid-persia intro ("The Persians of the homeland ... paid
   no tribute").
5. saint-domingue intro (Code Noir 1685; from the 1760s laws took rights from free people of colour, office and honour
   for whites). spanish-america intro ("colonial law and ideology ranked people by descent and colour"; casta
   paintings "show a neat ladder of mixtures"). classical-india intro (four varnas fixed by birth; Chandalas outside;
   jatis). classical-india rank "Chandalas and other outcastes" text (Manusmriti: corpses of people with no kin,
   executing condemned criminals). joseon rank "Outcasts" text (baekjeong "butchers, tanners, basket-makers and
   executioners, work that Buddhist ideas of pollution led others to avoid"). tokugawa-japan rank "Outcasts of the
   leather trades" text (skinned dead animals, tanned hides; Shinto and Buddhist ideas of pollution tied to death and
   blood). The last sentence is the essay's reading of those three texts and of SOCIETIES-GUIDE.md's rule.

## s11-women Women on the Ladder
1. athens women (kyrios). kievan-rus women (poluvir'ye, half). old-babylonia women (laws 209, 211, 213: ten, five and
   two shekels). heian-japan women (field two-thirds of a man's). abbasid-caliphate, mamluk-sultanate, ottoman-empire,
   mughal-empire women (daughter half a son's/brother's share; free women own property). ancien-regime-france women
   (about half a man's wage). british-raj women (nine pence to a man's shilling).
2. haudenosaunee women and intro (mother's clan; women held the dwellings and farmed land; clan mothers chose the
   chiefs). ashanti women (chiefs represented their mothers' lines; queen mothers' power to nominate). hawaii women
   (rank through mothers and fathers; Keōpūolani outranked Kamehameha I). dahomey women (female counterpart for each
   great office). benin women (Iyoba ranked with the senior chiefs, own palace at Uselu and own regiment). sparta women
   (married at eighteen to twenty, not at fourteen like many Athenian girls; own and inherit land; Aristotle,
   nearly two-fifths).
3. ancient-egypt women (Hatshepsut ruled as king). mamluk-sultanate women (Shajar al-Durr ruled Egypt as sultan in
   1250). delhi-sultanate women (Razia Sultana 1236–1240). majapahit women (Tribhuwana and Suhita reigned in their own
   right). classic-maya women (Yohl Ik'nal, holy lord at Palenque 583–604). mughal-empire women (Nur Jahan held the
   imperial seal, coins in her name). joseon women (early inheritance; from the 17th century male line; yangban widow
   could not remarry; seven grounds; she could not divorce him).
4. heian-japan women (not trained in Chinese, the language of government; kana; Tale of Genji). spanish-america women
   (Sor Juana, nun 1669, defence of women's learning 1691; Micaela Bastidas executed in Cusco 1781). saint-domingue
   women (rape by owners and overseers unpunished; Cécile Fatiman with Boukman at Bois Caïman, August 1791).
   ashanti women (Yaa Asantewaa, 1900, last armed resistance to British rule).

## s11-ideal Ideal and Practice
