## s14-day Why 25 December
1. Entry nativity-of-jesus (Matthew and Luke; Bethlehem in Judea, reign of Herod; "The gospels state no date"; 6–4 BCE
   from Herod's death). Profile Christianity, beliefs section ("The Christian year is marked by Christmas, the birth of
   Jesus, and above all by Easter"). Last clause ("had to find its day later") is the essay's bridge.
2. Entry christmas (Chronograph of 354, part generally agreed written in Rome in 336). Day 12-25 336 ("A Roman
   calendar compiled in 354 … the earliest evidence for the date. Why it was chosen remains debated.").
3. Entry christmas (25 December the traditional date of the winter solstice in the Roman Empire); Entry winter-solstice
   (same). Day 12-25 274 (temple, vow after Palmyra, paid for with its spoils, chief god; tagged Roman Empire). Entry
   sol-invictus ("Unconquered Sun"; dedicated 25 December 274; "according to some scholars he also instituted on that
   day … Dies Natalis Solis Invicti"; coinage of Constantine I until 325/326; last inscription 387). The closing clause
   (336 falls while the Sun was still honoured) is the essay's reading of those dates. NOTE: Entry winter-solstice
   states flatly that Aurelian made 25 December the festival of the birthday of Sol Invictus; the essay keeps the
   sol-invictus entry's hedge ("according to some scholars").
4. Entry saturnalia (17 December, extended by the 1st century BCE to 23 December; banquet, gift-giving, gambling,
   masters served slaves; popular into 3rd and 4th centuries CE; "may have influenced"). Entry yule (heathen Germanic
   peoples; merged with Christmas; Hákonar saga góða, Hákon the Good's law). Entry koliada (period from Christmas to
   Epiphany; rituals some pre-Christian). Profile Slavic paganism ("Rites followed the farming year, from Koliada at
   Christmastide to the fire and water festivals of Yarilo and Kupala"). Entry christmas (last sentence: customs mix).
5. Entry christmas (Julian 25 December = currently 7 January). Profile Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia, governance
   ("6 January 1198, the Armenian Christmas"). Entry epiphany-holiday (traditionally 6 January). Entry advent (existed
   from about 480). Entry twelfth-night-holiday (Council of Tours 567; 5 or 6 January by the counting). Entry candlemas
   (2 February, traditionally the 40th day and conclusion of the Christmas–Epiphany season).

## s14-crowns A Day for Crowns
1. Day 12-25 800 ("During Christmas Mass in St Peter's … Emperor of the Romans … unused since 476"). Entry
   st-peters-basilica (the old basilica on this site). Profile Carolingian Empire, foundation ("acclaimed Imperator
   Romanorum … first emperor in the West since the deposition of Romulus Augustulus in 476 CE"). Entries pope-leo-iii,
   charlemagne. Day 12-26 795 (Leo III elected pope).
2. Entry coronation-of-charlemagne (high point of the Carolingian Empire; later model for the Holy Roman Empire).
   Profile Carolingian Empire, foundation ("establishing the ideal of a unified Christian Europe under Carolingian
   leadership"). Profile Holy Roman Empire, foundation (traced its origin; "deliberately invoked …"; Otto I 962, "the
   date most historians treat as the functional founding"). Glance Carolingian Empire and Glance Holy Roman Empire
   (ev 800: "Pope Leo III crowns Charlemagne emperor in Rome on Christmas Day").
3. Day 12-25 1000 (son of Grand Prince Géza; Esztergom; first king; Christian kingdom; "dated either 25 December 1000
   or 1 January 1001"). Profile Kingdom of Hungary (1000-1301), foundation ("crowned king at Christmas 1000 or on
   1 January 1001, with a crown that, according to his later legends, was sent by Pope Sylvester II with the approval
   of Emperor Otto III"). Entry holy-crown-of-hungary (traditionally linked with Stephen; surviving form: later
   11th-century Byzantine enamel and a Latin upper part). Entries stephen-i-of-hungary, esztergom.
4. Day 12-25 1025 (Gniezno Cathedral; son of Bolesław I; attacks, revolts, loss of the royal crown). Entry gniezno
   (early kings crowned in its cathedral). Entry boleslaw-i-the-brave (967–1025; "first crowned king in 1025"). Entry
   mieszko-ii-lambert.
5. Day 12-25 1046 (Henry king of Germany since 1039; Clement II former bishop of Bamberg, made pope by Henry after
   Sutri removed three rival claimants). Entry pope-clement-ii (born Suidger; crowned Henry). Entry sutri (synod of
   1046 "in the presence of Emperor Henry III"). Entry henry-iii-holy-roman-emperor.
6. The essay's reading, stated as such ("None of these pages says why …"). Rests on Day 12-25 800 (during Christmas
   Mass), Day 12-25 1066 (congregation's acclamation mistaken for a riot). Day 12-25 820 (Leo V murdered in a church of
   the Great Palace by followers of Michael II; first Amorian ruler).

## s14-normans Norman and Crusader Christmases
1. Entry battle-of-hastings (14 October 1066; crowned Christmas Day 1066). Day 12-25 1066 (Westminster Abbey; Norman
   soldiers set fire to nearby houses). Entry westminster-abbey (rebuilt by Edward the Confessor, consecrated 1065;
   every monarch since, except Edward V and Edward VIII). Entry norman-conquest (Norman aristocracy and church hierarchy
   replaced the English elite). Profile Duchy of Normandy, military and impact (Hastings; the Conquest's effects);
   Profile Kingdom of England, foundation ("a succession crisis that ended with the Norman Conquest").
2. Profile Kingdom of Jerusalem, foundation (stormed 15 July 1099; Godfrey chosen, declined the title, Advocate of the
   Holy Sepulchre; died July 1100; "his brother Baldwin, count of Edessa, came south and was crowned King Baldwin I at
   Bethlehem on Christmas Day 1100"). Entry baldwin-i-of-jerusalem (first crowned king). Entry bethlehem (Christian
   tradition holds it the birthplace of Jesus). Entries first-crusade, godfrey-of-bouillon.
3. Profile Kingdom of Sicily, foundation (count from 1105; united the Norman south; schism of 1130, Anacletus II
   granted a royal title; crowned in Palermo Cathedral on Christmas Day 1130; Innocent II, Emperor Lothair III and
   rebellious barons and cities fought him for most of the 1130s; 1139 Innocent captured at Galluccio, Treaty of
   Mignano). Entry antipope-anacletus-ii ("In return for his support"). Entry palermo-cathedral ("crowned in the
   earlier building on Christmas Day 1130"). Entry roger-ii-of-sicily.
4. Profile Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia, governance (first century a principality; Prince Levon II; Frederick
   Barbarossa and Henry VI; Tarsus, 6 January 1198, "the Armenian Christmas"; Catholicos Gregory VI Abirad). Levon is
   left unlinked: the entry leo-i-king-of-armenia may be the same man but the profile calls him Levon II.
5. Day 12-24 1144 (siege begun 28 November; atabeg of Mosul and Aleppo; Edessa, capital of the first of the crusader
   states; prompted the Second Crusade). Entry imad-al-din-zengi (founding the Zengid dynasty). Entry
   baldwin-i-of-jerusalem (ruler of Edessa in 1098, "founding the first crusader state").
6. Entry hird (law code read when the whole hird assembled at Christmas and Easter; Norway). Entry
   uilliam-buidhe-o-ceallaigh (Christmas 1351 feast; poem). Entry ulf-jarl (regent of Denmark, married Cnut's sister;
   killed in Roskilde at Christmas 1026 after a quarrel over chess). First and last sentences are the essay's reading.

## s14-surprise A Day for Surprise
1. Entry santa-maria-ship (flagship; aground off the north coast of Hispaniola on Christmas Day 1492; timbers; some 39
   men). Entry la-navidad (first European settlement in the Americas; Guacanagarí; destroyed by late 1493; attributed
   to Caonabo of Maguana). Entry kiritimati (also known as Christmas Island; sighted by Cook on Christmas Eve 1777).
2. Profile Cossack Hetmanate, foundation (Khmelnytsky Uprising of 1648 against the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth;
   "entered Kyiv at Christmas 1648, hailed as a liberator from Polish captivity").
3. Entry battle-of-trenton (defeats in New York, retreat through New Jersey). Entry
   george-washingtons-crossing-of-the-delaware-river (night of 25–26 December 1776; commander-in-chief; McConkey's
   Ferry; rain to sleet and snow; other crossings called off or ineffective; about 2,400 men). Day 12-25 1776.
4. Entry battle-of-trenton (morning of 26 December; about 1,400 Hessians under Rall; no long-distance outposts or
   patrols; 22 killed including Rall, 896 captured; two deaths on the march, five wounded; morale, re-enlistments).
   Entry george-washingtons-crossing… (crossed back with prisoners and stores). The sentence on what the entries do
   not say is the essay's caution.
5. Entry baptist-war (Christmas Rebellion; began 25 December 1831; up to 60,000; strike for wages; plantations burned;
   crushed by early 1832; hundreds executed). Entry samuel-sharpe (peaceful general strike intended; hanged in Montego
   Bay, May 1832). Profile Abolitionism, events ("at Christmas 1831 … Its brutal suppression, reported by missionaries,
   helped persuade a newly reformed Parliament to pass the Slavery Abolition Act of 1833").
6. Entry christmas-battles (Russian offensive near Riga, late December 1916 and January 1917; Latvian Riflemen broke
   through; several thousand casualties). Profile Republic of Latvia, military ("became part of national memory").
   Entry christmas-uprising (early January 1919, around Orthodox Christmas; opposed union with Serbia). Entry
   bloody-christmas-1920 (24 to 29 December 1920; ended D'Annunzio's occupation of Fiume).

## s14-truce A Day for Truce
1. Entry treaty-of-ghent (24 December 1814; pre-war borders of June 1812; news took a month; New Orleans 8 January
   1815 under Andrew Jackson; took effect February 1815). Day 12-24 1814.
2. Entry christmas-truce (unofficial ceasefires; Western Front; roughly 100,000 British and German troops; no man's
   land; burials, carols, football; hostilities continued in other sectors). Day 12-24 1914 (sang carols across the
   lines on Christmas Eve).
3. Entry christmas-truce (Benedict XV, 7 December 1914, both sides refused; next year only a few units, orders
   prohibiting truces).
4. Song o-holy-night, story ("a legend holds that French troops sang it on Christmas Eve 1870 in the Franco-Prussian
   War, bringing a brief ceasefire"; "Cantique de Noël", 1847).

## s14-forbidden Forbidden and Reinvented
1. Entry puritans (purify the Church of England; parliamentary side in the Civil War). Entry christmas ("Puritans
   prohibited the celebration in England from 1647 to 1660"; nothing more is on the site about the ban). Entry
   stuart-restoration (1660). Song god-rest-ye-merry-gentlemen / its entry (early version in an anonymous manuscript of
   the 1650s). Entry boxing-day (Christmas boxes, Pepys 1663; origin of the name "not definitive").
2. Entry christmas-carol (4th-century Rome; 13th century France, Germany, Italy, Francis of Assisi; Awdlay 1426,
   twenty-five; Reformation, Luther). Entry nativity-scene (Francis, Christmas 1223, cave near Greccio, "is said to
   have"). Entry in-dulci-jubilo (c. 1328, German and Latin). Entry huron-carol (Canada's oldest; probably 1642;
   Brébeuf; Huron-Wendat language).
3. Song hark-the-herald-angels-sing (1739; "Hymn for Christmas-Day"; Whitefield, Madan revisions; 1855 Cummings
   adapted Mendelssohn's Festgesang). Song silent-night (Christmas Eve 1818; Mohr, Gruber; guitar; "flooding of the
   river had possibly damaged the church organ"; fictional tale of the 1930s; about 300 languages).
4. Entry christmas-tree (Central Europe; German Lutherans, 16th century; Strasbourg 1539; Queen Charlotte 1800;
   Victoria and Albert). Song o-tannenbaum (1824; older folk song that had nothing to do with the holiday; "not,
   strictly, about such a tree"). Entry santa-claus (Saint Nicholas; Sinterklaas; New Amsterdam). Entry saint-nicholas
   (4th-century bishop of Myra). Profile New Netherland, legacy ("the feast of Saint Nicholas gave Santa Claus").
5. Entry a-christmas-carol (19 December 1843, London; Field Lane Ragged School; Scrooge and the spirits; sold out by
   Christmas Eve; thirteen editions by end of 1844; readings 1849–1870). Song good-king-wenceslas (1853; Feast of
   Stephen, 26 December; "In his lifetime he was only a duke"). Entry wenceslaus-i-duke-of-bohemia.
6. Entry messiah-handel (composed 1741; Dublin, 13 April 1742, charity concert). Day 12-26 1709 (Agrippina, Venice).
   Entry the-nutcracker (set on Christmas Eve; 18 December 1892, Saint Petersburg; "not deemed a success"; since the
   late 1960s danced by many companies, especially in North America). "In spring and not at Christmas" restates the
   April date.

## Doubts for the editor
- "A feast when courts gathered" is not stated by any page; the essay says so twice (crowns §6, normans §6) and offers
  it as the Institute's reading from the hird, the Connacht feast, the Christmas Mass of 800 and Westminster 1066.
- No page says the Hessians were surprised because of Christmas, nor why Gorbachev resigned on 25 December; the essay
  says the pages do not say.
- Puritan ban: one sentence in the christmas entry is all the site has; the essay adds only the Restoration year and
  the 1650s carol manuscript beside it.
- Messiah: its entry does not connect it with Christmas (first performed 13 April 1742); the essay says so rather than
  calling it music made for the season.
- Calendars: The Nutcracker's "18 December 1892" (Saint Petersburg) and the Decembrist revolt's 26 December 1825 are
  given as the pages give them, without a note on Julian dating, because the pages carry none; the essay does not use
  the Decembrist date.
- Winter-solstice entry vs sol-invictus entry on Aurelian's festival (see s14-day §3).
- Aurelian and the Chronograph of 354 have no entries; Leo V, Michael II, Benedict XV, Johann Rall, Francis of Assisi,
  Charles Wesley, Mohr, Gruber, Bing Crosby, Otto I are unlinked (no entry found, or not checked).
- The valley-forge and battle-of-the-bulge links and the Khmelnytsky "February 1649" sentence were cut for length.
