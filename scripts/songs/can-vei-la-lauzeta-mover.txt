== story
"Can vei la lauzeta mover" is a song in the Occitan language by Bernart de Ventadorn, a troubadour of the 12th century. It is among the oldest and the best known of all troubadour songs. Both its words and its melody survive, in variants from three different manuscripts. Its lauzeta is a lark.

The song is a first-person poem of seven stanzas of eight lines each, with a closing coda of four lines. It has been called one of the first poems to dramatise the effect of someone actually speaking in the present.

It travelled beyond the Occitan-speaking south. In the 13th century it was one of three Occitan songs set into the French romance Guillaume de Dole, and it is one of two similar insertions in Gerbert de Montreuil's Le roman de la violette. Some scholars have suggested that it inspired a tercet in Dante's Divine Comedy (Paradiso XX:73–75), which also describes a lark in flight; others think Dante may have met the image through the poet Bondie Dietaiuti, or that a lark's flight was too common a sight for any link to be drawn. In the 20th century Ezra Pound translated parts of the song in Canto 6 of The Cantos and returned to its image in Canto 117.

The song is still performed, and it has been recorded on many albums.
== meaning
A song of unrequited love. The singer watches a lark rise joyfully towards the sun and fall, and wishes he could feel such joy, but his love for a woman who does not return it keeps him from it. He compares himself to figures of legend who were undone by love and resolves to go into exile if she will not love him. The mood is longing and bitter.
