== story
"May There Always Be Sunshine", also known as "Solnechny krug" (The Circle of the Sun), is a Soviet children's song of 1962 with music by Arkady Ostrovsky and words by Lev Oshanin. The writer and children's poet Korney Chukovsky later wrote that it grew from the four lines of its refrain, made up in 1928 by a four-year-old boy, Kostya Barannikov.

Maya Kristalinskaya first sang it on the radio programme "Good Morning!" in July 1962. In 1963 Tamara Miansarova won first prize with it at the Sopot International Song Festival, and it quickly became popular across the Soviet Union and beyond. Young Pioneers sang it at their camps and school meetings, Little Octobrists in primary schools, and even children below school age; in the Soviet Union it was widely taken as an expression of the people's wish for peace.

The song travelled far. In 1964 the Swedish Hootenanny Singers, led by Björn Ulvaeus, later of ABBA, recorded "Gabrielle" based on it, their first song to be heard abroad. In German, as "Immer lebe die Sonne" (translated by Ilse and Hans Naumilkat and Manfred Streubel), it was popular among the Ernst Thälmann Pioneers of East Germany; Finnish Pioneers sang it as "Paistaa aurinko aina", and Estonian Pioneers in Heljo Mänd's translation, best known in Georg Ots's recording. Gidi Koren wrote Hebrew words, made popular by his Israeli group The Brothers & the Sisters. Pete Seeger sang it in English, and it is on his live album with Arlo Guthrie, Together in Concert (1975).

Children's musicians took it up in the West: Charlotte Diamond sang its chorus in six languages on her 1985 album 10 Carrot Diamond, and Raffi in three on his album Let's Play. It was part of a medley at the opening ceremony of the 2014 Winter Olympics in Sochi, and its title is inscribed on the pedestal of the bronze monument to Samantha Smith at Artek in Crimea.
== meaning
A children's song wishing for peace and a world without war; bright and hopeful.
