== story
"Suliko" is a love poem written in 1895 by the Georgian poet Akaki Tsereteli. Suliko is a Georgian name, given to both women and men, meaning "soul". At the poet's request his relative Varinka Tsereteli set it to music in the same year, and in that form it became widely known throughout the Soviet Union. Under Joseph Stalin it was often played on the radio, reputedly because it was his favourite song. It was translated and sung in many languages, among them Russian, Ukrainian, Polish, Romanian, English, German, Basque, Chinese and Hebrew.

Tsereteli was closely tied to the Georgian national liberation movement, and the song has been read as carrying his wish for an independent Georgian homeland. Scholars have given it other meanings too. Akaki Bakradze saw in the succession of the rose, the nightingale and the star a rising search for God and for the lost paradise from which mankind was exiled; Emzar Kvitaishvili likewise read the search for Suliko as the search for a supreme love and for God. Others have taken Suliko as a symbol of any distant, unreachable dream, whether a person's peace with himself or his place in life.

A folk story about its origin was recorded by the poet and folklorist Eter Tataraidze in the village of Toneti, in the Tetritskaro region. In it, Akaki dedicated the poem to Suliko Samadashvili of Toneti, the brother of a friend: he had gone to town to buy clothes for his wedding and was killed in the forest, and his remains were found only months later. When Akaki came to the grave he found Suliko's beloved weeping there, and so changed his first line, which had spoken of a brother's grave, into one about a beloved's. The story is folklore, not established history, but it shows how deeply the song entered Georgian popular belief.

The song is performed in many traditions, from Georgian ensembles such as Mdzlevari and the Batumi ensemble to the Alexandrov Ensemble and university choirs in Russia, and singers abroad.
== meaning
A mourner searches in vain for the grave of a beloved, and the song passes through images of a rose, a nightingale and a star. The mood is gentle and sorrowful, a longing that many have read as a search for something beyond reach.
