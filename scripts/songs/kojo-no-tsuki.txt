== story
"Kōjō no Tsuki" ("The Moon over the Ruined Castle") is a Japanese song of the Meiji period. The pianist and composer Rentarō Taki wrote the music in 1901 as a song for music lessons, to be sung without accompaniment, and it was printed in a songbook for junior high school pupils. The words are a poem by Bansui Doi. The ruins of different castles stand behind each part: the music was inspired by the ruins of Oka Castle, the poem by those of Aoba Castle and Aizuwakamatsu Castle.

Taki wrote the song in B minor. A slower D minor version by Kosaku Yamada, with accompaniment, is also widely sung. Taki's second bar has an E sharp, which the modern version usually sings as E, probably because the original did not fit traditional Japanese music.

The tenor Yoshie Fujiwara recorded the song in 1925 and was the first Japanese singer to make it known around the world. It later passed into jazz and rock: Thelonious Monk recorded it as "Japanese Folk Song" on his 1967 album Straight, No Chaser, and the German band Scorpions sang it as a power ballad at a Tokyo concert released on their 1978 live album Tokyo Tapes, one of the few versions that keeps close to Taki's original. Guitarists, pianists and singers in Japan and abroad have recorded it since, and in 1998 the asteroid 8957 Koujounotsuki was named after it.
== meaning
As its title says, the song looks at the moon over a ruined castle, and its words were inspired by the ruins of real castles. The mood of the music, written in a minor key and often sung slowly and with nostalgia, is reflective and sad.
