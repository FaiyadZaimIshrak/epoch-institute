== story
"The East Is Red" is a revolutionary song of the Chinese Communist Party. Its melody came from a peasant love song of the Loess Plateau, "Bai Ma Diao" (White Horse Tune), also called "Zhima You" (Sesame Oil), which was widely sung around Yan'an in the 1930s and whose words often changed with the singer. The modern lyrics, attributed to Li Youyuan, a farmer from Shaanbei (northern Shaanxi), were produced in 1942 during the Second Sino-Japanese War; he is said to have been inspired by the rising sun. An earlier version may have named Liu Zhidan, a local communist hero killed in Shanxi in 1936, whose name was later replaced by Mao Zedong's.

The song was popular in the communist base area of Yan'an but was heard less after the party won the Chinese Civil War and founded the People's Republic of China in 1949, possibly because some senior leaders disagreed with its portrayal of Mao as China's saviour. Its words idealise Mao, and after the Korean War its spread became one of his first efforts to present himself as a perfect hero in popular culture. In 1956 a political commissar proposed teaching it to the army; the defence minister, Peng Dehuai, refused and called it a personality cult. His opposition to the song and to the cult around Mao contributed to his purge in 1959, after which Mao pressed the cult further.

In 1964 Zhou Enlai made the song the central chorus of a stage work he created, co-produced, wrote and directed, first titled "March Forward under the Banner of Mao Zedong Thought". Performed by 2,000 artists with a chorus and orchestra of 1,000, it was staged repeatedly at the Great Hall of the People in Beijing for the 15th National Day and later filmed as "The East Is Red". The definitive version of the song was first heard in this production.

During the Cultural Revolution Tian Han, the author of the official anthem "March of the Volunteers", was purged, and "The East Is Red" served as China's unofficial anthem; by 1966 Mao had succeeded in having it sung in place of the anthem. Loudspeakers in towns and villages played it at dawn and dusk, the Central People's Broadcasting Station opened each day with it on a set of bronze bells cast in the Warring States period, and broadcasts commonly began with it and closed with "The Internationale". Students sang it together at the start of the first class each morning. In 1967 the commune of Ujme in Xinjiang was renamed Dongfanghong ("The East Is Red") Commune. In 1969 Jiang Qing added its tune to the Yellow River Piano Concerto, and in 1970 China's first satellite took its name and sent the melody back to Earth as its first signal.

From 1 October 1978 the "March of the Volunteers" was again played at national events, and "The East Is Red" lost its standing as unofficial anthem. Because of its association with the Cultural Revolution it was rarely heard after Deng Xiaoping's rise in the late 1970s, and some in China regard it as an uncomfortable reminder of the cult of personality around Mao. The tune nonetheless remains familiar: in 2009 it was voted the most popular patriotic song in a government-run internet poll, it is the chime of clocks at Beijing railway station, the Beijing Telegraph Building, the Shanghai Custom House and the Drum Tower in Xi'an, some radio stations have used it as an interval signal, and it plays from novelty lighters bearing Mao's face sold to tourists.
== meaning
A song in praise of Mao Zedong and the Communist Party, solemn and celebratory in the manner of a hymn.
