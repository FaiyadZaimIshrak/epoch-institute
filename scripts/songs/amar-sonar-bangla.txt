== story
"Amar Sonar Bangla" ("My Golden Bengal") is an ode to Mother Bengal written by the Bengali poet Rabindranath Tagore. The exact year it was composed is not known, because the original manuscript was never found. Its melody is derived from "Ami Kothay Pabo Tare", a song of the Baul singer Gagan Harkara, set to the rhythm cycle Dadra tala.

It is linked with the first Partition of Bengal. In 1905 the British authorities split the province of the Bengal Presidency in two: the Viceroy, Lord Curzon, announced the decision on 20 July, and it took effect on 16 October. The division ran along communal lines, with a Muslim majority in East Bengal and Assam and a Hindu majority in West Bengal, and it is claimed to have been politically motivated. With many other songs, this one was meant to revive the unified spirit of Bengal and raise public feeling against the divide. Tagore's biographer Prashanta Kumar Paul held that it was first sung on 25 August 1905 at a gathering in Kolkata. The words first appeared in print in the September 1905 issues of the periodicals Bongodorshon and Baul, and the song with its musical notation appeared the same month in the music journal Shongeet Biggnan Praveshika. The notation was taken down by Tagore's niece Indira Devi as she heard him sing it.

In 1971, during the Bangladesh Liberation War, the Provisional Government of Bangladesh adopted it as the national anthem; the first ten lines of the poem are the version usually sung. The orchestral rendition was composed by Samar Das. When the Constitution of Bangladesh was drafted in 1972, it was chosen over "Dhana Dhanya Pushpa Bhara" by Dwijendra Lal Roy. Syed Ali Ahsan translated it into English.

It has faced criticism since at least 1975. Opponents have argued that it was written against a separate East Bengal, the territory that became Bangladesh, that its author was not a Bangladeshi, and that the mother it addresses is a Hindu goddess. After the coup of 15 August 1975, President Khondaker Mostaq Ahmed set up a committee to replace it, which recommended Kazi Nazrul Islam's "Notuner Gaan" (today the national march) and Farrukh Ahmed's "Panjeri"; the process stopped when he was removed. In 1979 Prime Minister Shah Azizur Rahman proposed Shahnaz Rahmatullah's "Prothom Bangladesh" instead, and under President Ziaur Rahman that song was played after the anthem on national television and at government events until his death in 1981. In 2002 the minister Motiur Rahman Nizami proposed changing its words, which the Cabinet Division did not accept. Calls to replace it were made again in 2024 and 2025.

On 26 March 2014, Bangladesh's independence day, 254,537 people sang it together at the National Parade Ground in Dhaka, a world record for the most people singing a national anthem at once, approved by Guinness World Records on 9 April 2014; India broke the record on 21 January 2017.
== meaning
A song of love for the land of Bengal, addressed to it as a beloved mother and praising how precious it is. The mood is tender and devoted.
