== story
"Dhana Dhanya Pushpa Bhara" ("Lavished with a wealth of grains and flowers") is a patriotic song in Bengali composed in 1905 by the writer Dwijendra Lal Roy. On 20 July 1905 the British colonial government split the Bengal Presidency in two along communal lines, into East Bengal and Assam, with a Muslim majority, and West Bengal, with a Hindu majority. The partition set off a nationalist surge, above all among Bengali Hindus, who held the move to be politically motivated. Songs such as this one, written in the same months, were meant to revive the spirit of a united Bengal and to raise public feeling against the communal divide. Roy put the song into his play Shajahan in 1907, and his son Dilip Kumar Roy later made a Sanskrit version of it.

The song remains one of the most popular Bengali patriotic songs. It was among the songs that encouraged the freedom fighters in the Liberation War of Bangladesh, and when the Constitution of Bangladesh was drafted in 1972 it was one of the two songs proposed for the national anthem; Rabindranath Tagore's "Amar Sonar Bangla", also written in the context of the partition of 1905, was chosen. In a 2006 survey by BBC Bangla it ranked tenth among the twenty greatest Bengali songs of all time. During the July Revolution in Bangladesh, on 3 August 2024, thousands of protesters sang it in chorus.
== meaning
A patriotic song of love for the land of Bengal, rich in grain and flowers, written to call Bengalis to the unity of their divided province; proud and tender in mood.
