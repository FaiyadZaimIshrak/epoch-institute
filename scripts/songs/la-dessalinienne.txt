== story
"La Dessalinienne" is the national anthem of Haiti, a march with words by Justin Lhérisson and music by Nicolas Geffrard. It is named in honour of Jean-Jacques Dessalines, the revolutionary leader and first ruler of Haiti; the historian Clément Lanier suggested the title.

In 1903, to mark the 100th anniversary of the Haitian Revolution, a competition was held for a national anthem. The judges preferred Lhérisson's verse and Geffrard's martial music to "L'Artibonitienne" by the diplomat Louis Edouard Pouget. The anthem was first performed in October 1903, at a celebration of the Armée Indigène's entry into Port-au-Prince organised by the Association du Petit Théâtre, sung by Auguste de Pradines, known as Kandjo. The words and music were printed at Bernard's in Port-au-Prince and sent throughout the country during that week. It was officially adopted as the national anthem in 1904.

Because one verse is short, performances are often lengthened by singing the first verse followed at once by the last. A Haitian Creole version, "Desalinyèn", was made by Raymond A. Moyse, and the singer Ansy Dérose (1934–1998) helped make it popular in 1980.
== meaning
A patriotic march in honour of the Haitian Revolution and of Dessalines, written for its centenary. Its tone is martial and solemn.
