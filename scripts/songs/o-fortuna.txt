== story
"O Fortuna" is a medieval Latin Goliardic poem of uncertain authorship, written in the early 13th century. It belongs to the collection known as the Carmina Burana, preserved in the Benediktbeuern manuscript. The poem is a complaint against Fortuna, in Roman mythology the inexorable fate that rules both gods and mortals.

Its subject belongs with a familiar medieval image, the Rota Fortunae or Wheel of Fortune, which the goddess spins at random so that some people suffer while others find wealth; the wheel appears in the version of the collection known as the Codex Buranus. Writing in Harper's, Scott Horton observed that the text shows how few people of its time "felt any control over their own destiny", while it also "rings with a passion for life".

The poem reached a wide modern audience through a later work. In 1934 the German composer Carl Orff came across the collection and, with the Latin and Greek enthusiast Michel Hofmann, chose and arranged 24 of its poems into a libretto. His cantata Carmina Burana, composed in 1935–1936 and first staged by the Frankfurt Opera on 8 June 1937, opens and closes with "O Fortuna" in the sections titled "Fortuna Imperatrix Mundi" ("Fortune, Empress of the World"). Orff's setting is a separate, much later work that is still in copyright.

Through that setting the medieval words became one of the most widely heard pieces of classical music. In 2009 it topped a BBC list of the most widely heard classical tracks of the previous 75 years in the United Kingdom. Its use in film trailers, television and advertising, from the trailer of Excalibur (1981) onward, has led writers such as Horton and David Clem to note how far the music has been detached from the poem's subject of fate and human struggle.
== meaning
The poem is a complaint addressed to Fortune, the fate that rules gods and mortals alike and, like the turning Wheel of Fortune, brings some people suffering and others wealth at random. Its mood is one of helplessness before fate, while still, as Horton put it, holding on to a passion for life.
