== story
"Bilādī, Bilādī, Bilādī" ("My Homeland, My Homeland, My Homeland") is the national anthem of Egypt. Its words were written by Muhammad Yunis al-Qady and its music composed by Sayed Darwish, a composer who kept close ties with early leaders of Egypt's national movement for independence, among them Mustafa Kamel. The chorus is drawn from one of Kamel's most famous nationalist speeches, given in 1907, in which he declared his love for his country.

Egypt had several anthems before it. The first, known as the "Khedive's Special Salute" or "Salam Affandina", dated from the reign of Khedive Ismail: it was played at the opening of the Suez Canal and adopted in 1871, an instrumental piece without official words whose most likely author was Giuseppe Pugioli, an Italian cornetist who had come to Egypt to perform Verdi's opera Aida and stayed. Without words it never became popular inside Egypt; after the revolution of 1919 Ahmad Shawqi won a competition held in 1920 to give it lyrics, but his text was not taken up. "Eslami ya Misr", written by Mustafa Sadiq Al-Rafei and composed by Safar Ali, was the anthem from 1923 to 1936. King Farouk restored the royal anthem in 1936, and after the end of the monarchy it was kept under a new name, the Egyptian Republican anthem.

In 1960 Republican Decree No. 143 adopted Kamal El-Tawil's melody for "Wallah Zaman Ya Selahy", a song with words by Salah Jahin for Umm Kulthum that had become popular in 1956 during the Tripartite Aggression; it was played without words. It remained the anthem until 1979, and served too as the anthem of the United Arab Republic and, from 1963 to 1981, of Iraq.

In 1979 Republican Decree No. 149 made "Bilādī, Bilādī, Bilādī" the national anthem, in an arrangement by the musician Mohamed Abdel Wahab made at the direction of President Anwar Sadat. A decree of December 1982, No. 590, laid down that the words of its first verse accompany the music at popular and national celebrations, while the music alone is played to welcome presidents and foreign delegations. An unofficial Coptic version, not codified in law, circulates widely online.
== meaning
An anthem of love and devotion to the homeland, built on the patriotic language of Mustafa Kamel's nationalist speech. The mood is warm and solemn.
