== story
Sari Gelin (Azerbaijani Sarı Gəlin), Sari Aghjik in Armenian and Dâman Kešân in Persian, is the name of a number of folk songs popular among the people of Iran, the southern Caucasus, above all present-day Azerbaijan and Armenia, and eastern Anatolia in present-day Turkey. All versions use the same melody in the Bayati makam or mode but are sung with different words, and the country of its origin is contested. In Armenian, sari means "of the mountain" and aghjik "girl", so the title means a girl or bride from the mountains; in Azerbaijani and Turkish, sarı is the Turkic word for "yellow" and gelin a bride, someone who comes into the family, so Sarı Gelin can mean a golden, blond or fair-skinned bride.

The Armenian Sari Aghjik uses the same melody as another Armenian song, Vard Siretsi ("I loved a rose"). In Azerbaijan, Sarı Gəlin is a legend of the love between a Muslim Azerbaijani and a Christian Armenian girl who are kept apart. The Azerbaijani words are in the literary form called bayati, one of the most popular kinds of poetry across most of the Middle East, known for its reflective tone and usually made of lines of seven syllables in a simple rhythm. The Azerbaijani version was arranged by the composer Asaf Zeynally (1909–1932); its text was published in Baku in 1982 in an edition by Hamid Arasly, and again in 2001 by Rafik Babayev. There are versions in Sorani Kurdish, one sung by the Kurdish singer Mohammad Mamle, and a Persian version, Dāman Kešān or Sāqi e Mey Xārān, whose words are entirely different. A Greek song to the same anonymous tune has words by Christos C. Papadopoulos.

The Armenian, Azerbaijani and Turkish versions share much: a fair, tall girl from the mountains (the Turkish version places the meeting in the market of Erzurum and names the nearby Palandöken mountain), a young man's cry of pain, the lovers kept apart, and the name Leyli, which recalls Layla, the famous beloved of Persian poetry. Read together, the versions tell of a Muslim young man in love with a Christian girl from the mountains whom he is not allowed to marry.

In Armenia the Armenian-Soviet opera singer Pavel Lisitsian, a national artist of the USSR, sang his own version in the 20th century, and many singers have recorded it since, among them Andre (2013), Emmy (2014) and Garik and Sona (2020); in 2020 its melody was used for an Armenia TV series named after it. In Azerbaijan the poet and playwright Huseyn Javid retold the story in his play Sheikh Sanan, about a Muslim young man and a Christian girl, and Yaver Rzayev directed the film Sari Gelin (1999), about the country's fight with Armenia, which was shown at the London and Karlovy Vary film festivals in 2000; in it a bride dressed in yellow, in both cultures a symbol of death and of the cruelty of fate, appears in a vision. The Azerbaijani singer Safura, the Italian singer Toto Cutugno (in Baku in 2013) and Lara Fabian (in Baku in 2014) have also sung it.

In Iran the Persian version was sung by Viguen, a pop and jazz singer of Armenian descent, and the song appears on Endless Vision (2006), an album by Djivan Gasparyan and Hossein Alizadeh recorded at the Niavaran Palace in Tehran and nominated for a Grammy Award. In Turkey the multi-ethnic group Kardeş Türküler recorded it in 1997 as "Sari Gyalin (Dağlı Gelin)"; a documentary of the same title, distributed in Turkish schools, denies the Armenian genocide and drew criticism. The singer Cem Adrian put out a live recording from 2010 in 2017, and in 2014 the British-Azerbaijani singer Sami Yusuf recorded a version mainly in English that ends in Azerbaijani.
== meaning
A love lament in many versions: a young man sings of a girl he loves and cannot have. In the Armenian, Azerbaijani and Turkish words she is a fair girl from the mountains, the two are kept apart, and he cries out in his pain; in the Armenian Sari Aghjik she has left him for someone else, and the Persian words tell instead of an unkind girl who runs from him.
