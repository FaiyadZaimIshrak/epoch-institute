== story
"Au clair de la lune" ("By the Light of the Moon") is a French folk song of the 18th century whose composer and lyricist are not known. Its first verse appears in print as early as 1820, in Les Voitures versées; four verses were published in the 1858 collection Chants et chansons populaires de la France, and the 1870 collection Chansons et rondes enfantines kept only the first two. Some sources report that the word for "pen" in the first verse was once an old word for "light" or "lamp", which fits the song's setting better. Much of the text carries sexual double meanings.

Its simple melody made it a favourite for teaching beginners on an instrument, and composers returned to it often. Ferdinand Hérold wrote piano variations on it in 1820 and Muzio Clementi a fantasia in 1821. Claude Debussy built his song "Pierrot" (1882) on it, Camille Saint-Saëns quoted its opening in "The Fossils" in The Carnival of the Animals (1886), and Erik Satie quoted it in Sports et divertissements (1914). Samuel Barber, Marc Blitzstein and Frank Martin made settings of their own in the 20th century, Martin's for one of his children to practise octaves. In 1964 the singer France Gall recorded it with new words that made it a love song.

The song has a place in the history of sound recording. On 9 April 1860 Édouard-Léon Scott de Martinville recorded himself singing its beginning on his phonautograph. It is the earliest recognisable recording of a human voice and of music. In 2008 researchers at the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory converted the recording digitally into sound.

It was long sung to children. A story in The Ladies Pocket Magazine of 1835 describes it as sung every night by the little boys of Paris. In his memoir Witness (1952), Whittaker Chambers remembered his mother singing it to his brother at bedtime, and how its sad turn troubled him as a small child. It also appears in F. Scott Fitzgerald's Tender Is the Night, in Maxwell Anderson and William March's play Bad Seed (1957) and in Lauren Groff's story collection Florida (2018).
== meaning
A small night scene under the moon: one person comes to a neighbour's door in the dark, asking for help to write a note because their light has gone out, and is not let in. The tune is simple and calm, which suits a bedtime song, though later verses play on double meanings meant for adult listeners.
