== story
"Indonesia Raya" ("Great Indonesia") was written by Wage Rudolf Supratman, a musician and young reporter. Living in Jakarta, he read an essay in Timbul magazine that challenged Indonesian musicians to compose a future national anthem, and he took up the challenge, finishing a song called "Indonesia" in Bandung in 1924. According to Tempo, he revised the words after reading Tan Malaka's book Massa Actie (1926), and the song went through several revisions before reaching its final form in 1928.

In 1928 young people from across Indonesia met at the Indonesian Youth Congress to press for independence. Supratman asked to report on it; the organisers, fearing that the Dutch colonial authorities would ban the meeting, asked him not to publish, and let him attend freely when he agreed. Encouraged by the congress leader Sugondo Djojopuspito, he played the song on the violin on 28 October 1928, during the Second Youth Congress that issued the Youth Pledge, hoping it would one day be the national anthem. The song marked the birth of a nationalist movement for the whole archipelago.

Political and student organisations spread the text, and the press printed it: the daily Soeloeh Ra'jat Indonesia on 7 November 1928 and the Chinese Indonesian weekly Sin Po on 10 November, which openly published both words and music in defiance of the Dutch authorities. In 1929 Supratman renamed the song "Indonesia Raya", adding "national anthem of Indonesia" beneath the title, and sold a thousand printed copies to friends and family. The record executive Tio Tek Hong issued the first records, with Supratman keeping the copyright, and another businessman, Yo Kim Tjan, had a recording made abroad. The records sold well, but in 1930 the Dutch colonial authorities banned the song and seized the unsold copies. Supratman died in 1938.

When Indonesia proclaimed its independence on 17 August 1945, the first stanza became the national anthem. In 1950 the government asked the Dutch arranger Jozef Cleber, then with the Cosmopolitan Orchestra sent by the Netherlands to help Indonesian musical life, for a symphonic version. Sukarno found his first recording, made in early 1951 with the orchestras of Radio Republik Indonesia, too ornate and asked for something as plain as the red and white flag and as grave as the Dutch anthem "Wilhelmus". Cleber, who heard something of the "Marseillaise" in the tune, made a second arrangement, maestoso con bravura, to which Sukarno asked him to add a gentle grazioso passage before the climax. That version was used for 47 years, until Addie MS recorded a new one in Melbourne in 1998 with his Twilite Orchestra, the Victorian Philharmonic Orchestra and the Melbourne Symphony Orchestra.

The rights passed after Supratman's death to his four sisters, but as the national anthem the song became the property of the state. The government obtained the sole rights from his family in 1958, Yo Kim Tjan handed over the original record the following year, and on 31 May 1960 each sister received 250,000 rupiah. A 1958 State Gazette limits the anthem to the first stanza, and Article 36B of the Constitution names it. It is played at flag-raising ceremonies in schools every Monday, timed so that the flag reaches the top as the music ends, and at the ceremony led by the President on Independence Day, 17 August.
== meaning
A song of devotion to Indonesia as a homeland and a single nation. It speaks of loyalty to the land and its people and looks forward to a great and free Indonesia. The mood is proud and resolute.
