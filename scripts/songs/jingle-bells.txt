== story
James Lord Pierpont copyrighted the song as "The One Horse Open Sleigh" on 16 September 1857, credited as "Song and Chorus written and composed by J. Pierpont", and dedicated it to John P. Ordway, organiser of a troupe called Ordway's Aeolians. It was first performed the day before, on 15 September 1857, at Ordway Hall in Boston, by the blackface minstrel performer Johnny Pell. It belonged to the then-popular genre of sleighing songs and was possibly intended as a drinking song. Where and when Pierpont wrote it is not known. A plaque at 19 High Street in Medford, Massachusetts, claims that he wrote it there in 1850, at what was then the Simpson Tavern, and local histories said it was inspired by the town's sleigh races; the researcher Kyna Hamill proposes that it was written in Boston before Pierpont moved south in the autumn of 1857. She also argues that, because its words closely resemble lines of many other sleighing songs and Pierpont constantly needed money, he wrote it purely as a business venture.

By the time it was published Pierpont had moved to Savannah, Georgia, as organist and music director of the Unitarian Church, where his brother, the Rev. John Pierpont Jr., was minister; in August 1857 he married Eliza Jane Purse, daughter of the mayor of Savannah, and he never returned north. Oliver Ditson and Company of Boston republished the song in 1859 under a new title, "Jingle Bells; or, The One Horse Open Sleigh". In New England before the motor car, horses' harnesses carried straps of bells to avoid collisions, since a sleigh on snow is almost silent, and the rhythm of the tune seems to imitate a trotting horse's bells.

The song had no original connection with Christmas. It became associated with winter and Christmas in the 1860s and 1870s and appeared in parlour-song and college anthologies in the 1880s. The banjoist Will Lyle recorded it on an Edison cylinder on 30 October 1889, believed to be the first Christmas record; no copy survives. The earliest surviving recording, by the Edison Male Quartette in 1898, is part of a Christmas medley called "Sleigh Ride Party". The chorus sung today is simpler than the 1857 original, whose descending chords bear some resemblance to Pachelbel's Canon; who changed it, and who changed the words of the first two stanzas and chorus, is not known, but the modern refrain dates back at least to 1898.

It became a Christmas favourite in the early twentieth century and one of the most performed secular holiday songs in the world. Hit recordings include those of Benny Goodman and His Orchestra (1935), Glenn Miller and His Orchestra (1941), Bing Crosby and the Andrews Sisters (1943, over a million copies sold), Les Paul (1951) and Kimberley Locke (2006). On 16 December 1965 the Gemini 6 astronauts Tom Stafford and Wally Schirra reported a mysterious object to Mission Control as a Christmas prank and then played the tune on a smuggled harmonica and sleigh bells, one of the first songs broadcast from space; Schirra gave the tiny harmonica to the Smithsonian in 1967. Pierpont was voted into the Songwriters Hall of Fame.
== meaning
A sleighing song: it celebrates winter fun and the speed of a one-horse open sleigh, its rhythm imitating a trotting horse's bells. Nothing in it was written for Christmas.
