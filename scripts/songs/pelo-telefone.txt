== story
"Pelo Telefone" ("On the Telephone") is held, according to the records of the National Library of Brazil, to be the first samba song recorded in Brazil, although earlier recordings exist, such as "Samba – Em Casa da Bahiana" (1913) and "Urubu Malandro" (1914). It was made in 1916 in the backyard of Tia Ciata, in Praça Onze (now Cidade Nova), whose house, a famous Candomblé house, was frequented by the popular musicians of the time. First titled "Roceiro", it was a collective creation, born of improvisation in a samba circle, with João da Baiana, Pixinguinha, Caninha, Hilário Jovino Ferreira, Sinhô and others taking part.

Its authorship has been disputed ever since. Donga (Ernesto dos Santos) registered it on 27 November 1916 under his own name alone, and later added the journalist Mauro de Almeida as his partner. He justified registering it with a saying attributed to Sinhô, that music is like a bird and belongs to whoever catches it first. Because the song was a huge success, various musicians claimed it. On 4 February 1917 the newspaper Jornal do Brasil printed a note from the Grêmio Fala Gente announcing that "the true tango 'Pelo Telephone'", by João da Mata, Germano, Tia Ciata and Hilário, would be sung on Avenida Rio Branco, "dedicated to the good and remembered friend Mauro".

The original words were later changed into the version best known today, in which the chief of police sends word by telephone that there is a roulette wheel to play at the Carioca. Donga told Brazil's Museum of Image and Sound that this was a parody made by the journalists of the newspaper A Noite: in 1913 its reporters had set up a roulette wheel in the Largo da Carioca to show how the police tolerated gambling. The musician and broadcaster Henrique Foréis Domingues confirmed this in the newspaper O Dia on 13 February 1972, and accused Donga of having taken a collective work for himself. Donga conceded that he had not written the words, which were Mauro de Almeida's, and blamed the record label: "The omission of Mauro's name on the recording by Casa Edison cannot be attributed to me", he said.
== meaning
A light carnival samba. In its first words the chief of the revelry sends word by telephone that the merrymaking is to be enjoyed without question; in the version best known today he becomes the chief of police, sending word that a roulette wheel is waiting in the Carioca, a mocking jab at the police's tolerance of gambling. The mood is playful and satirical.
