== story
The Wexford Carol, also called the Enniscorthy Carol (in Irish Carúl Loch Garman or Carúl Inis Córthaidh), is a traditional Irish Christmas carol that comes from Enniscorthy in County Wexford. It is sometimes known by the opening of its first verse. Its subject is the nativity of Jesus Christ.

Its origins are uncertain. It is occasionally claimed to date from the early Middle Ages, but its musical and lyrical style suggest that it was more likely composed in the 15th or 16th century. Neither an author nor a composer is known.

The carol owes its renewed popularity to William Grattan Flood (1859–1928), organist and musical director at St. Aidan's Cathedral in Enniscorthy. He transcribed it from the performance of a local singer and had it published in The Oxford Book of Carols, after which other carol books took it up as well.

Traditions have gathered around the song, for example that only men should sing it. Many well-known women singers have nevertheless recorded it, among them Julie Andrews in 1966, whose version has an additional verse, and Loreena McKennitt in 1987. Nanci Griffith recorded it for the Chieftains' 1989 album A Chieftains Celebration, and John Rutter recorded his arrangement in 2002 with the Cambridge Singers and the baritone Stephen Varcoe. Yo-Yo Ma and Alison Krauss recorded it for Ma's 2008 album Songs of Joy & Peace.

It is now sung by choirs and soloists well beyond Ireland. The English boys' choir Libera recorded an arrangement at Armagh Cathedral for a 2013 album, the Mormon Tabernacle Choir sang an arrangement by its conductor Mack Wilberg on a 2016 album, and the Irish tenor Anthony Kearns performs it every year. In 2014 it gave its name to The Wexford Carols, a collection of traditional Irish carols by the Irish early-music singer Caitríona O'Leary.
== meaning
A carol that calls its hearers to remember the birth of Christ at Christmas and tells the nativity story in turn: the birth at Bethlehem, the shepherds and the wise men. Its tone is devout and quietly joyful.
