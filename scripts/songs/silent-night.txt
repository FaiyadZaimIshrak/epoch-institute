== story
Joseph Mohr, a young Catholic priest, wrote the poem "Stille Nacht" in 1816, in the aftermath of the Napoleonic Wars, at Mariapfarr in the Lungau region of Salzburg, his father's home town, where he worked as an assistant priest. In 1817 he came to Oberndorf, a village on the Salzach river, then in the Austrian Empire.

On Christmas Eve 1818 Mohr brought the words to Franz Xaver Gruber, schoolmaster and organist in the nearby village of Arnsdorf, and asked him to compose a melody with guitar accompaniment for that night's mass; flooding of the river had possibly damaged the church organ. The song was first performed that night in the Nikolauskirche, Oberndorf's parish church. The church was later destroyed by repeated flooding and replaced by the Silent-Night-Chapel. The story, told in many films, that the organ had broken down comes from a fictional tale published in the United States in the 1930s.

According to Gruber, the organ builder Karl Mauracher, who serviced the Oberndorf instrument, took the song home with him to the Zillertal. There two travelling families of folk singers, the Strassers and the Rainers, took it into their shows. The Rainers were singing it around Christmas 1819, once performed it before an audience that included Franz I of Austria and Alexander I of Russia, and gave its first performance in the United States, in New York City in 1839. By the 1840s it was well known in Lower Saxony and reportedly a favourite of Frederick William IV of Prussia. Over these years the melody changed slightly: Gruber's original was a moderato tune in 6/8 time with a siciliana rhythm, while the version sung today is a slow, meditative lullaby or pastorale. The first edition was published by Friese in 1833 in a collection of Four Genuine Tyrolean Songs.

For a long time the song was attributed to more famous Austrian composers, including Haydn, Mozart and Schubert. In 1995 a manuscript in Mohr's handwriting, dated by researchers to about 1820, was discovered; it showed that Mohr wrote the words in 1816 and Gruber composed the music in 1818. It is the earliest surviving manuscript and the only one in Mohr's hand.

In 1859 John Freeman Young, an Episcopal priest serving at Trinity Church in New York City, published the English translation most often sung today, made from three of Mohr's six verses. The carol has been translated into about 300 languages. It was first recorded in 1905 and has more than 137,000 known recordings. UNESCO declared it an intangible cultural heritage in 2011.
== meaning
A quiet Christmas night song about the birth of Jesus: the newborn child sleeps under the watch of his parents while shepherds hear the news from angels. Its mood is calm, tender and devotional, close to a cradle song.
