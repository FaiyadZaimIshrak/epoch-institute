== story
"Siyahamba" is a Zulu chorus that arose in a rural Protestant congregation, possibly in the former province of Natal in South Africa. It may have been sung orally before 1952, when Andries van Tonder of Dundee, Natal, first wrote it down. Van Tonder is credited with the earliest known Afrikaans version of the words; who made the melody and the Zulu words is not known, and van Tonder may have composed it or it may be a Zulu folk song. Its words may draw on Methodist or Pentecostal hymns, while its melody is closer to African and African-American patterns than to conventional European church music. The title means "We are marching" or "We are walking" in Zulu.

In 1978 the Swedish choir Fjedur toured South Africa at the invitation of the Evangelical Lutheran Church of South Africa, and its musical director, Anders Nyberg, heard and recorded the song at a girls' school in Appelsbosch, Natal. In 1984 Nyberg arranged it for four voices and published it in the songbook and recording Freedom is Coming: Songs of Protest and Praise from South Africa. It then entered North American hymnals as "We Are Marching in the Light of God": GIA Publications' Gather Comprehensive (1994), widely used in American Catholic parishes, the United Church of Christ's New Century Hymnal (1995), Community of Christ Sings (2002) and the Unitarian Universalist supplement Singing the Journey (2005).

The song was not written as a protest song and seems not to have been prominent among anti-apartheid campaigners inside South Africa. After Nyberg brought it to Europe and the United States in the 1980s, however, it was often used in the international effort to end apartheid, above all in the campaign of the Western churches, because of its devotional message. Today it is seen in South Africa and abroad as a liberation song. It is sung in churches, at concerts and by children's groups, as a processional and at offertory, and at rallies, demonstrations and processions, where its words are sometimes changed to fit the cause, as they are in some secular performances.
== meaning
A hymn of faith whose theme is a people moving forward together; its mood is joyful and steady, and its one repeated phrase gives it a cyclical, communal feel suited to processions.
