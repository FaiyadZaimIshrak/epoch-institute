== story
"Malaika" is a Swahili love song, possibly the most famous in Tanzania, Kenya and East Africa and one of the most widely known Swahili songs in the world. Malaika means "angel" in Swahili, a word long used for a beautiful girl. The title varies from version to version, as "Malaika Nakupenda Malaika", "Ewe Malaika" or "My Angel", and so do the words, whose verses are often rearranged, left out or combined.

Who wrote it is still disputed. Most people credit Adam Salim, a little-known Tanzanian songwriter born in 1916, who by this account composed it in Nairobi in 1945 for his girlfriend Halima Ramadhani Maruwa; their parents disapproved, and Halima was made to marry a wealthy man. The Kenyan singer Fadhili William always claimed the song, describing in detail how he wrote it, and he is recognised as its composer for royalty purposes. The producer Charles Worrod credited it instead to Grant Charo, William's brother-in-law, a claim Charo is not known to have confirmed, and Lucas Tututu of Mombasa has also claimed it.

Fadhili William was the first to record it, with his band the Jambo Boys, in 1959 or 1960. Charles Worrod re-recorded it at Equator Sound Studios and marketed it until it became known internationally, and Miriam Makeba's early recording, which added a third verse, made it famous across Africa and then the world. Through her it reached Harry Belafonte, Pete Seeger, Usha Uthup and Angélique Kidjo, and it was also recorded by the Brothers Four, Helmut Lotti and Rocco Granata, among many others.

In 1967 the Swedish group the Hep Stars, who had heard the song from a hotel chef in Nairobi while filming there, recorded it in Stockholm. Their single spent five weeks at No. 1 on the Swedish chart Kvällstoppen, the only song wholly in an African language to top a Swedish chart. The German group Boney M. released it in 1981 as the first single from their album Boonoonoonoos; it reached No. 13 in Germany and the top ten in Spain, and a "Lambada Remix" followed in 1989. In 2023 the South African group The Soil recorded a version inspired by Makeba's from the 1980s.
