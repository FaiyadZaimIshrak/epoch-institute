== story
"Ya Rayah" ("you, the one leaving") is an Algerian chaâbi song written and performed in 1973 by Dahmane El Harrachi, whose name was Amrani Abderrahmane. It belongs to the chaâbi and Andalusian tradition of Algiers. It is a ballad of the traveller and the exile, of the emigrant and the longing to come back, and that theme has given it a wide appeal.

In 1993 the French-Algerian singer Rachid Taha recorded it on his second album, which bore his own name. He released it as a single in August 1997, from the compilation album Carte Blanche; it reached number eleven on the French singles chart and was certified silver in France in February 1998. It also appeared on his album Diwân (1998). In September 1998 Taha sang it with the Algerian singers Khaled and Faudel at the Palais omnisports de Paris-Bercy, and their version appeared on the live album 1,2,3 Soleils the following year. In 2003 he sang it as a duet with Enrico Macias at the Olympia in Paris. After Taha's death the song returned to the French top 20.

It has been sung in many languages, among them Hebrew, Hindi, Turkish, Greek, Russian and Serbian. Its tune was used in the Bollywood film Mann (1999), and it was sampled in an Israeli rap song in 2010. In 2012 the Algerian singer Amel Bouchoucha recorded it with African dance beats and Berber sounds.
== meaning
A song of exile and emigration, about leaving home and the longing to return. The mood is melancholy and reflective.
