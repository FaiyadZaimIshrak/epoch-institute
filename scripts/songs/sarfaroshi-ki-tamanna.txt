== story
"Sarfaroshi Ki Tamanna" ("the desire for sacrifice") is an Urdu patriotic poem written by Bismil Azimabadi as a dedication to the young freedom fighters of the Indian independence movement. It was written in 1921, after the Jallianwala Bagh massacre and other atrocities by the British colonial rulers, and first published in the journal Sabah, published from Delhi. It is a ghazal of 11 couplets. The Khuda Bakhsh Library has preserved the original copy and the page of the poet's diary holding the poem, with corrections by his mentor, Shad Azimabadi.

The poem was popularised by Ram Prasad Bismil, an Indian freedom fighter, who made it a war cry against British rule; when he was put on the gallows, its opening lines were on his lips. It has also been associated with the younger generation of freedom fighters between the wars, among them Ashfaqullah Khan, Bhagat Singh and Chandrashekhar Azad.

It has kept its place in public life. Manmohan Singh recited its first line in his Budget Speech of 1992 in the Lok Sabha, and in recent years it has been taken up by mass movements such as the anti-CAA protests in India and the Pakistani Students Solidarity March. It has been used in many Hindi films, from Manoj Kumar's Shaheed (1965), on the life of Bhagat Singh, to Rang De Basanti (2006) and Bhuj: The Pride of India (2021).
== meaning
A poem of readiness for sacrifice in the cause of freedom, dedicated to the young people who fought for it. The mood is fierce and unafraid.
