== story
"Tala al-Badru Alayna" is a traditional Islamic nasheed, a devotional song in Arabic. Many sources state that it was first sung when Muhammad reached Medina after leaving his home city of Mecca in the Hijra, as the people of the city welcomed him.

The account has been debated over a place the song names, Thaniyat al-Wadāʿ (in the song, in the plural). Some read this as a pass north of Medina and argue that the report is geographically unlikely, since Muhammad is said to have arrived first at Qubāʾ, south of the city. Classical geographers, however, show that more than one place bore the name. The medieval geographer Yāqūt al-Ḥamawī described Thaniyat al-Wadāʿ as a pass used by travellers heading towards Mecca, and other early writers also mention a pass of that name on the Meccan side of Medina. Ibn Hajar al-ʽAsqalani recorded that many authorities placed a Thaniyat al-Wadāʿ on the Mecca road, while noting another on the Syrian side, and reports cited by the historian al-Samḥūdī describe Muhammad coming from Qubāʾ and passing near a place of that name before entering the city. Some scholars take the plural to mean several passes. On this basis a number of historians regard the geographical objection as inconclusive and the reference as compatible with an approach from the south as well as from the north.

An alternative view, put forward mainly by Ibn Hajar al-ʽAsqalani, is that the song was sung to welcome Muhammad back to Medina after the campaign of Tabuk.

The song is widely performed today. Recorded versions include those of Oum Kalthoum, Mishary Rashid Alafasy, Sami Yusuf, Yusuf Islam (Cat Stevens), Mesut Kurtis, Native Deen, Raef, Maher Zain, Junaid Jamshed, Payam Azizi and Marufur Rahman. It was used in the soundtrack of Moustapha Akkad's 1976 film The Message, in the scene of the Hijra, and has been taken up far beyond the Muslim world: Olivia Newton-John sang it as an interlude on Grace and Gratitude (2006), and in December 2015, as Syrian refugees first began arriving in Canada, a children's choir in Ottawa sang an arrangement by Laura Hawley at a holiday concert.
== meaning
A song of welcome in which the community greets an arrival as a light rising over them, and gives thanks for him. In the tradition it voices the joy of the people of Medina at receiving Muhammad.
