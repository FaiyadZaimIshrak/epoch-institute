== story
"Maamme" ("Our Land"), known by its original Swedish title as "Vårt land", is the de facto national anthem of Finland. Its words are the prologue to Johan Ludvig Runeberg's verse cycle The Tales of Ensign Stål (Fänrik Ståhls sägner), a classic example of Romantic nationalism. The poem, written in Swedish in 1846 but not printed until 1848, has eleven stanzas. The music was composed by the German immigrant Fredrik Pacius, who is said to have written the tune in four days, and the song was first performed on 13 May 1848.

The Finnish text sung today is usually credited to Paavo Cajander's 1889 translation of Ensign Stål, but it in fact comes from the 1867 translation by Julius Krohn. In the 1880s and again in the 1920s there were attempts to replace the song with one written in Finnish, but these ceased by the 1930s. The song was popular throughout the 19th century, yet it gained its present position only after Pacius's death. Its melody is also used for the Estonian anthem "Mu isamaa, mu õnn ja rõõm" ("My Fatherland, My Happiness and Joy") and for the Livonian anthem "Min izāmō" ("My Fatherland").

No law makes "Maamme" the anthem, in the way that laws define Finland's coat of arms and flag; its place has been established by convention, and in 2018 the Government of Finland described it as the "Finnish national anthem". Its status is still debated from time to time. Some Finns would prefer "Finlandia" by Jean Sibelius, with words by V. A. Koskenniemi in Finnish and Joel Rundt in Swedish: opposing legislative initiatives in the Finnish Parliament in 2003 proposed making either "Maamme" or the Finlandia Hymn the official anthem, a citizens' initiative of 2014 asked for the Finlandia Hymn, and in 2016 members of the National Coalition Party campaigned for it, but opinion in the party was divided and the campaign fell short.

In practice "Maamme" serves as the national anthem: it is played during state visits and represents Finland at international sporting events. Usually only the first and the last verses are sung. When Finnish and Swedish speakers are gathered together, three verses are sung: the first in Finnish, the first in Swedish and the last in Finnish.
== meaning
A hymn of love for the homeland: the singers declare that no land, however rich or fair, is dearer to them than their own poor northern country, honour its forebears' struggles, and end in the hope that its future will one day flower. The mood is earnest and tender.
