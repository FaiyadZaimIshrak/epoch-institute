== story
"Victimæ paschali laudes" is a sequence, a sung text of the Mass, prescribed for Easter Sunday in the Catholic Mass and in Anglican and Lutheran Eucharistic services. It is usually attributed to Wipo of Burgundy, an 11th-century chaplain to the Holy Roman Emperor Conrad II, but it has also been credited to Notker Balbulus, to Robert II of France and to Adam of St. Victor.

Before the Council of Trent (1545–1563) many feasts had sequences of their own, and some sixteen different sequences were in use for Easter alone. The Roman Missal of the Tridentine Mass, published in 1570 after the council, kept only four medieval sequences: this one, "Veni Sancte Spiritus" for Pentecost, "Lauda Sion" for Corpus Christi and "Dies irae" for the Requiem Mass. A fifth, the "Stabat Mater", was added by Pope Benedict XIII in 1727. "Victimæ paschali laudes" is one of the few sequences still in liturgical use today.

The 1570 missal also changed the text. It deleted a section with a pejorative reference to the Jews, altered the wording of one line, and added "Amen" and "Alleluia" at the end.

Many Renaissance and Baroque composers set the text, among them Busnois, Josquin, Lassus, Willaert, Hans Buchner, Palestrina, Byrd and Fernando de las Infantas; later settings include Perosi's and one for four-part choir and organ by the French composer Jehan Revert. German chorales grew from it: "Christ ist erstanden" in the 12th century and Martin Luther's "Christ lag in Todes Banden". In English it is often sung in Jane E. Leeson's metrical paraphrase, to tunes such as Victimae Paschali and St George's Windsor or, with alleluias, Easter Hymn and Llanfair.
== meaning
A song of praise for Easter Sunday, offered to Christ as the Easter sacrifice and celebrating his rising from the dead. The mood is solemn and joyful.
