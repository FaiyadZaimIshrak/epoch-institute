== story
"Dama Dam Mast Qalandar", "Every breath for the ecstasy of Qalandar", is a Sufi qawwali in honour of Lal Shahbaz Qalandar (1177–1274) of Sehwan Sharif, the most revered Sufi saint of Sindh. Its origins are unknown: no recording or written document mentions it before the 1950s. Legends at his shrine in Sehwan hold that the 13th-century Sufi poet Amir Khusrow first wrote it and that Bulleh Shah reworked it in the 18th century, adding verses in praise of Shahbaz Qalandar and giving it a strong colour of Sindhi culture. The poem names the town of Sehwan; its word "Lal", red, may refer to the saint as a young man, to his legendary ruby glow or to his red dress. It also honours Ali, the cousin and son-in-law of Muhammad.

It became a popular traditional Sufi folk song across the northern Indian subcontinent, above all in Punjab and Sindh, and in Iran. It is performed with dhamaal, the devotional whirling by which Sufis seek spiritual ecstasy, in tribute to Lal Shahbaz Qalandar and to Ali. The historian and anthropologist Michel Boivin writes that the name Jhulelal in the song was used historically by Sindhi Hindus for Jhulelal, an avatar of the god Varuna, and was later taken up by Sufi Muslims in this song; since its popularity, Lal Shahbaz Qalandar himself is often called Jhulelal or Jhulelal Qalandar.

The best-known modern melody is by the Pakistani composer Master Ashiq Hussain. It was first set, as "Lal Meri Pat", for the 1956 Pakistani film Jabroo and sung by Inayat Hussain Bhatti, Fazal Hussain and A. R. Bismil. Contrary to a popular legend, Hussain said he only composed a dhamaal version, at the request of the poet Saghar Siddiqui, who brought him the words in his own edited version of the original poem. Hussain later fell into poverty in a slum in Lahore, and when he died in 2017 most Pakistanis did not know he had composed the tune.

The most popular version, on Hussain's melody, is Noor Jehan's in the 1969 Pakistani film Dillan Dey Soudey, modified by Nazir Ali. Many have sung it since, in Pakistan Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, Aziz Mian, Abida Parveen, the Sabri Brothers, Reshma and the band Junoon, in Bangladesh Runa Laila, and in India Hans Raj Hans, the Wadali brothers and the Nooran Sisters, as well as in Indian films from Nasbandi (1978) to D-Day (2013). In 1991 Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan and M. Arshad made a new song on its theme, with a different melody, released as "Dam Mast Mast"; the Bollywood composer Viju Shah reworked it as "Tu Cheez Badi Hai Mast Mast" for the film Mohra (1994), whose soundtrack sold more than eight million copies.
== meaning
A devotional song that calls on Lal Shahbaz Qalandar, the red-robed saint of Sehwan, and on Ali, with every breath. Its driving refrain carries the singers and dancers towards ecstasy, and its verses praise the saint and ask for his help and blessing. The mood is fervent and joyful.
