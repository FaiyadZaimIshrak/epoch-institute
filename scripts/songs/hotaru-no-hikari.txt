== story
"Hotaru no Hikari" ("Glow of a firefly") is a Japanese song that sets new and entirely different words by Chikai Inagaki to the tune of the Scottish folk song "Auld Lang Syne". It was first published in 1881 (Meiji 14) in a collection of songs for elementary school pupils. Putting new words to an existing tune without changing the music much is known as contrafactum.

The words draw on the story of Che Yin in volume 83 of the Book of Jin, about a student who kept studying by the light of fireflies when he had no other light.

The song is commonly heard at graduation ceremonies and at the end of the school day, and many shops and restaurants play it to tell customers that they are closing for the day. It has become a tradition for all the performers to sing it as the last song of NHK's New Year's Eve programme Kōhaku Uta Gassen, and from 1993 until 2020 it was played at Tokyo Disney Resort during the New Year's Eve fireworks. "Aogeba Tōtoshi", another graduation song of the same period, is also thought to be based on a Scottish folk song.
== meaning
The song follows a hard-working student through a series of images of the hardships he endures in his pursuit of learning, beginning with the firefly's light by which he reads. Its mood is earnest and, in its use at graduations and closings, one of parting.
