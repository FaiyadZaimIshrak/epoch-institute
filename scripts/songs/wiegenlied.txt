== story
"Wiegenlied" ("Lullaby" or "Cradle Song"), Op. 49, No. 4, is a song for voice and piano by Johannes Brahms and one of his most famous pieces. He dedicated it to his friend Bertha Faber on the birth of her second son. Brahms had been in love with her when she was young, and he built the melody so that a song she used to sing to him runs beneath it as a hidden counter-melody. The music also draws in part on "S'Is Anderscht", a duet by Alexander Baumann published in the 1840s.

The words come from Des Knaben Wunderhorn, a collection of German folk poems. In its first publication the song had a single verse; Brahms later adapted a second verse from an 1849 poem by Georg Scherer. Simrock published Brahms's Op. 49 in November 1868, and the lullaby was first performed in public on 22 December 1869 in Vienna, by the singer Luise Dustmann with Clara Schumann at the piano.

The song has been called deceptively simple. In 1877 Brahms used its tune as the basis of the second theme of the first movement of his Second Symphony. It became one of his most popular songs and has been arranged and recorded many times: Percy Grainger set it for solo piano in 1922, a 1936 film about Brahms, with Albert Florath as the composer, took its title from the song's opening words, and the poet Wendy Cope wrote a poem about it. In 2005 the scholar Karen Bottge analysed it as an expression of the maternal voice.
== meaning
A cradle song sung over a child at bedtime. The mood is quiet and tender.
