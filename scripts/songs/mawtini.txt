== story
"Mawṭinī" ("My Homeland") is an Arabic patriotic poem by the Palestinian poet Ibrahim Tuqan, written around 1934 and set to music by the Lebanese composer Mohammed Flayfel, who also composed "Humat ad-Diyar", the national anthem of Syria. It became a popular patriotic song across the Arab world.

For much of the 20th century it served as an unofficial anthem of the Palestinians and of other peoples of the Middle East. In 1996 "Fidā'ī" officially took its place as the Palestinian national anthem, but many Palestinians still identify strongly with "Mawṭinī" and regard it as a second, unofficial national anthem.

In 2004 it became the national anthem of Iraq, on the order of Paul Bremer, the head of the Coalition Provisional Authority. Iraq had used an earlier, unrelated anthem of the same name: an instrumental "Mawṭinī" by Lewis Zanbaka, played in the late 1950s and early 1960s after Iraq became a republic. After the Ba'athist government was overthrown in 2003, that older piece was brought back for a short time in place of "Arḍulfurātayni", the anthem in use since 1981 and associated with Saddam Hussein's government, until Tuqan's song replaced it in 2004.

The song is widely seen as a reflection of the shared experiences of Arab peoples, and as a symbol of resistance and of the longing for liberation under occupation and oppression. Many Arab artists have recorded it anew; Elissa, Christina Sawaya and Faia Younan sang it in a video clip depicting the Syrian civil war.
== meaning
A song of deep love for the homeland and of commitment to its freedom. Its feelings move between sorrow, anger and hope, toward a vision of a free and united nation.
