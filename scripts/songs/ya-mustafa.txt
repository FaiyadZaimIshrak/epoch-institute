== story
"Ya Mustafa", also spelled "Ya Mustapha", is a song from Egypt composed by the Egyptian musician Mohamed Fawzi and recorded in many languages for its catchy tune. It was featured in the Egyptian film El Hub Keda (That's What Love Is, 1961), in which the singer Bob Azzam made a cameo appearance singing it alongside the leading actors Salah Zulfikar and Sabah, and it appears in other Egyptian films, among them one of the 1950s starring Ismail Yassin. The music shows the influence of Greek music. The song was very popular in the 1950s and early 1960s, and newer versions have revived it.

The song first became popular in Europe through Bob Azzam, an Egyptian-born singer, who released it in France in 1960. His version spent 14 weeks on the UK Singles Chart, peaking at number 23, and in Spain in 1960 the song reached number one in two versions, by Azzam and by José Guardiola. The same year a German version by the Greek singer Leo Leandros reached number 2 in Germany, Lola Novaković sang it in Serbian, and a Danish version, addressed to a lazy camel named Mustafa, was sung by the actors of the film Soldaterkammerater på vagt. It became especially popular in Greek, Turkish and Serbian. Darío Moreno recorded it in 1962, Dalida in 1984, and it was sung in Japanese by Kyu Sakamoto, in Albanian in Kosovo by Muharrem Qena, and in English and several Singaporean dialects by Dick Lee as "Mustapha".

Its tune travelled into other music. The British folk-blues guitarist Davy Graham played an instrumental jazz version on his album Folk, Blues and Beyond (1964), and Jimmy Page of Led Zeppelin played a short version of it in a BBC session as "White Summer/Black Mountainside". The Indian composers Nadeem-Shravan adapted the music for the film Aatish, the comedy troupe Warkop parodied the song in their first film, Mana Tahan (1979), and in Indonesia the tune is used, with new words, as a yell of the Scouts.

The rhythm and melody of the song also inspired many Egyptian football chants. For more than 30 years it has been the tune of the best-known chant of the fans of Al Ahly in Cairo against their rivals Zamalek, and of Zamalek's fans against Ahly: it opens with "Hela hela", a call of encouragement first used by labourers digging the Suez Canal, and goes on to insult the rival club. In 2019, during the earliest anti-government protests in Lebanon, protesters turned the chant against the caretaker Foreign Minister Gebran Bassil. His party, the Free Patriotic Movement, made a version praising him that did not catch on, and feminist activists in Beirut made another that aimed the insult at Bassil and his father-in-law, President Michel Aoun.
== meaning
A light popular song with a catchy, dancing tune, addressed, as its title says, to a man named Mustafa; cheerful in mood.
