== story
"Hawaiʻi Aloha", also called "Kuʻu One Hanau", is a revered anthem of the Native Hawaiian people and of the residents of Hawaiʻi. Its words were written by the Reverend Lorenzo Lyons (1807–1886), a Christian minister also known as Makua Laiana. He set them to an old hymn, "I Left It All With Jesus", composed by James McGranahan (1840–1907).

The Hawaiʻi State Legislature considered it for the official state song in 1967, and the Hawaiʻi State Constitutional Convention again in 1978, but both times "Hawaiʻi Ponoʻī" was chosen instead, a song written by King David Kalākaua and composed by Henri Berger, master of the Royal Hawaiian Band.

The song is sung at gatherings small and large, formal and informal, in Hawaiʻi and abroad, with the singers standing in a circle and holding hands. By tradition the last chorus is sung with hands raised above the head, a gesture that matters especially to advocates of the Hawaiian sovereignty movement. It is part of the inauguration of the Governor of Hawaiʻi and of the opening sessions of the Hawaiʻi State House of Representatives and State Senate. In 1968 the Kamehameha Boys' Concert Glee Club sang it at the funeral of Duke Kahanamoku, in an arrangement with nose flute, chanting and organ.
== meaning
A song of love for Hawaiʻi as a homeland, sung in Hawaiian to the tune of a Christian hymn. The mood is tender and devotional, and the singers' joined and raised hands make it a song of unity.
