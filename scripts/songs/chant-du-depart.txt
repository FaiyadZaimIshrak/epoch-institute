== story
"Le Chant du départ" ("The Song of Departure") is a war song of the French Revolution, with music by Étienne Méhul and words by Marie-Joseph Chénier, written in 1794. Its original title was "Anthem to Liberty"; Robespierre changed it to the present one. It was first performed on 14 July 1794, and 18,000 copies of the music were at once printed and sent out to the army. Republican soldiers called it "the brother of the Marseillaise".

The song is a musical tableau: each of its seven stanzas is sung by a different character or group. In the first a deputy rallies soldiers to fight for the Republic; in the second a mother offers her son's life to the fatherland. The fourth is sung by children in praise of Joseph Agricol Viala and Joseph Bara, boys of 15 and 13 who had died for Revolutionary France. By the legend, Bara was surrounded by royalist Vendeans and ordered to cheer for Louis XVII, cheered for the Republic instead and was killed on the spot; Viala was shot while trying to sabotage an enemy bridge.

It became the official anthem of the French Empire. The French Army still sings it, and it was sung during World War I. Valéry Giscard d'Estaing used it as his campaign song in the presidential election of 1974 and, as president, often had troops play it together with "La Marseillaise". Today it is the official presidential anthem of France and the unofficial regional anthem of French Guiana.
== meaning
A patriotic call to leave home and fight for the Republic, in which a series of voices, a deputy, a mother, children and others, each give their reason to defend the country. The mood is solemn and stirring, a march of farewell and duty.
