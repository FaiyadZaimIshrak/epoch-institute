== story
"Kaulana Nā Pua" ("Famous Are the Flowers") is a Hawaiian patriotic song written in 1893 by Ellen Kehoʻohiwaokalani Wright Prendergast. It is also known as "Mele ʻAi Pōhaku" ("Stone-Eating Song") and "Mele Aloha ʻĀina" ("Patriot's Song").

In 1893 Queen Liliʻuokalani was overthrown and the Hawaiian Kingdom ended. Those who seized power proclaimed the Provisional Government of Hawaii, which later became the Republic of Hawaii and then a territory and a state of the United States. Members of the Royal Hawaiian Band refused to accept it: when their bandmaster demanded that they sign an oath of loyalty to the Provisional Government, they walked out of their jobs. As the political scientist Noenoe Silva of the University of Hawaiʻi at Mānoa has explained, the bandmaster told them that if they did not sign they would be eating rocks. Prendergast wrote the song for these players. Its words express deep opposition to annexation by the United States.

According to Samuel H. Elbert and Noelani Mahoe (1970), the song was considered sacred and not for dancing. Today, however, hālau hula perform it as a hula ʻauana, a graceful dance for mature women. It remains popular in Hawaiʻi, though it is not clear how many listeners who do not speak Hawaiian know its history. The Hawaiian Renaissance has given it new significance, and its words are often cited in the Hawaiian sovereignty movement as an expression of opposition to United States rule.
== meaning
A song of loyalty to the Hawaiian land and to its overthrown queen. It praises the band players who refused the new government's oath, rejects trading loyalty to one's country for money, and holds that the land will go on feeding those who stay faithful to it. The mood is proud and defiant.
