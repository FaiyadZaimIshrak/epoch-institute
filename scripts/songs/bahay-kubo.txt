== story
"Bahay Kubo" is a famous Filipino folk song, often sung in schools, about a small house surrounded by vegetables. It takes its name from the báhay kúbo, a type of stilt house indigenous to the Philippines and the traditional basic design of houses among almost all the lowland peoples of the islands. The Filipino term roughly means "country house", from Tagalog: báhay ("house") comes from Proto-Malayo-Polynesian *balay, a public or community building, and kúbo ("hut") from *kubu, a field hut in the rice fields. The English name "nipa hut" was introduced in the American colonial era.

A bahay kubo was raised on posts of bamboo or hardwood against floods and pests, built of wood, bamboo, palm leaves and cogon grass, traditionally without nails, with a tall, steep thatched roof and large windows for air. Usually it was one room where the whole family ate, slept and worked, reached by a ladder; the space beneath held harvested crops, tools and livestock, and a separate granary, the kamálig, held the rice. Under Spanish rule its design shaped the stone-based bahay na bato of the wealthier classes.

The nipa hut is an icon of Philippine culture and stands for the Filipino value of bayanihan, a spirit of communal unity and shared effort. The song gives a generalised picture of what such a house might have looked like before colonial rule: a home surrounded by locally grown plants. It does not take into account the many other native house forms, such as those of the rulers of Mindanao, whose architecture shows strong Islamic influence.
== meaning
A song about a small country house and the vegetables and plants that grow around it, a homely picture of rural life.
