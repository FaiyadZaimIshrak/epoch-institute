== story
"Gaudete" ("Rejoice") is a sacred Christmas carol in Latin, thought to have been composed in the 16th century, though there is a known entry from around 1420 in the Hussite Jistebnice hymnal (Jistebnický kancionál). It was published in 1582 in Piae Cantiones, a collection of Finnish and Swedish sacred songs printed in the North German city of Greifswald. The collection gives no music for the verses; the standard tune comes from older liturgical books.

The text is a typical medieval song of praise and follows the usual pattern of its time: a uniform series of four-line stanzas, each preceded by a two-line refrain, which in the early English carol was called the burden. Carols of this kind could be on any subject, but most were about the Virgin Mary, the saints or the Christmas season.

The carol became widely known in the 1970s. The British folk rock group Steeleye Span recorded it a cappella, and the single reached number 14 in the UK singles chart in 1973. Their guitarist Bob Johnson had heard it at a folk-carol service in Cambridge, which he attended with his father-in-law. It is one of only two British top-20 hits sung entirely in Latin, the other being Sarah Brightman and Paul Miles-Kingston's "Pie Jesu" of 1986, and one of only a handful of a cappella hits. When the band performed it on Top of the Pops, the resident dance troupe walked on in medieval-style robes holding candles.

Since then it has been recorded by choirs, early-music ensembles and rock bands alike: the Boston Camerata under Joel Cohen (1985), the Finnish singer Pertti Neumann (1986), the King's Singers (1990), Anúna with Eimear Quinn (1996), the Mediæval Bæbes (1997), the boy choir Libera (2001), Tenebrae in an arrangement by Karl Jenkins (2004), Dominican friars in Kraków (2004), St Paul's Cathedral Choir under Andrew Carwood (2015) and the synth-pop group Erasure, whose electronic version of 2013 was the first single from their album Snow Globe. It has also been parodied, and Steeleye Span's version is sung along to in a car in the television comedy I'm Alan Partridge.
== meaning
A Christmas call to rejoice. A short refrain urging the listener to rejoice comes round before every four-line stanza of praise. The mood is festive and lively.
