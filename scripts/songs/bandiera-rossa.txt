== story
"Bandiera Rossa" ("Red Flag") is one of the best-known songs of the Italian labour movement. It celebrates the red flag as the symbol of the socialist and communist movements. Carlo Tuzzi wrote the text in 1908 and set it to a melody taken from two Lombard folk songs. The song is often called by its opening words instead, partly to avoid confusion with "Le tre bandiere", another Italian socialist song.

The text did not stay fixed. Several variants came to be identified with particular socialist or communist parties. After the rise of Benito Mussolini, two closing lines in praise of communism and liberty were added and the opening line was changed; in more recent renderings the word for communism at the end of the chorus is often replaced by the word for socialism.

The song has been sung in many languages, among them English, Serbo-Croatian, Slovenian, German, French, Swedish, Turkish, Kurdish, Finnish, Maltese, Russian, Esperanto, Indonesian, Japanese, Arabic, Hindi, Punjabi, Vietnamese and Filipino. Composers and performers have returned to it: Frederic Rzewski quoted it in two piano works of the 1970s, punk bands in Yugoslavia and Britain recorded it from the 1980s, and the 1986 Israeli film named after its opening words shows Israeli and Egyptian soldiers singing it mockingly in the Sinai after the 1967 War.
== meaning
A song in praise of the red flag as the emblem of the workers' cause. It calls working people forward to struggle and ends in a cheer for communism (or, in later versions, socialism) and liberty. Its mood is rousing and confident.
