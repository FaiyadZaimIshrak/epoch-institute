== story
"Hawaiʻi Ponoʻī" ("Hawaii's Own") was written in 1874 by King David Kalākaua of the Hawaiian Kingdom. The music is by Captain Henri Berger, then the king's royal bandmaster. Its melody recalls "God Save the King" and the Prussian anthem "Heil dir im Siegerkranz".

In 1876 it was adopted as the national anthem of the Hawaiian Kingdom, replacing "He Mele Lāhui Hawaiʻi", a composition by Liliʻuokalani. It remained the national anthem under the Republic of Hawaii, and after the United States annexed Hawaii in 1898 it continued in use as the islands' own song, the adopted song of the Territory of Hawaii.

In 1967 an act of the Hawaii State Legislature made it the official anthem of the U.S. state of Hawaii. It is commonly sung at sporting events in Hawaii immediately after the national anthem of the United States, and at many schools and at the meetings and events of local service organisations.
== meaning
A national anthem of loyalty to Hawaii and its own people, written by a reigning king. Its tone is stately, in the manner of the European royal anthems its melody recalls.
