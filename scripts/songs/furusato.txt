== story
"Furusato" ("old home" or "hometown") is a Japanese children's song of 1914, with music by Teiichi Okano and words by Tatsuyuki Takano. Takano's own hometown was Nakano, in Nagano, but the words do not seem to describe any particular place.

For decades the song was sung without its makers' names: its composer and writer were unknown until the 1970s. Since 1992 the names of both Teiichi Okano and Tatsuyuki Takano have been printed with it in Japanese music textbooks. The Japanese government has designated it as one of the children's songs to be taught in the public schools, and it was included in the collection Nihon no Uta Hyakusen.

The song was played at the closing ceremony of the 1998 Winter Olympics in Nagano. Plácido Domingo sang it in Japanese at a concert in NHK Hall on 10 April 2011. In 2014, for its hundredth anniversary, a children's chorus with orchestra performed it at the Saito Kinen Festival Matsumoto in Nagano, under the direction of Seiji Ozawa. An English version by Greg Irwin was published on the album "Japan's Best Loved Songs of the Season" in 1998 and has also been sung by Lexi Walker.
== meaning
The song speaks in the voice of someone working in a distant land who longs for the hills and fields of a childhood home. Its mood is quiet and wistful.
