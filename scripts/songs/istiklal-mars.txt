== story
"İstiklal Marşı" ("Independence March") was chosen in a nationwide competition organised in 1921, before the Ottoman Empire had been fully dissolved, by the Turkish National Movement, the forces led by Mustafa Kemal Atatürk that were fighting both invading foreign powers and the Ottoman court, which the movement held to be treasonous for its part in the partition of the homeland after the Treaty of Sèvres of 1920. The competition was to find a National March that would motivate the fighters of the Turkish War of Independence and give pride to the new homeland that victory would bring. A total of 724 poems were submitted. Mehmet Âkif Ersoy, a well-known poet of the time, at first refused to take part because a money prize was offered, but the National Parliament persuaded him to send a poem and set the reward aside. His manuscript was dedicated Kahraman Ordumuza, "To Our Heroic Army".

Ersoy's ten-stanza poem was recited to the National Assembly by the deputy Hamdullah Suphi on 1 March 1921 and, after a parliamentary committee had examined it, the deputies adopted it unanimously; it was officially adopted on 12 March 1921, two and a half years before the Republic of Turkey was proclaimed on 29 October 1923. The prize went to a society of veterans. An early setting by Çağatay was used for only six years. In 1930 Parliament adopted a new composition by Osman Zeki Üngör, a virtuoso composer and the first conductor of the Presidential Symphony Orchestra, and in 1932 the government commissioned the musician Edgar Manas to harmonise and orchestrate Üngör's melody, which gave the anthem its final, official form.

The full text has 41 lines in ten stanzas, but only the first two quatrains are sung at official ceremonies. The anthem is heard at state and military events, national festivals and bayrams, sporting events and school ceremonies. A framed copy usually hangs above the blackboard in Turkish classrooms, beside the flag, a photograph of Atatürk and his address to the nation's youth from the speech he ended on 20 October 1927, and the first two quatrains were printed on the back of the Turkish 100 lira banknotes of 1983–1989. In 1983 the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus also adopted the anthem, under Article II of its constitution.
== meaning
A poem of love for the homeland, of freedom and of faith, written for an army at war. It speaks to the flag, to the soil and to the human spirit, praising hope, devotion and sacrifice in the cause of liberty and remembering the soldiers who died for it. The mood is fervent and resolute.
