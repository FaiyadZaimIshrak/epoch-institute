== story
The American folk singer Woody Guthrie wrote "This Land Is Your Land" on 23 February 1940, in his room at the Hanover House hotel on 43rd Street in New York. He wrote it in critical response to Irving Berlin's "God Bless America": tired of hearing Kate Smith sing that song on the radio in the late 1930s, he first called his own, sarcastically, "God Blessed America for Me". The melody is close to "Oh, My Loving Brother", a Baptist gospel hymn that the Carter Family had recorded as "When the World's on Fire", and he used it for both verses and chorus. According to his biographer Joe Klein, Guthrie then forgot about the song for about five years.

In 1944, during the Second World War, Guthrie prepared a version without two verses critical of the United States; by then, after Germany's invasion of the Soviet Union in 1941, he supported American involvement in Europe. The earliest known recording, made in April 1944 and now held by the Smithsonian, still has one of those verses; it was issued in 1997. The song was first published in 1945, and the popular short version first appeared on record in 1951, on the album This Land Is My Land: American Work Songs (Folkways). It became widely known through songbooks from the mid-1940s through the 1950s. The critical verses, which can be read as a protest against the inequalities and hunger of the Great Depression, are still seldom sung in schools or at official occasions.

The first popular hit was a Canadian version. Encouraged by Pete Seeger, whose group the Weavers had recorded an unreleased version and who was then blacklisted, the Toronto folk group the Travellers rewrote it with Canadian places in about 1956; delegates sang it at the founding convention of the New Democratic Party in 1961. In the 1960s Bob Dylan, the Kingston Trio, Trini Lopez, Peter, Paul and Mary and others recorded it, drawn by its political message. Bruce Springsteen sang it on tour from 1980, and in January 2009 he and Pete Seeger, with Seeger's grandson Tao Rodríguez-Seeger, sang it with the verses restored at the celebration of Obama's inauguration at the Lincoln Memorial. Jennifer Lopez sang part of it at Joe Biden's inauguration in 2021.

Its copyright has been disputed. In 2004 the publisher The Richmond Organization, through Ludlow Music, threatened the website JibJab over a parody of the presidential election; the Electronic Frontier Foundation, defending JibJab, found that the song had been published in 1945 but registered only in 1956, so that the copyright had not been renewed in time and the 1945 version had been in the public domain since 1973. The publisher settled but still claims copyright in the later published versions. A 2016 lawsuit against those claims was dismissed in 2020 without a ruling on them. A 1947 recording entered the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1989, and in 2002 the song was added to the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry.
== meaning
A song claiming the country and its landscapes for all of its ordinary people, written as an answer to a patriotic hymn. Its tone is warm and plain, with an edge of protest against poverty and exclusion.
