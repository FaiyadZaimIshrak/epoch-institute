== story
"¡Ay Carmela!" is one of the best-known songs of the Republican troops in the Spanish Civil War. Its tune was first a nineteenth-century folk song, "El Paso del Ebro", which commemorated the rout of Napoleonic troops across the river Ebro in 1807, during the War of Independence.

During the Civil War, as with many older folk songs, the Republican side set new words to the melody, in several versions: "El Ejército del Ebro", "El paso del Ebro", "¡Ay, Carmela!", "¡Ay, Manuela!", "Rumba la Rumba" and "Viva la XV Brigada". The two most popular are "El Paso del Ebro", tied to the Battle of the Ebro, and "Viva la XV Brigada", which names the Battle of Jarama: two of the main battles of the war. One verse refers to the Regulares, the much-feared Moroccan units that served as the Nationalists' shock troops. The Nationalists also made a less known version of their own, "El Río del Nervión".

The song has lived on as an anti-fascist song. In 2000 the Croatian singer and songwriter Darko Rundek released a version with anti-fascist words on his album U širokom svijetu, and it was widely sung in Belgrade, above all during the Serbian protests of 2018–2020. Carlos Saura's film ¡Ay, Carmela! (1990) takes its title from the song, which opens and closes the film.
== meaning
A Republican soldiers' song of the Spanish Civil War, defiant and lively, recalling the crossing of the Ebro and the battles of the war.
