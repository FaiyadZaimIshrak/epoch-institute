== story
"Morq-e Sahar" is a tasnif, a Persian art song, with words by the poet Mohammad-Taqi Bahar and music by Morteza Neidavoud. It was made in the early 20th century in the wake of the Iranian Constitutional Revolution, and it has often been sung in protest against injustice, dictatorship and tyranny in Iran; it is often called the "anthem" of Iran's struggles for freedom.

The first singer to perform it was Qamar-ol-Moluk Vaziri, who sang it in 1924 at a Tehran hotel in her first formal concert. It was first recorded in 1927 by Iranoddole Helen (Taj Esfahani); one of the first recordings is also attributed to Moluk Zarrabi. The song has two stanzas. The first was described as the more lyrical and was often sung in public; the second, which deals with social and political questions, was sung at private parties, where talk turned more easily to politics. After Reza Shah Pahlavi heard the song performed at a gathering in the home of his minister Teymourtash, he banned the second stanza in 1925, and it stayed banned until his forced abdication in 1941.

Many Iranian singers have performed it since, among them Mohammad-Reza Shajarian, Homayoun Shajarian, Leila Forouhar, Farhad Mehrad, Shakila, Mohsen Namjoo, Reza Sadeghi and Hengameh Akhavan. It became the signature piece of Mohammad-Reza Shajarian, who was widely seen as a public voice for democracy and closed all his concerts with it. When Shajarian died in 2020, crowds of mourners gathered at the Jam Hospital in Tehran, where he had been treated, sang "Morq-e Sahar" and chanted slogans against authoritarian rule, which led to a confrontation with the Iranian authorities.
== meaning
A song of lament and of longing for freedom, built on the image of the dawn bird of its title. It was heard in its time as a call for liberty.
