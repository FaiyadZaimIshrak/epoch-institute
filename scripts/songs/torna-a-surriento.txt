== story
"Torna a Surriento" ("Come back to Sorrento") is a Neapolitan song composed by the Italian musician Ernesto De Curtis to words by his brother, the poet and painter Giambattista De Curtis. Tradition holds that it was written in 1902, when Guglielmo Tramontano, the mayor of Sorrento, asked his friend Giambattista to write a song for the Prime Minister, Giuseppe Zanardelli, who was then staying at Tramontano's seaside hotel, the Imperial Hotel Tramontano; it was said to celebrate Zanardelli's stay, and some say it was a plea to him to keep his promise to help the poor town, which needed above all a sewage system. More recent research suggests the song was only reworked for the occasion: family papers show that the brothers had deposited a copy with the Italian Society of Authors and Editors in 1894, eight years before they said they wrote it. It was officially copyrighted in 1905.

It became one of the most popular songs of the Neapolitan tradition, beside "'O sole mio", "Funiculì, Funiculà" and "Santa Lucia". Claude Aveling wrote English words for it, "Come Back to Sorrento", and it has been sung by performers of many kinds: the musician Bono ends his film Bono: Stories of Surrender (2025) by singing its last two verses, after remembering his father, a tenor. It was heard throughout the 1954 film Seagulls Over Sorrento, played on the concertina.
== meaning
A love song set against the beauty of Sorrento and its surroundings, asking a loved one to come back. The mood is longing and tender.
