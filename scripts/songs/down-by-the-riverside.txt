== story
"Down by the Riverside", also known as "Ain't Gonna Study War No More" and "Gonna Lay Down My Burden", is an African-American spiritual whose roots go back to before the American Civil War. It was first published in 1918, in Plantation Melodies: A Collection of Modern, Popular and Old-time Negro-Songs of the Southland (Chicago, the Rodeheaver Company), and has been known under many other titles, among them "Ain't Gwine to Study War No More", "Down by de Ribberside" and "Going to Pull My War-Clothes". The Fisk University jubilee quartet first recorded it in 1920 (Columbia published the record in 1922), and there were at least 14 Black gospel recordings of it before the Second World War.

The song has many variants. Each stanza usually keeps the same frame and changes one line: in place of laying down a burden the singer may lay down a sword and shield, try on a long white robe or a starry crown, shake hands around the world, talk with the Prince of Peace, cross the river Jordan or walk down the road of peace. Its pictures come from the Bible. The riverside suggests baptism and the crossing of the Jordan into the Promised Land, and the refrain echoes the Book of Isaiah (2:4), where nations "neither shall they learn war any more"; the "Prince of Peace" is a common title of Jesus, and the road and the mountain stand for the hard journey of life.

Because of these images of peace, the spiritual has also been sung as an anti-war protest song, especially during the Vietnam War, and it appears in collections of socialist and labour songs. Its tune has been borrowed for jingles and parodies, from Allan Sherman's "Don't Buy the Liverwurst" and a McDonald's jingle of the 1960s to "U.N. Me" in the cartoon series Animaniacs.
== meaning
A spiritual of letting go: by the riverside, before crossing over, the singer lays down burdens, anger and the weapons of war, and puts on new clothes of the spirit. Its refrain, after the prophet Isaiah, vows to study war no more, which is why it later served as a song of peace; the mood is joyful and resolute.
