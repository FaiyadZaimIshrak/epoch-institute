== story
"Sumer is icumen in", also called the Summer Canon and the Cuckoo Song, is a medieval English round, or rota, of the mid-13th century. Its first line means roughly "Summer has come". It is written in the Wessex dialect of Middle English. Its maker is not known: it may have been W. de Wycombe, or John of Fornsete, a monk of Reading Abbey. The manuscript that preserves it was copied between 1261 and 1264, in mensural notation, and is now in the British Library.

It is the oldest known musical composition in six-part polyphony. A rota (Latin for "wheel") is a kind of round: one singer begins, and a second starts again from the beginning when the first reaches a point marked with a red cross. The main tune runs over twelve such bars, while two further voices sing a repeated two-bar figure called the pes ("foot") underneath. The instructions for performing it are written in Latin in the manuscript itself. Because the earliest known copy was found at Reading Abbey it is sometimes called the Reading Rota, though it was probably not drafted there; a stone relief of the manuscript now hangs on the wall of the ruined chapter house of the abbey.

Beneath the English words the manuscript carries a second, Latin text on the Crucifixion of Jesus, to be sung to the same music. The English words celebrate summer much as the French reverdie ("re-greening") celebrates spring, but scholars have questioned that reading: the poem lacks the usual words of spring renewal, and its cuckoo and farmyard noises may carry double meanings. One phrase about a buck has been argued over since Victorian times, some editors reading it as earthy, others as a more decent word, and a study of medieval dialect spellings from Reading and Leominster has questioned the earthy reading.

The rota found new audiences in the 20th century. The English Singers made the first studio recording in New York around 1927, and recordings for music-history lectures and albums followed in 1929, about 1930 and 1936. A boys' choir sings it at the climax of Benjamin Britten's Spring Symphony (first performed 1949), and children danced to it around the stadium track at the opening ceremony of the 1972 Summer Olympics in Munich. Little John whistles its tune in the 1938 film The Adventures of Robin Hood, and villagers sing a version of it at the climax of the 1973 film The Wicker Man. It has been recorded by the Hilliard Ensemble, Richard Thompson, The Futureheads and others, and parodied by Ezra Pound, Peter Schickele and A. Y. Campbell.
== meaning
The English words welcome the summer with the call of the cuckoo and the lively noises of the farmyard animals. The mood is cheerful and noisy, a round meant to be sung over and over. The Latin words beneath, sung to the same music, turn instead to a sober meditation on Christ's sacrifice.
