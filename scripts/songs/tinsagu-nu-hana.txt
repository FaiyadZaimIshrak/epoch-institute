== story
"Tinsagu nu Hana", also "Chinsagu nu Hana", is an Okinawan children's song in the Okinawan language. Its title can be translated as "The Balsam Flowers": Okinawan children would squeeze the sap from balsam flowers to stain their fingernails, as a way to ward off evil. Its words are Confucian teachings, of the traditional Ryukyuan values such as filial piety. Of its first six verses, the first three are about filial piety and the last three about respecting one's body and one's goals. Every verse has exactly the same number of notes, in the language and metre that are uniquely Okinawan and are called ryūka. The song has ten verses, but the last four are missing from the text usually printed.

On 18 March 2012, after a public survey, the government of Okinawa Prefecture designated "Tinsagu nu Hana" as "Okinawa Prefecture's favorite song". It then became an official symbol of the prefecture, its second musical symbol beside the "Song of Okinawa Prefecture".

The song has also travelled beyond Okinawa. Ryuichi Sakamoto used it on his album Beauty; according to Felicity Collins of La Trobe University, this was the first time the song was "appropriated for a 'world music' audience". It is heard at the end of the Australian film Japanese Story (2003), sung by Shelley Scown with orchestral work by Elizabeth Drake, as the heroine, Sandy, watches the aircraft that carries the body of Hiromitsu Tachibana, a Japanese man she has come to know, taxi and prepare to take off. Collins wrote that some Japanese critics found this use contentious: the song has a specifically Okinawan character and is not something generally associated with Japan, and the fragility and beauty of a song about upholding Okinawan identity seemed to them out of place in an Australian film.
== meaning
A song of moral teaching for children. Each verse sets an image beside a lesson: as balsam juice colours the fingertips, a parent's teaching should colour the heart; the stars of the sky can be counted, but not a parent's teachings; a ship at night steers by the north star as a child is guided by its parents; a jewel left unpolished tarnishes, so a person must work on themselves every day; the sincere will see their wishes come true; and anything can be done if one tries. The mood is gentle and earnest.
