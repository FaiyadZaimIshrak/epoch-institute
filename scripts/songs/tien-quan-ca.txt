== story
"Tiến quân ca" ("The Song of the Marching Troops") is the national anthem of Vietnam. Văn Cao wrote the words and composed the music of the march in 1944, drawing its words and title from his earlier work "Thăng Long" (a former name of Hanoi). The words went through many changes in their early stages; Văn Cao revised several lines after many suggestions, toning down the fiercest of them. When the song was finished he let Vũ Quý try it out, and it was published in the papers in November 1944 with lithographs by Văn Cao.

On 17 August 1945 the song was sung for the first time in public, at a rally of civil servants in Hanoi under the flag of the Viet Minh. The poet and musician Nguyễn Đình Thi, moved on hearing Văn Cao sing it, asked each of them to write another song for the Viet Minh front: he wrote "Diệt Phát Xít" ("Kill the Fascists"), later the start-up song of Voice of Vietnam, and Văn Cao wrote "Chiến Sĩ Việt Nam" ("Vietnam Warriors").

On 17 August 1945 Hồ Chí Minh approved the song as the anthem of the newly established Democratic Republic of Vietnam. On 2 September 1945, the day independence was declared, the Liberation Army band under Đinh Ngọc Liên played it in a parade at Ba Đình Square; the day before, Đinh Ngọc Liên, Nguyễn Hữu Hiếu and Văn Cao had shortened two notes to make it more snappy. In 1946 the first National Assembly recognised it as the national anthem, in Article 3 of the first Constitution. In 1955 the Assembly invited authors to edit it again, and Văn Cao regretted that the song's heroic spirit had been lost. After the fall of the South Vietnamese government and the reunification of the country on 2 July 1976, it became the anthem of the Socialist Republic of Vietnam. A contest for a new anthem opened in 1981 came to nothing. It has two verses, but usually only the first is sung.

Its ownership has been disputed. In 2010 Văn Cao's widow, Nghiêm Thúy Băng, wrote to the Minister of Culture, Sports and Tourism proposing to give the work to the public domain, as Văn Cao had wished. In 2015, however, the family registered it with the Vietnam Center for Protection of Music Copyright to collect royalties on public performances, which angered veteran musicians such as Nguyễn Quang Long and the singer Ánh Tuyết; on 25 August 2015 the ministry told the agency to stop. On 8 July 2016 Văn Thao, the composer's eldest son, confirmed that the family would give the song to the nation for free use, and on 15 July 2016 the National Assembly Office received it at a ceremony in Hanoi and awarded Văn Cao the Ho Chi Minh Order. Disputes over particular recordings continued: in December 2021 the anthem was muted in a broadcast of a Laos–Vietnam football match, the government published a free recording, and in June 2022 the National Assembly amended the intellectual property law so that rights in the national anthem may not obstruct its use, from 1 January 2023.
== meaning
A marching song that calls the nation's soldiers to fight for their country's independence. Its mood is martial, solemn and resolute.
