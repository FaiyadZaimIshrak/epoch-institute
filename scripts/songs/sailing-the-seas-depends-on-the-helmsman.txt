== story
"Sailing the Seas Depends on the Helmsman", sometimes called "The Helmsman Sets the Ocean Course" in English, is a revolutionary song of the Chinese Communist Party. It was written in the spring of 1964 by two artists of the Harbin Opera House, the composer Wang Shuangyin and the lyricist Yu Wen (Li Yuwen), at a time of a nationwide campaign to study Mao Zedong's writings; its first title was "Making Revolution Depends on Mao Zedong Thought". Wang Shuangyin sang it at the second Harbin Summer music festival, where it caused a stir.

In June 1964 he sang it at an evening reception in Harbin for the North Korean leader Choe Yong-gon. Premier Zhou Enlai met him afterwards, praised the song and suggested syncopating a few notes, and the authors revised it accordingly. On the advice of Wang Jingzhi, deputy head of the arts department of Beijing Television (the forerunner of China Central Television), the song took its present title. Its melody closely resembles the first four lines of an earlier song, "I Dedicate Oil to My Motherland", composed by Qin Yongcheng with words by Xue Zhuguo; because of the charge of plagiarism, Wang Shuangyin apologised to Qin Yongcheng in person.

During the Cultural Revolution (1966–1976) the song was sung widely by the public, and especially by the Red Guards, in praise of Mao Zedong Thought and the party. Short and easy to sing, its single verse was usually repeated over and over. Its title phrase appeared on the cover of a junior middle school textbook on Mao Zedong Thought in the 1960s, and Lin Biao used the song's words in an inscription; according to Zhou Enlai, the title came from a slogan of Lin Biao. The song was also popular in the Third World.

Its success brought its composer office: Wang Shuangyin became a deputy to the National People's Congress, a member of the Heilongjiang provincial party committee, head of the province's model opera troupe and deputy director of its culture bureau, and was received by Jiang Qing. After the Gang of Four fell in 1976 he was detained for investigation, stripped of all his posts and expelled from the party, and questioned about his ties to Lin Biao and Jiang Qing; his membership was restored in 1987. On 24 October 1976, at a rally celebrating the fall of the Gang of Four, the new party chairman Hua Guofeng led the other leaders onto the Tiananmen rostrum to the song, which for a short while served to praise him and was used in newsreels and documentary films.

== notes
Status: copyright (1964 words and music; no death years given).
Lyricist: the English article says Yu Wen (郁文), the Chinese one Li Yuwen (李郁文); I gave both.
Lin Biao conflict: the English article says the title is based on a slogan of Lin Biao "according to Premier Zhou Enlai"; the Chinese article says Lin Biao borrowed the song's words for an inscription during the Cultural Revolution (and the song was written before, under another title). I gave both, the English one as Zhou's account; editor may prefer to keep only one.
"Third World": the Chinese article says only that it was "fairly popular" there, no details.
The song's original title is my translation of 干革命靠的是毛泽东思想; it is a title, not a lyric line, but it overlaps the second line Lin Biao wrote (幹革命靠毛澤東思想). Editor may prefer to drop it.
"Helmsman" in the meaning is the title's word.
kids: no, as a song of the personality cult around Mao and the Red Guards (guide rule).
