== story
"Ubi caritas" is a hymn of the Western Church whose text is attributed to Paulinus of Aquileia in 796 CE; its traditional melody probably also comes from the late 8th century. For a long time it was one of the antiphons sung at the washing of feet on Maundy Thursday, part of the Holy Thursday evening liturgy, and it is now and then sung at Eucharistic Adoration and at Benediction of the Blessed Sacrament. The Roman Missal of 1970 (third typical edition 2002) moved it from the foot-washing rite, the mandatum, to the offertory procession of the Holy Thursday evening Mass of the Lord's Supper. In the second typical edition of 1975 the response was altered, after certain very early manuscripts, to "Ubi caritas est vera, Deus ibi est": "Where true charity is, God is there". The hymn is also found in Anglican and Lutheran hymnals.

Modern composers have set it again and again. Maurice Duruflé used the Gregorian melody, with the refrain and the first stanza, as the first of his Quatre Motets sur des thèmes grégoriens. In 1960 an English translation, "Where Charity and Love Prevail", was copyrighted, to a tune Dom Paul Benoit adapted from the chant of Veni redemptor gentium. Jacques Berthier's Taizé chant of 1978 keeps only the refrain, with verses from 1 Corinthians 13. Later choral settings include those of David Conte, Stephen DeCesare, Ola Gjeilo and Paul Mealor, whose setting was sung at the wedding of Prince William and Catherine Middleton in 2011.
== meaning
The refrain declares that God is present where charity and love are; since 1975 the Roman Missal reads "Where true charity is, God is there". Sung for centuries while feet were washed on Maundy Thursday, it is a calm and earnest chant of love among the faithful.
