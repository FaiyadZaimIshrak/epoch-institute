== story
The "Agincourt Carol", also called the Agincourt Song or the Agincourt Hymn, is an English song written some time in the early 15th century. It recounts the Battle of Agincourt of 1415, in which the English army led by Henry V of England defeated the army of the French king Charles VI, in what is now the Pas-de-Calais region of France.

It survives in two sources of its own time. It is one of thirteen carols on the Trinity Carol Roll, which probably comes from East Anglia and has been held in the Wren Library of Trinity College, Cambridge, since the 19th century. The other is the Selden Carol Book in the Bodleian Library, Oxford.

Its form is that of the religious carols of the period: each verse is sung in English and followed by a refrain, the burden, in Latin.

In the 20th century it found new listeners. The composer Ernest Farrar based his Heroic Elegy: For Soldiers of 1918 on it, and Laurence Olivier used it in his film Henry V of 1944. It has been recorded by choirs and folk groups, among them The Young Tradition on the album Galleries, with the Early Music Consort and Dave Swarbrick; the Poxy Boggards on Bawdy Parts; and the Silly Sisters (Maddy Prior and June Tabor) on No More to the Dance.
== meaning
A song of thanksgiving for a victory in war. The English verses tell of the king's campaign and battle in France, and the Latin refrain gives the victory to God. The mood is proud and solemn, like a hymn.
