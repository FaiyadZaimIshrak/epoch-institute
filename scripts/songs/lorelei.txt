== story
The Lorelei is a steep slate rock, 132 metres high, on the right bank of the Rhine in the Rhine Gorge at Sankt Goarshausen. Its strong currents and loud echo made it a place of shipwrecks from the 10th century on, and its name is usually read as "murmuring rock". Old tales placed dwarfs in caves inside it.

The woman of the rock was the invention of a poet. In 1801 Clemens Brentano wrote the ballad "Zu Bacharach am Rheine", part of a fragmentary continuation of his novel Godwi. In it the beautiful Lore Lay, betrayed by her sweetheart and accused of bewitching men, is sent to a nunnery instead of being sentenced to death; on the way she climbs the rock to look at the Rhine once more and falls to her death, and the rock keeps the echo of her name. Brentano drew on Ovid and the myth of Echo. Joseph von Eichendorff also used the figure in his poem "Waldesgespräch" (1812), later set by Robert Schumann.

In 1824 Heinrich Heine took up Brentano's theme in "Die Lorelei", one of his most famous poems. His Lorelei is a kind of siren who sits on the cliff combing her golden hair and, without meaning to, distracts the boatmen with her beauty and song, so that they are wrecked on the rocks. In 1837 Friedrich Silcher set Heine's words to music as the art song "Lorelei", which became well known throughout the German-speaking lands. Franz Liszt's setting was also popular, and dozens of other musicians set the poem.

Through the Heine–Silcher song the figure passed into German popular culture, and it is often wrongly believed to come from an old folk tale. Under the Nazi regime and during the Second World War, Heine, who was born a Jew, was discredited as the author of the words, as part of an effort to hide the Jewish contribution to German art.

The theme was taken up again by Guillaume Apollinaire in "La Loreley", in his collection Alcools, which Dmitri Shostakovich used in the third movement of his Symphony No. 14, and it recurs in popular songs from Townes Van Zandt (1969) to The Pogues (1989).
== meaning
A ballad of the Rhine: a beautiful maiden sits on the high rock combing her golden hair and singing, and a boatman, lost in her beauty and her song, is wrecked on the rocks below. The mood is dreamy and melancholy rather than frightening.
