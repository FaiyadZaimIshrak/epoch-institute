== story
"Ar soudarded 'zo gwisket e ruz" ("The soldiers are dressed in red") is a Breton song written around 1880 by Max Ar Fur about a relative of his who died in the Franco-Prussian War. Its words are in the Gwenedeg (Vannetais) dialect of Breton. The song's soldier, who bears the family name Ar Fur, asks to be buried in the village of Brizeux, now part of Vannes, if he dies at war. The music is in a minor key and in 3/4 time.

The song became well known in the 1970s through the songwriter's grandson, the singer Gweltaz Ar Fur, who sang it in his Hennebont dialect; later it was also performed in standard Breton. Other artists took it up, among them Soazig Kermabon on her 1980 album Chant et harpe celtique, Gilles Servat and Zoltán Arany, and it was included in the 2017 compilation album Breizh eo ma bro!
== meaning
A lament for a Breton soldier of the Franco-Prussian War and his attachment to his home village. Its mood is sorrowful and quiet.
