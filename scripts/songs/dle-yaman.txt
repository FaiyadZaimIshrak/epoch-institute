== story
"Dle Yaman" is a traditional Armenian tune that tells the story of a tragic love affair between two people. It was discovered by Father Komitas during his travels in the Armenian countryside. Komitas re-arranged both its words and its music for the piano. In Western Armenian spelling the title is written Տըլէ Եաման, and in French it is often given as "Délé Yaman".

The song became one of the best-known Armenian songs, with hundreds of interpretations and covers. It is very often performed with solo passages on the duduk. Among the best-known versions are those of the soprano Lusine Zakaryan, of Levon Minassian, of Djivan Gasparyan on the duduk, of Flora Martirosian, and of Nune Yesayan with Jivan Gasparyan. Singers outside Armenia have taken it up as well, among them the Canadian Isabel Bayrakdarian, the French singers Hélène Segara and Patrick Fiori, and the Russian singer Zara.

Over time the song came to be sung as one of the hymns of the Armenian genocide, which is commemorated every year on 24 April. The French-Armenian director Henri Verneuil made it the main music of his semi-autobiographical film Mayrig (1991).
== meaning
A folk song of love that ends in sorrow, told as the story of two people whose love goes wrong. Its slow, grieving mood is why it later came to be sung in remembrance of the Armenian genocide.
