== story
"Steal Away", or "Steal Away to Jesus", is an American spiritual, best known through the variations of its chorus. It was composed some time before 1862 by Wallace Willis and his daughter Minerva Willis, who were enslaved in the old Indian Territory by a Choctaw owner. Alexander Reid, a minister at a Choctaw boarding school, heard Willis singing his songs and wrote down their words and melodies. He sent them to the Jubilee Singers of Fisk University in Nashville, Tennessee, who made them widely known on their tours of the United States and Europe.

"Steal Away" is counted, with "Swing Low, Sweet Chariot", "Wade in the Water" and "The Gospel Train", among the spirituals said to carry hidden codes: besides their faith in God, messages telling enslaved people to run away, on their own or by the Underground Railroad. It became a standard gospel song and is in the hymnals of many Protestant churches. Michael Tippett set it in his oratorio A Child of Our Time, first performed in 1944; Pat Boone and Nat King Cole are among the many who recorded it, and in the Slovene film Valley of Peace (1956) it is sung by Jim, an American airman played by John Kitzmiller.
== meaning
A call to steal away home to Jesus, because the singer has not long to stay here. On its face a song of faith and of longing for heaven, it has also been heard as a hidden call to slip away from slavery. The mood is quiet and urgent.
