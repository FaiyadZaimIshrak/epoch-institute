== story
Maria Konopnicka, a poet and activist for Polish independence, wrote "Rota" ("The Oath") in 1908 while staying in Cieszyn, as a protest against the German Empire's policy of forced Germanisation of the Poles in the lands Prussia had annexed in the partitions of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth. Under Prussian and then German rule there, political leaders such as Otto von Bismarck and Eugen von Puttkammer had pressed for the "uprooting" of the Poles, and the poem was written as a reply. It was first published in the newspaper Gwiazdka Cieszyńska on 7 November 1908.

Feliks Nowowiejski, a composer, conductor and organist, set it to music two years later. It was first sung in public in Kraków on 15 July 1910, at a patriotic demonstration marking the 500th anniversary of the Polish-Lithuanian victory over the Teutonic Knights at Grunwald, and it quickly became popular across partitioned Poland. Until 1918 it was the anthem of the Polish Scouting movement. From 1920 to 1922 it was the state anthem of the Republic of Central Lithuania, a puppet state wholly dependent on interwar Poland, and in Poland itself it was among the songs considered for the national anthem, favoured especially by the political right; "Poland Is Not Yet Lost" was chosen instead.

During the German occupation in the Second World War, on the eve of Independence Day, 11 November 1939, scouts in Zielonka near Warsaw put up posters bearing the poem; in reprisal the occupiers executed nine scouts and other inhabitants of the town. The communist authorities after the war also kept the song, making it the anthem of the 1st Tadeusz Kościuszko Infantry Division. After 1989 it became the official anthem of the Polish People's Party and later of the League of Polish Families. Its melody was played by the Gdańsk carillon until 2003 and served as the signature theme of two regional television stations; in 2010 the Sejm honoured the song and its author by a special resolution, and since May 2024 an autograph score of 1910 has been on permanent display in the Palace of the Commonwealth in Warsaw.
== meaning
A solemn oath of loyalty to Poland and its culture, sworn in defiance of forced Germanisation. Its mood is grave and resolute.
