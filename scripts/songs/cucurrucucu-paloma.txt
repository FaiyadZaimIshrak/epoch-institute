== story
"Cucurrucucú paloma" ("Coo-coo dove") is a Mexican song in the huapango style, written by Tomás Méndez in 1954. Its title imitates the call of the mourning dove, which its refrain echoes. It was first heard in the Mexican comedy film Escuela de vagabundos (1955), sung by the film's star, Pedro Infante, and it gave its name to Miguel Delgado's film Cucurrucucú Paloma (1965), in which Lola Beltrán sang it in the role of "Paloma Méndez". Its first release on record was a version sung by Harry Belafonte in 1956.

The song has since travelled far beyond Mexico. It has been recorded by singers from Luis Miguel, Rocío Dúrcal, Miguel Aceves Mejía and Lila Downs to Perry Como, Nana Mouskouri, Julio Iglesias, Hibari Misora and Joan Baez, and Franco Battiato took up its refrain in his own song "Cuccurucucù". It is heard in many films, among them The Last Sunset (1961), Happy Together (1997) and Moonlight (2016); in Pedro Almodóvar's Talk to Her (2002) the Brazilian singer Caetano Veloso sings it as an art song, far from the mariachi style of its first appearance on screen. In 2019 the tenor Juan Diego Flórez sang it at La Scala in Milan, accompanying himself on the guitar, in a performance watched 17 million times on YouTube.
== meaning
A song of lovesickness, sorrowful and yearning, built around the mourning dove's call.
