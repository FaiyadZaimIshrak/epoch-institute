== story
The poet Ferenc Kölcsey completed the poem on 22 January 1823. The title of his manuscript was "Hymnus", the Latin word for a hymn, and it bore the subtitle "From Stormy Centuries of the Hungarian Folk". It is often argued that the subtitle, by placing the nation's troubles in the past rather than the present, was added so that the poem would pass the Habsburg censors. The poem was first published in 1829 in Károly Kisfaludy's Aurora, without the subtitle, and appeared with it in a collection of Kölcsey's works in 1832. Its full meaning is evident only to those who know Hungarian history well.

In 1844 a competition was held for a musical setting that the public could sing, and Ferenc Erkel won it. His version was first performed in July 1844 at the National Theatre, where he was conductor, and then before a larger audience on 10 August 1844, at the first voyage of the steamship Széchenyi. By the end of the 1850s it had become customary to sing it on special occasions, on its own or together with Vörösmarty's "Szózat". From 1844 it served in practice as the hymn of the Kingdom of Hungary, and over time the Hungarian word himnusz, taken from the Latin, came to mean a national anthem.

In the early 1900s members of the Hungarian parliament proposed making its status as the national anthem of Hungary within Austria-Hungary official, but no such law was passed. In the 1950s the communist leader Mátyás Rákosi planned to replace it with an anthem better suited to communist ideology, but the poet Gyula Illyés and the composer Zoltán Kodály, whom he had in mind for the task, both refused. Only in 1989 did Erkel's setting gain official recognition, when the Constitution of Hungary named it as the national anthem of the Third Hungarian Republic.

Only the first stanza is sung at official ceremonies. By tradition "Himnusz" opens a ceremony and "Szózat", often called a second national anthem, closes it. Kossuth Rádió plays it at the close of its AM transmissions each night, the state television channels at the end of the day's broadcasts, and Hungarian television at midnight on New Year's Eve. On 7 May 2006 a sculpture by Mária V. Majzik was inaugurated in Budakeszi, near Budapest: the full text of the poem in a circle around a bronze figure of God, with 21 bells on which the melody can be played, paid for by public subscription.
== meaning
A prayer to God for the Hungarian people. It looks back over the nation's misfortunes across the centuries and asks God's blessing on them; the mood is solemn and grave, and the melody resembles a Protestant chorale.
