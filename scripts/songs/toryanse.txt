== story
"Tōryanse" is a traditional Japanese children's song (warabe uta). There are many theories about its origin, but all agree that it portrays an exchange between an ordinary person and a guard who keeps a checkpoint; one theory places the checkpoint at Kawagoe Castle. In earlier times, when many infants died, people celebrated when a child survived to the age of seven (and also three and five, the ages kept in the festival of Shichi-Go-San), and ordinary people were allowed to visit the shrine inside the castle grounds only on such special occasions.

The song belongs to a traditional game. Two children face each other and join hands to form an arch, the "checkpoint", and the others walk underneath in a line and round again in circles. The child who is under the arch when the song ends is caught, much as in the English-language game "London Bridge Is Falling Down".

The tune is now a common choice for the music played by traffic lights in Japan when it is safe to cross, a reminder of the game: one may cross until the music stops. It is also heard in many forms of popular culture, among them crossing scenes in anime.
== meaning
A dialogue between a traveller and the guard of a checkpoint on the way to a shrine, sung as children pass one by one under an arch of hands, not knowing who will be caught when it ends.
