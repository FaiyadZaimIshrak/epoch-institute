== story
"Jambo Bwana" (Swahili for "Hello, sir") is a Kenyan pop song, also popular in Tanzania, first released in 1982 by the Kenyan band Them Mushrooms. Its words are built from common Swahili greetings and phrases, and the original version also celebrated the Swahili language, reggae music and Africa. It sold more than 200,000 copies between 1982 and 1987 and was certified platinum in Kenya, and the band released it on several of their albums.

Its success led many other bands to cover it, among them Mombasa Roots, Safari Sound Band, Khadja Nin, Adam Solomon and, in 2016, the Kenyan musician Mani Kollengode. Some versions carry other titles, such as "Jambo Jambo" and "Hakuna Matata", and changed words. Safari Sound Band's "Jambo Jambo", which replaced the middle of the song with other Swahili phrases for tourists, became one of the most played songs in tourist venues in East Africa, and the song as a whole became a hotel pop song for visitors.

In 1983 the German group Boney M. released "Jambo – Hakuna Matata", with lead vocals by Liz Mitchell and backing by Reggie Tsiboe, Frank Farian, Marcia Barrett and others. Its words are in English and almost entirely different, keeping only two lines of the original. It was meant for a seventh album planned for the autumn of 1983 that never appeared, and it came out in mixes of different lengths in different countries.

Disney's The Lion King took up a Swahili phrase from the song's chorus as a reference to it. Versions of the song are on many compilations of African pop music, including collections for parties and for children such as Putumayo Kids' African Playground, and it was heard in Richard Linklater's 2019 film Where'd You Go, Bernadette, starring Cate Blanchett.
== meaning
A light, welcoming song of greetings in Swahili; cheerful and easy-going.
