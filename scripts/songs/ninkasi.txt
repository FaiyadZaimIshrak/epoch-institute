== story
Ninkasi was the Mesopotamian goddess of beer and brewing, worshipped in many parts of Mesopotamia from the Early Dynastic period on. A Sumerian hymn dedicated to her, known simply as the Hymn to Ninkasi, survives in three copies: one from Nippur and two from unknown places. Because the hymn names no historical events and literary texts were written in a purposely archaic style, its composition cannot be dated precisely, but the known tablets are agreed to come from the Old Babylonian period.

The hymn praises the goddess and gives her a family history found nowhere else: although her parents were Enki and Ninti, the hymn says she was raised by Ninhursag, a goddess not otherwise linked with raising children. It also names a city of Ninkasi; scholars read this not as an unknown cult centre but as a poetic way of saying that any city where beer was drunk was hers.

The hymn is usually taken as a poetic description of brewing. Miguel Civil published a translation in 1964, revised in 1991, and it is now commonly quoted in professional literature and in modern writing on the history of beer. Read in that translation, the main ingredient is bappir, presumably a dry bread similar to biscotti. More recent work by the archaeologist Adelheid Otto, the brewing technician Martin Zarnkow and the Assyriologist Walther Sallaberger finds that the usual translation does not fully match what is known of Mesopotamian brewing, and calls for parts of the hymn to be translated again. Sallaberger argues that bappir most likely meant sourdough. The hymn's honey is probably a literary flourish to stress the quality of the goddess's beer: honey was an expensive luxury, and administrative texts list only barley and sometimes emmer as brewing ingredients.

== meaning
A hymn of praise to the beer goddess that celebrates her and the beer she makes, describing the steps of brewing as her work. Scholars still debate how literally its account of ingredients and methods should be read.
