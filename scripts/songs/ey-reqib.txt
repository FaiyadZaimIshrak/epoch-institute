== story
"Ey Reqîb" was written in 1938 in Sorani (Central Kurdish) by the Kurdish poet and political activist Dildar, while he was in jail. Its title means "O Enemy!". It addresses the jail guards of the prison where Dildar was held and tortured, and beyond them the broader oppression of the Kurds.

In 1946 the poem was adopted as the national anthem of the Kurdistan Republic of Mahabad, a short-lived Kurdish republic in Iran that lasted for a year. It is known as the Kurdish national anthem and is the official anthem of the semi-autonomous Kurdistan Region of Iraq. It is also used by the Kurdistan Workers' Party. Besides the official Sorani text, it is sung in other Kurdish dialects, among them Northern Kurdish (Kurmanci), Southern Kurdish and Zazaki.
== meaning
An address to an enemy: the guards who held the poet in jail and, beyond them, the oppression of the Kurdish people. The mood is defiant.
