== story
"Cielito Lindo" (roughly "Lovely Sweet One") is a Mexican folk song, or copla, popularized in 1882 by the Mexican author Quirino Mendoza y Cortés (c. 1862–1957). It is widely known by words from its refrain, "Canta y no llores", or as the "Ay, Ay, Ay, Ay song". Its verses follow the seguidilla, a classical Castilian stanza of seven lines of alternating seven and five syllables, and singers add and drop verses freely.

Its first verse is older than the song. In an essay, "¡Hasta que me cayó el veinte!", the researcher Ortega found that in the early 17th century armed bandits sheltered in Spain's Sierra Morena mountains, and travellers crossing them feared for their lives; the words of the first verse appear in a song of that time, reflecting that fear. Over time the verse was romanticized, set to other melodies and varied, until Mendoza set it to his own tune. Some read its "Sierra Morena" as the mountains of southern Spain, others as the Sierra de Morones in Zacatecas, and many Mexicans hear it as "la sierra, morena", with "morena" a term of endearment for the woman addressed.

It became one of the best-known songs of Mexico, played above all by mariachi bands. A Mexican orchestra recorded it for Victor in 1926, and Carl Sandburg printed it among the "Mexican Border Songs" of The American Songbag in 1927. Pedro Infante sang it in the 1947 film Los tres García, Carmen Costa recorded a Portuguese version in Brazil in 1942, and Trini Lopez a widely known Spanish version in 1963; José Feliciano's recording of 1989 won a Grammy Award, and the Three Tenors sang it in many concerts. In Nazi-occupied Poland its tune carried the street chant "Teraz jest wojna" ("Now there is war"), sung by street musicians and the resistance. Not every use was welcome: a Fritos advertisement in which the Frito Bandito character sang new words to it was taken by many Mexicans as a racist insult.

Today it is sung especially by Mexicans living abroad, and by Mexicans at international events such as the Olympic Games and the FIFA World Cup. It should not be confused with "Cielito lindo huasteco", a different son huasteco from La Huasteca that uses the same fillers; mariachis sometimes combine the two.
== meaning
A tender love song addressed to a dark-eyed woman, in which the singer praises her beauty. Its refrain turns to the listener with cheerful comfort, urging song over sorrow, which is why it is sung at partings and gatherings far from home.
