== story
Bogurodzica, "Mother of God" (a calque of the Greek Theotokos), is a medieval Christian hymn composed in Poland sometime between the 10th and 13th centuries. It is believed to be the oldest religious hymn, or patriotic anthem, in the Polish language, and it was sung in Old Polish together with the Greek phrase Kyrie eleison, "Lord, have mercy". Its origin is not clear: several scholars name Saint Adalbert of Prague as the likely author or a contributor, while the two opening stanzas may date from the middle or end of the 13th century or the very beginning of the 14th. The hymn draws heavily on Latin Christian liturgy.

It was first written down at the beginning of the 15th century. Two records survive from that time: the Kcynia record, with the first two stanzas and musical notation, and the Kraków record, with thirteen stanzas but no notes. Later copies date from the second half of the 15th century and the early 16th, and in 1509 the hymn was printed in Kraków in the Statutes of Bishop Jan Łaski. Since May 2024 the third-oldest manuscript version, from 1456, has been on permanent display in the Palace of the Commonwealth in Warsaw.

At first the hymn belonged to the Mass and to processions, but by the 15th century it had become a war hymn and battle cry. According to the historian Jan Długosz, Polish knights sang it at the Battle of Grunwald in 1410 and before other notable battles in the years that followed; it also accompanied the coronation of Władysław III of Poland and of the first Jagiellonian kings. Długosz called it carmen patrium, "the hymn of the fatherland".

Over the following centuries it gave way to newer patriotic songs, notably "Rota" and "Poland Is Not Yet Lost". Modern composers have quoted it, among them Andrzej Panufnik in his Sinfonia sacra (1963), Wojciech Kilar in a Bogurodzica for choir and orchestra (1975), Krzysztof Meyer in his Symphony No. 6 "Polish" (1982) and Henryk Górecki, who used its opening as a motto in several works.
== meaning
A prayer hymn. The first stanza calls on the Virgin Mary, the chosen mother of Christ, to win her Son's favour for the people; the second turns to Christ himself, recalling his baptism, and asks for a good life on earth and life in heaven after death. Later stanzas take up Easter, the Passion and a litany of the saints.
