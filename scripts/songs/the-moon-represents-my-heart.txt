== story
The music of "The Moon Represents My Heart" is by the Taiwanese composer Weng Ching-hsi. While studying at the Berklee College of Music in Boston, he often found ideas for his music in the nearby parks and wrote them down. Back in Taiwan he showed one of his compositions to his friend the lyricist Sun Yi. Weng thought little of this piece and had even meant to throw it away; Sun found it in a pile of works set aside for disposal, judged it a good song and wrote words for it. In 1972 the two sold the song to Li Ge Records. It was first recorded by the Taiwanese singer Chen Fen-lan for her album Dreamland in May 1973, and again by Liu Guan-lin in November of the same year. Sun Yi later sued Li Ge Records and lost, so the company owns the song's copyright.

The song became famous through Teresa Teng, who recorded it for her Mandarin album Love Songs of the Island, Vol. 4: Love in Hong Kong, released by Polydor Hong Kong on 19 December 1977. Her version has been described as a love song with a waltz-like lilt; the anthropologist Kevin Latham called it a fairly simple love ballad, like most gangtai songs of the time. The linguist Adrian Tien has noted that in Chinese culture the moon has long expressed sorrow over lost love or the hope of reunion, and cites the song as a leading modern example of that image.

For several decades foreign music had not been allowed into mainland China. After the Cultural Revolution and the Open Door Policy of 1978, this was one of the first popular songs from Hong Kong and Taiwan to spread there. Romantic songs had been almost absent for years, condemned as bourgeois and decadent, and Teng's soft, restrained singing stood in sharp contrast to the officially approved revolutionary songs. Shelley Rigger writes that the leaders of the People's Republic tried to suppress her music while it spread; by the end of the 1970s it was said that Deng Xiaoping ruled by day and Teresa Teng by night. Her popularity opened the way for much more Taiwanese music, which was called a "counter invasion", and Lauren Gorfinkel describes Teng as becoming a symbol of unity in Greater China and of the country's opening and reforms. The film director Jia Zhangke later recalled that the song brought his generation a personal, individual world after a time when everything had been collective.

The song has been sung around the world by singers such as Hayley Westenra, Nana Mouskouri, Faye Wong, Andy Lau, Leslie Cheung, Siti Nurhaliza and the saxophonist Kenny G, and according to NetEase almost every Hong Kong singer has covered it. It took first place in the Hong Kong Golden Songs Awards' list of the Top 10 Chinese Golden Songs of the 20th Century, and at the 2010 Chinese Music Awards critics ranked it first among the classic Chinese songs of the previous 30 years. It remains a karaoke favourite. In 2019 the Museum of Chinese in America in New York City named an exhibition on music and Chinese immigrant communities after it.
== meaning
A tender love song in which the moon stands for a lover's constant feeling. Its mood is soft, intimate and calm.
