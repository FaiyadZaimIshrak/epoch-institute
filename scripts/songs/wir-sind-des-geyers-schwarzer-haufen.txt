== story
"Wir sind des Geyers schwarzer Haufen" ("We Are Geyer's Black Band") is a German marching song of the years between the world wars. Its words come from the poem "Ich bin der arme Konrad", written in 1888 by the Bavarian poet and artillery officer Heinrich von Reder, and its melody was arranged by the songwriter Fritz Sotke in 1919.

The song looks back to the German Peasants' War of 1525. It is named after Florian Geyer (1490 – 10 June 1525) and his Black Company, a heavy cavalry unit that fought on the peasants' side. In its own time the company was notorious for destroying cathedrals and castles and for summary executions of clergy and noblemen, and the song draws on that reputation; its words are noted for their strong hostility to the clergy and the nobility.

Composed within the German youth movements of the Weimar Republic, the song was sung by groups across the whole political spectrum. It was later included in the official songbook of the National People's Army, the armed forces of the German Democratic Republic. It remains popular with German music groups today, often with its words softened or with lines left out, depending on the arrangement and performer. A line of its last stanza is inscribed on a memorial at Osterhausen near Eisleben in Saxony-Anhalt that commemorates both the Peasants' War of 1525 and the land reform of 1945, which the memorial's designers saw as fulfilling the peasants' cause generations later.
== meaning
A marching song in the voice of a peasant army of 1525, defiant and violent towards church and nobility, which looks ahead to later generations finishing their fight.
