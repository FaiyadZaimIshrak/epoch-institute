== story
From 1948 the National Party governed South Africa, and the white minority held all political power under the system of apartheid. Savuka, formed in 1985 by many members of the band Juluka, was a mixed band of three black and three white South Africans led by Johnny Clegg; its name means "awakening" in Zulu, and its music drew on Zulu traditions, Celtic music and rock. "Asimbonanga" was written while Nelson Mandela, imprisoned since 1962, was still held on Robben Island. Its title is a Zulu phrase that may be translated "We have not seen him", and the song alludes to Mandela's absence from society and names other activists against apartheid, among them Steve Biko, Victoria Mxenge and Neil Aggett. It has choruses in Zulu and verses in English, and belongs to a trend in South African music after the Soweto uprising of 1976 that set politically conscious words to jive and dance rhythms, called "township jive" or "bubblegum".

It was first released as a 12-inch single in 1986 and became the second track of Savuka's album Third World Child in 1987, with Clegg credited as composer. It reached No. 2 on the French singles chart, became one of the most popular songs against apartheid and was adopted as an anthem by the United Democratic Front. The scholar David Coplan called it a "haunting tribute". Its open dedication to Mandela, Biko and others brought the band trouble with the police: their concerts were raided and they were arrested repeatedly, and the song was among several of Clegg's that were banned in South Africa. Clegg's interest in Zulu traditional music and his work with the Zulu musician Sipho Mchunu had already brought him trouble with the apartheid government.

Joan Baez recorded it on her album Recently, with backing vocals drawn from South African music and arranged by Caiphus Semenya; it earned her a Grammy nomination. At a concert by Clegg in 1999, Mandela joined the band on stage and danced to the song, then said that music and dancing made him at peace with the world and asked them to play it again. Clegg, Baez and the Soweto Gospel Choir sang it together at the Nelson Mandela 90th Birthday Tribute concert in London in 2008, and the choir performed it as a flash mob in 2013.
== meaning
A tribute to Nelson Mandela and other activists of the struggle against apartheid, mourning their absence while he was imprisoned. Its mood is elegiac and longing, carried on a dance rhythm.
