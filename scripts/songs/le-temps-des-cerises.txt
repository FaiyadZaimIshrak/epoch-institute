== story
"Le Temps des cerises" ("The Time of Cherries") is a French song written in 1866, with words by Jean-Baptiste Clément and music by Antoine Renard. It became very well known throughout the French-speaking countries.

The song came to be closely linked with the Paris Commune. Verses were added to it during the Commune, and it was sung from then on as a revolutionary song: the "time of cherries" was read as an image of life as it would be once a revolution had changed social and economic conditions. Clément is believed to have dedicated it to a nurse who fought in the Bloody Week (semaine sanglante), when French government troops overthrew the Commune.

Many versions of the words exist. The one most often heard today is the version popularised by the singer Yves Montand; Marc Ogeret, Juliette Gréco, Cora Vaucaire and Charles Trenet are among the others who recorded it, and Bobbejaan Schoepen sang it with Geike Arnaert of Hooverphonic.

The song has remained a symbol of the left. The Communist Party of Bohemia and Moravia took two cherries into its logo because of it, the French Communist Party adopted a new logo inspired by it in 2018, and it served as a symbol of The Left, a political party in Luxembourg. The town hall clock of Saint-Denis, a suburb of Paris, plays it for its hourly chime, alternating with "Le roi Dagobert a mis sa culotte à l'envers". In the film Porco Rosso the character Madame Gina sings it in a cabaret.
== meaning
On its face a love song: the brief, joyful season of cherries, when lovers go out together, is set against the lasting ache of love lost, which the singer says he will carry always. The mood is tender and wistful; after the Commune, the season of cherries was heard as a hope for a better time to come.
