== story
"Pata Pata" means "touch touch" in Xhosa, the language in which the song was first written and sung. It was also the name of a dance popular in the shebeens of Johannesburg's townships in the mid-1950s, in which partners patted each other in time to the music. Makeba's song was not the only one the dance inspired. Its melody was based on an instrumental, "Phatha Phatha", by Shumi Ntutu and Isaac Nkosi, itself based on "Noma Kumnyama" by Alson Mkhize. Dorothy Masuka's popular "Ei Yow Phata Phata" of 1956 was a distinctly different song, though Masuka later recorded Makeba's version and claimed to have written it.

Makeba first recorded "Pata Pata" in South Africa with her girl group the Skylarks, in either 1956 or 1959. In 1967, after building a successful career in the United States, she recorded it again with Jerry Ragovoy producing, adding a spoken part in English, and Ragovoy was then billed as co-writer of words and music. Released on her album of the same name and as a single, with "Malayisha" on the other side, it reached No. 12 on the Billboard chart on 25 November 1967, and it is considered by many to be her signature hit.

Makeba returned to the song throughout her life: a duet with Chayanne in 1988, a new recording for her album Welela in 1990, and "Pata Pata 2000" on her album Homeland. On the night she died, she performed "Pata Pata" just before she collapsed on stage. The song has been recorded by many other artists, from the Supremes, Tito Puente and Manu Dibango to Thalía, Angélique Kidjo and Pink Martini, and has been used in films, television series and advertisements.
== meaning
A dance song about a township dance; its mood is joyful and inviting.
