== story
The Cantigas de Santa Maria ("Canticles of Holy Mary") are 420 poems with musical notation, written in medieval Galician-Portuguese at the court of Alfonso X of Castile, called El Sabio, "the Wise" (1221-1284). Tradition attributes them all to Alfonso, but scholars have established that the poets and musicians of his court wrote most of them, with the king credited with a few: he names himself in the first person, and the collection's prologue supports his part. Several studies suggest that the Galician poet Airas Nunes may have written a large number. It is one of the largest collections of monophonic (solo) songs to survive from the Middle Ages.

Alfonso's workshops wrote their histories and other prose in Castilian, but the Cantigas are in Galician-Portuguese, which was then fashionable as a language of lyric poetry in Castile; Alfonso had also spent part of his early years in Galicia and probably spoke it from childhood. The collection reflects the popularity at his court of other Galician-Portuguese poetry, the cantigas d'amigo and cantigas d'amor.

Every song mentions the Virgin Mary. Of the 420, 356 are narratives of Marian miracles; the others, apart from an introduction and two prologues, are songs of praise or for Marian feasts, and every tenth song is a hymn. The miracle songs show Mary in a very human way, often taking part in earthly episodes, and many speak of devotion to her in the language of the troubadours' courtly love. The verse forms vary widely, with 280 different formats for 420 songs, most often the virelai and the rondeau, with lines of two to 24 syllables. The music is written in a notation like that of chant but with some indication of note lengths; its rhythm remains a lively subject of debate.

The songs survive in four codices, thought to have been made in Alfonso's lifetime: the Toledo codex (To, now in the National Library in Madrid), the earliest, with 129 songs, perhaps from the 1270s; the "codex of the musicians" (E, El Escorial), the fullest, with 406 songs and miniatures of pairs of musicians playing a wide variety of instruments; the richly illustrated Códice Rico (T, El Escorial), with 195 surviving songs, each shown in six or twelve narrative pictures; and its unfinished sister volume in Florence (F), with 111, whose staves were never filled with music. T, F and E date from the early 1280s up to Alfonso's death in 1284.

The Cantigas are often performed and recorded by early-music groups, among them Eduardo Paniagua and Música Antigua, Federico Bardazzi and Ensemble San Felice, Esther Lamandier and Jordi Savall. Because the original notation is ambiguous, their recordings differ greatly from one another.
== meaning
The collection is a royal book of devotion to the Virgin Mary. Most of its songs tell short stories of miracles in which Mary helps people in everyday troubles, while the rest praise her or celebrate her feasts. They speak of loving her in the manner of a courtly lover serving a lady, and their mood ranges from wonder to joyful praise.
