== story
"Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Star" is an English lullaby. Its words are a poem, "The Star", written by Jane Taylor (1783–1824) and published in London in 1806 in Rhymes for the Nursery, a collection of poems by Jane and her sister Ann Taylor (1782–1866). The poem is written in couplets and has five stanzas, though only the first is widely known. Colchester and Chipping Ongar have both claimed to be where Jane wrote it, but Ann Taylor recalled in her autobiography that Jane first saw the name of Ongar, on a signpost in Epping Forest, in the summer of 1810, four years after the poem was published.

The words were first printed with the French melody "Ah! vous dirai-je, maman" in The Singing Master: First Class Tune Book in 1838, and it is to this tune, first published in 1761 and later arranged by several composers, among them Mozart in his Twelve Variations on "Ah vous dirai-je, Maman", that the song is sung today; when it is sung, the first two lines of the poem return as a refrain after each stanza. The same melody carries the ABC Song and "Baa, Baa, Black Sheep". The poem has had many versions and parodies, among them one in Frederick Marryat's novel Poor Jack (1840), another in Mildred J. Hill's Song Stories for the Kindergarten (1896) and "Twinkle, Twinkle, Little Bat", recited by the Mad Hatter in Lewis Carroll's Alice's Adventures in Wonderland. Its opening words also begin the murder ballad "Duncan and Brady", and the song can be played as a singing game.
== meaning
A child looks up at a star shining in the night sky and wonders what it is. The mood is quiet and full of wonder, a song for bedtime.
