== story
"Ey Iran" was made during the Second World War. In September 1941 Allied forces occupied Iran after the Anglo-Soviet invasion that overthrew Reza Shah. The poet Hossein Gol-e-Golab was moved to write a poem on Iranian nationalism when he saw Allied flags flying over an Iranian military barracks in Tehran; by his own account, a confrontation between a British soldier and an Iranian officer encouraged him further. He later recalled that the presence of the occupying armies in the streets in 1944 inspired the piece, and that it reached the people despite political opposition. Ruhollah Khaleghi set the poem to music, and Gholam-Hossein Banan sang it.

It was first performed over two consecutive nights from 27 October 1944 at the military primary school on Istanbul Street in Tehran, with Banan as soloist. The audience called for several encores. The Minister of Culture then commissioned a studio recording, which Radio Tehran broadcast daily.

In the transitional period immediately after the 1979 Iranian Revolution, the song briefly served as the country's de facto national anthem under the interim government. After the Islamic takeover in 1979, several people involved in its making were imprisoned, and public performance of the song was discouraged. Its melody nevertheless continued to circulate and served as a morale-boosting symbol during the Iran–Iraq War.

In 1990 Golnoush Khaleghi, the composer's daughter, arranged a new version in Tehran for the 25th anniversary of her father's death; it appeared on the album "May Nab", issued by Sorush Publications, with a monologue by Rashid Vatandoust. Since 1979 the song has been widely sung at marches and demonstrations of the Iranian diaspora against the Islamic Republic, and as a symbol of Iran's pre-1979 identity.
== meaning
A patriotic song about Iran, written while the country was under foreign occupation. Its mood is proud and solemn.
