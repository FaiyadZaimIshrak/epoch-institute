== story
"Maréchal, nous voilà !" ("Marshal, here we are!") is a French propaganda song of 1941 in honour of Marshal Philippe Pétain, head of the Vichy regime, an authoritarian and antisemitic state set up during the German occupation. According to Guy Guenoun, son of the Jewish founder of "Mon Bar" in Avignon, the songwriter and poet André Montagard wrote the words at a table there in 1941 to enter a competition the Vichy regime had organised to give Pétain an official song; Guenoun recalled that Montagard, often short of money, was moved more by the promised prize than by belief in the regime. It was published by the Ver Luisant music house, run by Rolf Marbot (born Albrecht Marcuse), which issued other songs in praise of Pétain; Charles Courtioux, the firm's printer of sheet music, signed the music with Montagard.

The melody was plagiarised from a tune written by the Polish Jewish composer Kazimierz (Casimir) Oberfeld for the 1933 film operetta "La Margoton du bataillon". When the song was declared to SACEM, the French authors' society noted its "obvious kinship" with Oberfeld's music and warned Courtioux of the "strange similarity". Under Vichy's antisemitic laws Oberfeld, who had been one of SACEM's members entitled to royalties before the occupation, could no longer register works or receive his royalties because he was a Jew. He was deported to Auschwitz in 1943 and died on a death march in 1945. The same tune had already been borrowed for Frédo Gardoni's "La Fleur au guidon", written for the 1937 Tour de France.

La Marseillaise remained the main anthem of France, though not formally named the national anthem; in the southern zone it was often followed by "Maréchal, nous voilà !", which became the regime's unofficial anthem. André Dassary recorded it for Pathé in 1941, with Marcel Cariven's orchestra, and Andrex also sang it. Radio-Paris and the Radio nationale broadcast it regularly, and it was played across France and the empire, in most schools, in the youth work camps (chantiers de jeunesse), in barracks and at meetings of the Milice. Its refrain shows the cult of personality built around Pétain from 1940.

As a pillar of Vichy propaganda, the song drew a pamphlet against Pétain and parodies from the Resistance, among them "Général, nous voilà !" in honour of General de Gaulle and Julien Clément's "Maréchal, les voilà !". It has since been used in film soundtracks to set the period, among them The Chorus (2004), La Rafle (2010), the two War of the Buttons films of 2011 and Fanny's Journey (2016), and the newspaper Libération echoed it in its headline on the death of Jean-Marie Le Pen on 8 January 2025.
== meaning
A propaganda song of loyalty to Marshal Pétain, part of the personality cult of the Vichy regime; rousing and devotional.
