# Batch 3 notes (wave A10b)

Wikimedia answered 429 (too many requests) for long stretches, so find-sources.js could not be used (it also needs the
draft in scripts/songs/, where I may not write). I looked the pages up by hand: Wikidata sitelinks, Wikisource search
and prefix search, then `fetch-texts.js --peek`. Videos: yt-search.js, then each candidate's watch page (channel,
category, length, description, playableInEmbed: all nine are embeddable). check-videos.js / audit-videos.js read the
repository's videos.tsv, so the editor should run them after merging.

## tevaram
- Status: pd (7th-8th century hymns).
- Words: the Tevaram is 796 hymns, so the rows take one: Sambandar's first patikam, "Todudaiya Seviyan", which opens the
  first Tirumurai, from ta: "திருஞானசம்பந்தர்- 'தோடுடைய செவியன்'". That page prints every line as its own block, and
  each verse twice (as written, then with the words separated), with a "பாடல்: NN" heading and an edit link between
  verses. So 11 rows, one per verse, each the four lines as written: #13..#16 /4, #23..#26 /4 … #113..#116 /4 (verse k
  at 13+10(k-1)); the closing "திருச்சிற்றம்பலம்" (#121) is left out. The separated forms (#17..#20 etc.) could serve
  as a reading aid, but they are not a romanization. No pre-1931 English translation found; no rows for en or rom.
- Video: P2N8QSbLQuY, Sounds of Isha (the Isha Foundation's ensemble; album "Thevaram: Outpourings of Tamil Devotion"),
  Thodudaiya Seviyan, the same hymn as the words, 8:51. Second choice: 6UdsKOMXdKc, RagamalikaTV, odhuvars of the
  Dharumai Adheenam singing in the odhuvar manner (2:39, which hymn is not stated). Sargurunatha Odhuvar's concerts on
  Ammaiappar Tv are 58 min and longer.
- Doubts: Sounds of Isha is a modern arrangement rather than temple odhuvar singing; the editor may prefer the
  odhuvars. year 650 is my reading.

## charyapada
- Status: pd (10th-12th century songs; Shastri's edition 1916).
- Words: one song, pada 1 by Luipa ("কাআ তরুবর পঞ্চ বি ডাল"), from Shastri's 1916 edition on Bengali Wikisource:
  "হাজার বছরের পুরাণ বাঙ্গালা ভাষায় বৌদ্ধগান ও দোহা (১৯১৬)/চর্য্যাচর্য্য-বিনিশ্চয়/১", stanza #8 (10 lines); the page then
  carries Munidatta's Sanskrit commentary, which the select leaves out. All 50 padas have subpages (/১ to /৫০), and a
  1951 edition is there too. No free English translation found (the article's external link "Mystic Poems of Eighth
  Century India" is not identified).
- Video: b0Y7a-8PfhE, channel Saymon Zakaria (the scholar behind the "Caryāpada চর্যাপদ" project channel),
  Charyapada 1 "কাআ তরুবর" sung by Sadhika Srijoni Tania with Parvathy Baul on the ektara, 3:12; matches the words row.
  Second choice: FcBJISHwusQ, same channel, Charyapada 1 by Srijoni Tania, 6:16.
- Doubts: whether these performances sing the old text or Saymon Zakaria's contemporary Bengali rendering (other
  videos of the project say "সমকালীন বাংলা: সাইমন জাকারিয়া") should be checked by ear. Category People & Blogs.

## japji-sahib
- Status: pd (Guru Nanak's composition; Macauliffe's translation 1909).
- Words: orig pa: "ਜਪੁ ਜੀ", #1..#65 (Mool Mantar to the closing Salok; the page "ਜਪੁ" interleaves a word glossary, so
  not that one). en: Macauliffe, "The Sikh Religion/Volume 1/Japji" (Oxford 1909), in three rows that skip the two
  blocks of footnotes (#28..#32, #41..#44): #4..#27, #33..#40, #45..#103. The ranges keep the Roman-numeral pauri
  headings (I, II …) as one-line stanzas: the editor may drop them (nolabels or a narrower select). The Bengali
  Wikisource has a 1915 Bengali translation (Kiranchand Chattopadhyay), not used.
- Video: uuAPRAydzqg, Harshdeep Kaur (her own channel), the full Japji Sahib sung, 37 min (her composition, recorded at
  Yashraj Studios). Second choice: JjW_SU0oBwU, Bhai Jagjit Singh Ji - Topic, a fast recitation (12:49), the way it is
  recited daily rather than sung.
- Doubts: year 1530 and Guru Nanak's dates are not in the article. A 37-minute video is long but is the whole text.

  that may be laid out differently from Wikisource's: compare before aligning; the rom and en follow the Wikipedia
  Gujarati (#13..#17), which can replace the Wikisource orig if the editor wants the three parts to match.
