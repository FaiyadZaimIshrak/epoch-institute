## la-paloma
- Copyright: pd. Written by Sebastián Iradier c. 1860, registered in Madrid 1879; Iradier "was to die in obscurity
  within a few years" (no year in the article). The article does not say who wrote the words; I credit Iradier.
- Region: kept the batch's Americas (habanera, written after a visit to Cuba, spread via Mexico); the article calls it
  "a popular Spanish song", so Europe is defensible. Editor decides.
- Words: row proposed: w-es "La paloma (canción)" #56..#106 nolabels noparen /8,12,8,8,8 (Spanish Wikipedia prints the words, marked "Dominio público", one line per block: four verses of 8 lines and a 12-line chorus printed once; the "1.", "Coro:" and "(Coro)" cues dropped by the flags). Not tested by an import (I may not write imported/): check the counts. Spanish Wikipedia notes that "two variants of the words" exist; this is the one it prints. Its own Wikisource page "La paloma" (es) is a disambiguation of other works. No free English translation found: an Institute translation (TRANSLATION-GUIDE.md) would be needed for an English column. The meaning was revised to follow these words (the English article says nothing of them).
- Video: Victoria de los Ángeles - Topic, "La paloma (Orch. Gamley)" (clE-hvcmsyY), sung. Second choice: the same
  singer's other Topic upload 8sl4xaimLJo, or Pumeza Matshikiza - Topic D7VfXkBG_8U.

## oh-susanna
- Copyright: pd (Stephen Foster, 1826–1864; published 1848).
- The original words are racist minstrel dialect and the second verse holds a slur. If printed, the editor decides
  (Dixie was printed in its first-edition dialect).
- Words: no live row. en Wikisource "Oh! Susanna (1848)" is a transcluded 1848 Baltimore edition (F. D. Benteen, "as sung by the Ethiopian Serenaders", "Written by WELLS": one of the editions registered under another name) whose first verse is printed only under the music (scores disabled), so only verses 2-4 come through (#16..#20); I left that row commented out in rows-5.tsv. "Oh! Susanna (forty-niners' lyrics)" on Wikisource is marked "source document not known". No complete free first edition found; the Library of Congress scan (W. C. Peters, 1848) named in the article is a scan, not a text.
- Video: 2nd South Carolina String Band - Topic (yA_xr4mu7h0). Not verified that it is sung (the band usually sings;
  run audit-videos.js). Second choice: "Stephen Foster - Topic" hheTHxirZwM (1:39, performer not named in the title).
  Tom Roush's popular upload (qSIj17xbAyk, "Original 1848 Lyrics") is a private channel, not chosen.

  is sung), or the Tabernacle Choir's x68qktoGDgI.

- Copyright: pd only for the 1867 text (Slave Songs of the United States). Saletan's 1954 adaptation was copyrighted

- Words: row proposed: w-en "Michael, Row the Boat Ashore" #9..#9: the 22-line version in unrhymed couplets from Slave Songs of the United States (1867), as the article quotes it. Do NOT use #13/#15 (Seeger/Saletan, 1958 copyright), #19/#22 (Carawan field recordings, 1960s), #25 (1961 jail note) or #27-#28 (Belafonte 1962).
