# Batch 6: notes for the editor

Wikimedia's API answered 429 (too many requests) for much of the run, so peeks were slow and retried; pages that never
answered are said so below.

## shall-we-gather-at-the-river
- Copyright: pd. Robert Lowry (1826–1899), written 1864; the article says it "is now in the public domain".
- Words: English Wikisource "The Army and Navy Hymnal/Hymns/Shall We Gather at the River" (found by a Wikisource
  search; no Wikidata link), all five verses and the refrain, #7..#11 nolabels. The page prints each pair of lines as
  one line ("Shall we gather at the river, Where bright angel feet have trod,"), so text-fixes are needed to split them
  ("
" after each mid-line comma before a capital); also "riv er" in verse 4 and "(Last verse: Amen. )" after the
  refrain. The page's last block, a hymnal note "Copyright property of Mary Runyan Lowry. Renewal. Used by
  permission", is left out: it is the hymnal's renewal notice for its setting; the 1864/65 words are long free. The
  hymnal's year is not shown in the peek. Fallback: the chorus alone in the English Wikipedia article (#20..#23 /4).
- Video: The Tabernacle Choir at Temple Square (m1OrVBy4S2E, own channel, 5:02, sung). Second choice: Broadway Festival
  Choir - Topic (k68f8LUVBVA).
- Doubts: place left as "United States" (the article does not say where Lowry wrote it).

## ive-been-working-on-the-railroad
- Copyright: pd as traditional, first printed 1894. But the modern text is a composite whose "Dinah, won't you blow"
  lines are a later addition by "some college students" (undated). The 1894 "Levee Song" uses the n-word twice and
  must not be printed.
- Words: English Wikipedia article, the modern version only, #13..#34 /8,6,8 (railroad verse, "Dinah, won't you
  blow" chorus, kitchen verse with the fiddly-i-o refrain). Not the 1894 text (#36..#55), nor the Family Car Songbook
  verses (date and author unknown). English Wikisource "I've Been Working on the Railroad" is only a list of versions.
  Doubt: whether the "college students'" lines are old enough to be free; the article gives no date.
- Video: Pete Seeger - Topic (8u4gGgb232E, Smithsonian Folkways, American Favorite Ballads Vol. 5, 1:28, sung; Seeger
  is named in the article). Second choice: Pete Seeger - Topic live (V9W8PZIxzoA).

## linstead-market
- Copyright: pd for Walter Jekyll's 1907 text (and Roberts's 1925 variants). Olive Lewin's 1975 text, also quoted in the
  article, is not free and is not proposed.
- Words: English Wikipedia article, Jekyll's 1907 text only, #6..#6 (5 lines). The lines are broken as the article
  prints them, mid-phrase ("Me carry me akee a / Linstead market."), so the editor may regroup them with text-fixes.
  Not Roberts's Christiana variant (#9, Sollas market, 1925) and never Lewin's 1975 text (#11).
- Video: Louise Bennett - Topic (0FnVC94Hzwo, Smithsonian Folkways, Jamaican Folk Songs, 1954 Folkways Records, sung).
  Second choice: Lord Messam - Topic (1vnjFIKHKA8, a mento band).
- Doubts: types changed from the batch's "work" to "lament, dance"; year 1907 makes the era twentieth (batch:
  nineteenth); language written "en | English (Jamaican)".

- Copyright: pd, traditional, 19th century.
- Words: English Wikipedia article, the 19th-century couplets #20..#29 (Paradise Street to the warning about pretty
