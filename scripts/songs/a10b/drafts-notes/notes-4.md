# Batch 4 notes (wave A10b)

All words rows were test-imported with a scratch copy of fetch-texts.js (since removed); the counts below are what it
returned. Every video was checked through YouTube's oEmbed (exists, embeddable); check-videos.js and audit-videos.js

## tinsagu-nu-hana: Tinsagu nu Hana
- Status pd: traditional Okinawan song, authors not known (the Japanese article says 作詞・作曲者不明).
- No date at all in the article: year `~1700` is a sorting placeholder (era earlymodern from the wave list). Type
  "lullaby" is the list's guess; the article says "children's song".
- Words: w-en "Tinsagu nu Hana", orig #25..#48 /4, rom #50..#73 /4, en #75..#98 /4: 6 stanzas, 24 lines each, aligned.
  The article prints six of the ten verses. The English is Wikipedia's own (uncredited, CC BY-SA) and loose in places
  ("She will prosper"); the editor may prefer an Institute translation. The Japanese Wikipedia (w-ja) has another
  text with kana readings and a Japanese gloss, if wanted.
- Video: Rimi Natsukawa - Topic (IJ6B4t-hxxY). Second: Takashi Hirayasu and Yoshikawa Chuei - Topic (IqcTePfJIF8).

## aogeba-totoshi: Aogeba Tōtoshi
- Status pd: first printed 1884 in the Ministry of Education's Shōgaku shōkashū, part 3; all credited authors died by
  1928 (Kabe Iwao's dates not given); the American original, "Song for the Close of School" (1871), is also free.
- Words: ja Wikisource "仰げば尊し" (PD-old), #14..#16 nonum noruby: 3 stanzas, 12 lines, historical kana, each half
  line ending in "。" (the 1884 edition's punctuation; the editor may want a fix). No free romanization or English:
  an Institute translation is needed. The original English of 1871 is quoted in the Japanese Wikipedia, interleaved
  line by line with a Japanese translation; not proposed.
- Video: Suginami Children Chorus - Topic (cjPIZz3QQuM). Second: Hibari Children Chorus - Topic (EDg8gQpHRBA).

## list-of-compositions-by-liliuokalani: SKIPPED as a list; drafted instead as the-queens-prayer
- The article is a list of the Queen's compositions, not a song: skipped, as the brief says. Its one sacred entry with
  a section of its own, "The Queen's Prayer" (Ke Aloha O Ka Haku, 22 March 1895, under house arrest), can carry two
  short paragraphs, so I drafted it under the slug the-queens-prayer for the editor's choice (drop it if a list should
  not be a source). Aloha ʻOe is already on the site.
- Status pd: Liliʻuokalani d. 1917; composed 1895.
- Words: w-en "List of compositions by Liliʻuokalani", Hawaiian and English lines alternate one per block:
  orig #567..#599 alt0 /4,4,4,4,1 and en #568..#600 alt0 /4,4,4,4,1: 17 lines each (four quatrains and "Āmene" /
  "Amen"), aligned. The English is uncredited (Wikipedia's, CC BY-SA), not said to be the Queen's own translation.
  Stanza numbers shift if the list page is edited; re-peek before importing.
- Video: Emma Veary - Topic (NjDPjXh6EYI). Second: Darlene Ahuna - Topic (i1eH8Ud1nDM).

## oh-my-darling-clementine: Oh My Darling, Clementine
- Status pd: sheet music of 1884 (Montrose) and 1885 (Bradford).
- Words: en Wikisource "College Songs/Oh My Darling Clementine" ("Words and Music by Percy Montrose"), #4..#11 nonum
  nolabels norefs: 8 stanzas of 4 lines (seven verses and the chorus). norefs takes out the chorus's bracketed echoes
  ("(Oh Clementine,)"); check that no verse loses words it should keep. Other free texts there: "Clementine
  (Bradford)" (PD-US, 1885) and "Down by the River Liv'd a Maiden" (1863). The songbook's date and editor are not
  printed in what I saw, so the credit names only the songbook.
- Video: Pete Seeger - Topic (h-vaqAcC5Tg). Second: Burl Ives - Topic (k7H-sOrj-Nk).
