== story
"Senzeni Na?" ("What Have We Done?"), also spelled Senzenina, is a South African folk song of the struggle against apartheid, sung in Xhosa. Its origins are unclear; it has been sung at least since the 1950s. It was one of several songs of a mournful kind that became popular among anti-apartheid activists in the 1960s, and it reached the height of its popularity in the 1980s.

The song was sung at funerals, at demonstrations and in churches. Its question is repeated again and again, which the musician Sibongile Khumalo described as leaving the listener with a sense of desolation. No single agreed text exists, and some versions are angrier than others. The activist Duma Ndlovu compared its influence to that of the American protest song "We Shall Overcome", and Pete Seeger already had an adaptation of it in his repertoire in the 1950s and 1960s.

A recording of the song as sung at the funeral of Steve Biko can be heard at the end of the album version of Peter Gabriel's "Biko". It was sung in the funeral scene of the anti-apartheid film The Power of One and over the opening credits of the film In My Country, and Kenneth Nkosi sang it in a medley in the 2012 film Mad Buddies. Its music carries an adaptation of Isaac Watts's hymn "When I Survey the Wondrous Cross" in the Mennonite Hymnal: A Worship Book. The Zimbabwean poet Albert Nyathi wrote a different song of the same title on the day the activist Chris Hani died.
== meaning
A lament in the form of a question, asked over and over by people suffering under apartheid: what wrong have they done to be treated so. The repetition gives it the weight of mourning at a graveside, and in some versions the grief turns to anger and defiance.
