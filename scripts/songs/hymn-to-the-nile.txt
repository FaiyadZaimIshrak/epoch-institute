== story
The "Hymn to the Nile", also called the "Hymn to Hapy", was created and sung by the ancient Egyptians about the flooding of the Nile. The Greek historian Herodotus called Egypt the "Gift of the Nile", because ancient Egyptian civilization shaped its culture around the river and depended on its resources.

The ancient Egyptians believed that the Nile was a god, and the hymn speaks of the offerings, festivals and sacrifices made to it. They also depended on the fertile valleys beside the river for their farming, and many passages of the hymn show their gratitude to the Nile for the growth of their crops: it is praised as the lord of the fish and as the giver of grain and barley that keep the temples supplied.
== meaning
A hymn of praise to the Nile's flood as a god. It thanks the river for the water that makes the fields grow and for the fish and grain that feed people and temples, and it tells of the offerings and festivals held for it. The mood is grateful and solemn.
