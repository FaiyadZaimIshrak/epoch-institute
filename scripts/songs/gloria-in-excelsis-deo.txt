== story
"Gloria in excelsis Deo" ("Glory to God in the highest") is a Christian hymn also known as the Greater Doxology, the Angelic Hymn or the Hymn of the Angels. It opens with the words the angels sing to the shepherds at the birth of Jesus in the Gospel of Luke, and other verses were added very early to make a hymn of praise. The scholar David Flusser linked the verse in Luke with ancient Jewish liturgy. The hymn belongs to the psalmi idiotici, "private psalms" written by individuals in imitation of the biblical Psalter, which were popular in the 2nd and 3rd centuries; the Te Deum and the Phos Hilaron are other surviving examples. In the 4th century it became part of morning prayers, and it is still recited in the Orthros service of the Byzantine Rite.

Its Latin translation is traditionally attributed to Hilary of Poitiers (c. 300–368), who may have learned the hymn while in the East in 359–360. It belongs to the early Latin translations of scripture known as the Vetus Latina: the Vulgate was commissioned only in 382, and the hymn's Latin wording for "the highest" differs from the one Jerome later preferred.

In Rome, a tradition recorded in the Liber Pontificalis credits Pope Telesphorus (128–139?) with its use at the Mass of Christmas Day and Pope Symmachus (498–514) with its use on Sundays and the feasts of martyrs, at first only by bishops. Priests were later allowed to sing it, first only at Easter and on the day of their ordination; by the end of the 11th century they used it on Sundays and feasts outside Lent. After the 12th century Advent came to be treated as a penitential season like Lent, and the Gloria was left out of it. Today the Roman Rite sings or recites it at Mass after the Kyrie on Sundays outside Lent and Advent and on feasts. In the Byzantine Rite it is one of the high points of festal Matins, chanted while the Holy Doors stand open and every lamp and candle is lit; the Coptic Church recites it as the "Praise of the Angels", and the Syriac Orthodox Church sings it every day at the end of the midnight office.

The Church of England kept it in its Book of Common Prayer of 1549, then moved it to the end of the communion service, where it stayed in the 1552 and 1662 books until the 20th century. Lutheran churches use it in the Divine Service.

The Gloria has been sung to a great many melodies: scholars have catalogued well over two hundred from the medieval church. In the Middle Ages "farced" Glorias added phrases, often in honour of the Virgin Mary, and some were still sung when the Roman Missal was revised under Pope Pius V in 1570. Almost every polyphonic setting of the Mass includes it, and composers from Vivaldi, Handel and Bach to Poulenc, Rutter and Karl Jenkins have set it alone. The Christmas carol "Angels We Have Heard on High" takes its refrain from its opening.
== meaning
A hymn of praise. It begins with the angels' greeting at the birth of Jesus, then adores and thanks God, and turns to Christ, honoured as the Most High, with a plea to receive the prayers of the faithful. The mood is festive and exultant.
