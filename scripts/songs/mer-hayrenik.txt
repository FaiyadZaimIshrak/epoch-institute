== story
"Mer Hayrenik" ("Our Fatherland") is the national anthem of Armenia. Its words come from "The Song of an Italian Girl", a poem Mikayel Nalbandian wrote in 1859 and published in 1861; the anthem uses the first, third, fourth and sixth stanzas of the poem. The music was composed in the early 20th century by Parsegh Ganatchian.

The words, with changes, and the music were adopted as the anthem of the First Republic of Armenia, which existed from 1918 to 1920. In November 1920 the Red Army invaded Armenia, only three months after the signing of the Treaty of Sèvres, which had recognised the country as a sovereign state. In 1922 Armenia was absorbed, with Azerbaijan and Georgia, into the Transcaucasian Socialist Federative Soviet Republic, which became part of the Soviet Union at the end of the same year. The Bolshevik authorities outlawed "Our Fatherland" as a symbol of Armenian nationalism; from 1944 the Anthem of the Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic was used in its place. In these years the banned song became a song of protest against Soviet rule.

After the dissolution of the Soviet Union, the Supreme Soviet of the republic reinstated it as Armenia's national anthem on 1 July 1991, with several words changed from the 1918 version. It is one of the national symbols displayed in Armenian classrooms, and teachers encourage pupils to sing it every day.

Its words, which praise dying for Armenia's freedom, have been criticised by some commentators as "wimpy" and "gloomy", and some members of the National Assembly have argued that a song written during the first struggle for independence does not reflect the present. The anthem was debated in the Armenian Parliament in 2006 and in 2019, when proposals were made to bring back the anthem of the Armenian SSR with new words. None of the proposals succeeded; the Armenian Revolutionary Federation (Dashnaktsutyun), part of the governing coalition, resisted them. In 2025 the law on the anthem changed the order of its stanzas: the second became the third, the third the fourth and the fourth the second.

The anthem has been arranged and adapted. The Italian violist Marco Misciagna composed an "Introduction and Variations on Mer Hayrenik" for solo viola and played it at the Komitas Chamber Music House in 2018, and Philip Sheppard recorded it with the London Philharmonic Orchestra for the 2012 Summer Olympics and Paralympics. A television channel for Armenians living in Russia, based in Novosibirsk, takes its name from the anthem.
== meaning
A patriotic hymn that calls on Armenians to defend their country's freedom and honours those ready to die for it. Its tone is solemn and earnest; critics have found it more mournful than triumphant.
