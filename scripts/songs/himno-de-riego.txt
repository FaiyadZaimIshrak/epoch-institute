== story
In 1820 Colonel Rafael del Riego led an uprising that restored Spain's liberal constitution of 1812 and opened the period known as the Trienio Liberal (1820–1823). Between 31 January and 6 February 1820, in the town of Algeciras, Evaristo Fernández de San Miguel, a lieutenant colonel of the flying column under Riego's command, wrote the words of a song in his honour; Riego had earlier turned down another text by Antonio Alcalá Galiano. The music is usually attributed to José Melchor Gomis.

On 7 April 1822 the Cortes Generales approved a decree declaring the song the national anthem of Spain. King Ferdinand VII signed it at Aranjuez two days later, and it was published in the Gaceta de Madrid on 14 April. In October 1823 Ferdinand overthrew the liberal government and repealed the constitution, and the Marcha Real became the national anthem again.

After the Glorious Revolution of 1868 overthrew Isabella II, the song had only a modest place in the First Spanish Republic (1873–1874), when "La Marseillaise" and the "Himno de Garibaldi" were more popular. By the time of the Second Spanish Republic (1931–1939) it had regained its popularity and became the chief musical symbol of the government. It was never formally declared the Second Republic's anthem; one reading is that the republicans regarded the 1822 decree as still in force. The republic used a shorter version made from three of the original verses, and the Republican government in exile went on using the song until it dissolved in 1977, after the end of Franco's rule.

During the Spanish Civil War a rude satirical text sung to the tune, mocking the church and the monarchy, was very popular, especially in Catalonia, and opponents of Franco kept singing it after the war.

Because it was once Spain's anthem, the song has more than once been played by mistake in place of the Marcha Real. Che Guevara claimed that when the bell of Cusco Cathedral in Peru was rededicated in 1952, at the Francoist government's expense, after a 1950 earthquake, it played the Himno de Riego instead of the Spanish anthem, to the dismay of Spanish officials. It was played in error at a European championship qualifying match in Prague on 1 October 1967, and at the 2003 Davis Cup final in Australia, where the musician James Morrison performed it; Australian officials blamed an error on the CD supplied to him, and the Spanish sports authorities lodged an official protest.
== meaning
A patriotic marching song of the liberal soldiers of 1820, celebrating their rising for the constitution and urging the defence of the country's freedom. Its mood is martial and confident.
