== story
"Deșteaptă-te, române!" ("Awaken Thee, Romanian!") began as a poem written during the revolution of 1848. Andrei Mureșanu published it that year under the title "Un răsunet" ("An Echo"), as a reply to Vasile Alecsandri's poem "Către Români" ("To Romanians"), later known as "Deșteptarea României" ("The Awakening of Romania"), from which he took many of his themes and motifs; the two poems are much alike. The original text was written in the Romanian Cyrillic alphabet. Mureșanu asked Gheorghe Ucenescu, a church singer of the Șcheii Brașovului quarter of Brașov, to find a melody for it; after Ucenescu sang him several secular tunes, Mureșanu chose instead a sentimental song by Anton Pann, "Din sânul maicii mele". It was first sung in late June 1848 in the streets of Șcheii Brașovului and at once became the anthem of the revolution. Nicolae Bălcescu called it the "Romanian Marseillaise".

It remained a favourite of Romanians and was sung during the country's major conflicts: at Romania's declaration of independence from the Ottoman Empire during the Russo-Turkish War of 1877–1878, in the First World War, and on the radio, very often, in the days after the coup of 23 August 1944, when Romania turned against Nazi Germany and joined the Allies. It was also used on solemn occasions in the short-lived Moldavian Democratic Republic of 1917–1918.

After the Communist Party abolished the monarchy on 30 December 1947, "Deșteaptă-te, române!" and other patriotic songs tied to the old regime were outlawed. Nicolae Ceaușescu's government later allowed it to be played and sung in public, but it was never the anthem of the Socialist Republic of Romania. It was sung during the anti-communist revolution of December 1989, and on 24 January 1990 it became the national anthem, replacing the communist-era "Trei culori" ("Three Colours"). From 1991 to 1994 it was also the national anthem of Moldova, which then replaced it with "Limba noastră" ("Our Language").

The poem has eleven stanzas. By Romanian law the first, second, fourth and last are sung on official occasions; the full version is sung at major events such as the National Holiday on 1 December, with a 21-gun salute when the President is present, while short versions, as at sporting events, keep only the first stanza and the first half of the second. 29 July is observed each year as National Anthem Day.
== meaning
A call to action: Romanians are urged to wake from a long sleep under oppression and seize their freedom now or never, taking courage from the memory of their ancestors and standing together as one nation. The mood is urgent and stirring.
