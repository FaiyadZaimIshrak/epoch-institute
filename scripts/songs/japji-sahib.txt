== story
Japji Sahib is the composition that opens the Guru Granth Sahib, the scripture of the Sikhs. Its original name is Jap, from the Sanskrit japa, the recitation of a mantra, "to utter in a low voice, repeat internally"; "ji" and "Sahib" were added out of respect. It is mostly the writing of Guru Nanak and was put together by Guru Angad: it begins with the Mool Mantra, continues with 38 paudis (stanzas) in different poetic metres, and ends with a Salok by Guru Angad. It is counted the first composition of Guru Nanak and the essence of Sikhism, which the rest of the Guru Granth Sahib expands; its themes are what true worship is and what the nature of God is.

Japji Sahib is the first text of the Nitnem, the daily prayers. The scholar Christopher Shackle describes it as designed for "individual meditative recitation" and as the first item of daily devotion for the devout. It is chanted in the morning and evening prayers of the gurdwaras, at the initiation ceremony of the Khalsa and at cremations. A related text, the Jaap Sahib of Guru Gobind Singh, opens the Dasam Granth; it is longer, at 199 stanzas, chiefly in Braj-Hindi and Sanskrit, and lists some 950 names of God.

In the second half of the 19th century many printed editions (Pōthi Japji) appeared: at Lahore from 1865, in Gurmukhi and in Perso-Arabic script, some with commentaries by Bihārī Lāl and Maṇi Siṅgh; at Amritsar in 1875 and 1882; at Karachi in 1875 in the Khoja-Sindhi script; at Sialkot and at Rawalpindi. Ernst Trumpp printed the original text as an appendix to his translation of the Guru Granth Sahib (1877), with spaces between the words instead of the traditional continuous script. Translations into Hindustani appeared at Lahore in 1870 and Gujranwala in 1879, and a Hindi translation at Lucknow in 1887. Max Arthur Macauliffe included a tentative English translation in a circular letter to the Sikhs dated Amritsar, 24 December 1897, and published a translation in the Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society in 1900. The Japanese scholar Tomio Mizokami later translated it into Japanese.
== meaning
A meditation on the one God and on true worship: outward rites, silence or riches cannot make a person pure, which only love of the divine and remembering God's name can. It describes God as formless, without beginning or end, the author of every fortune, praised by every tradition yet beyond all words, and it closes on the value of listening, faith and grace.
