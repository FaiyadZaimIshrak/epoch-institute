== story
"Where Are You, Dear General?" was written as the theme song of the 1971 revolutionary opera "A True Daughter of the Party" (Tangŭi ch'amtoen ttal). Its words are attributed to Kim Jong Il, later the country's Supreme Leader; the Korean article describes him as its presumed composer. The opera is set during the Korean War and follows an army nurse, Kang Yeon-ok. She sings the song when she finds that the hospital to which she is escorting patients has moved, and in it she voices her lifelong wish to meet her leader, the "dear general" Kim Il Sung.

The song belongs to the cult of personality around the Kim family. The state propaganda site DPRK Today called it an "immortal classic masterpiece". It became a regular piece for official choirs and for popular music groups such as the Pochonbo Electronic Ensemble, and is often broadcast on North Korean television.

A version by the Pochonbo Electronic Ensemble has been played at 6 a.m. every day, since at least 2008 by the reports of tourists, through loudspeakers on the clock tower of Pyongyang Railway Station, apparently as a city-wide morning call; it has often been played there in the evening and at night too, after the clock chimes. The writer Travis Jeppesen, in his 2018 book "See You Again in Pyongyang", likened its sound through the loudspeakers to an antiquated synthesizer such as a theremin; others have called it heavily distorted and hard to recognise.
== meaning
A song of longing for Kim Il Sung, sung in the opera by a nurse who wishes to meet him; tender and devotional.
