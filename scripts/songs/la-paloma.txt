== story
"La Paloma" ("The Dove") was written by the Spanish Basque composer Sebastián Iradier (earlier spelt Yradier) around 1860, after a visit to Cuba. It is one of the first written examples of the Afro-Cuban habanera rhythm to become widely popular. In 1879 it was registered at the copyright office in Madrid as a "Canción Americana con acompañamiento de Piano" (an American song with piano accompaniment). Iradier died in obscurity within a few years of writing it and never learned how popular it would become.

The song soon spread beyond Spain, above all to Mexico, and from there around the world. German and French versions appeared in the 1860s, and in the Portuguese novel O Crime do Padre Amaro, written in 1871 by José Maria de Eça de Queirós, it is called "la Chiquita, an old Mexican song". In places as far apart as Afghanistan, Cuba, Colombia, Hawaii, the Philippines, Germany, Romania, Venezuela, Zanzibar and Goa it came to be treated almost as a folk song. There are more than a thousand versions; with the Beatles' "Yesterday" it is counted among the most-recorded songs in the history of music, and it is the most-recorded Spanish song.

Its image of the dove is traced back to 492 BCE, before Darius the Great's invasion of Greece, when the Persian fleet under Mardonius was wrecked in a storm off Mount Athos and the Greeks saw white doves, probably homing pigeons carried by the fleet, escaping from the sinking ships. From this came the idea that such birds carry home a last message of love from a sailor lost at sea.

In the 20th century the song was taken up by opera singers, jazz and pop musicians, rock and folk performers and military bands. Harry James recorded it in 1941; "No More", with English words by Don Robertson and Hal Blair, was written for Elvis Presley to sing in the film Blue Hawaii (1961) and was also recorded by Dean Martin; Connie Francis's French version "Jamais" (1961) reached the top ten in Canada and France; Brendan Bowyer's version reached No. 1 in Ireland in 1963; and Mireille Mathieu's "La Paloma, Adieu" (1973) was a hit across Europe. In films it was sung by Hans Albers in a German version in Große Freiheit Nr. 7, which Nazi censorship kept from German screens in 1944 and which the Allies released in 1945, and by Rosita Serrano in Das Boot (1981); it plays in The Godfather Part II (1974) at the New Year party in Havana. On 9 May 2004 a choir of 88,600 people sang it in Hamburg, a Guinness world record, and Sigrid Faltin's documentary La Paloma. Sehnsucht. Weltweit (2008) is about the song.
== meaning
A light-hearted love song in the habanera rhythm. A man leaving Havana asks his sweetheart to treat kindly a dove that comes to her window, for the dove is himself, and later verses look forward playfully to their wedding and a houseful of children. Behind the image lies the old belief that a dove carries home a last message of love from a sailor lost at sea.
