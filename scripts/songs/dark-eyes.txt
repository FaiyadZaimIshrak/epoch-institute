== story
"Dark Eyes" (in Russian Ochi chyornye, literally "Black Eyes") is a Russian romance, a sentimental art song. Its words are by the Ukrainian poet and writer Yevhen Hrebinka, born in Poltava, who first published the poem in his own Russian translation in Literaturnaya Gazeta on 17 January 1843. The words appeared in a songbook in 1874, without a melody.

The tune now sung to them was taken from the "Valse hommage", Op. 21, for piano, by Florian Hermann, a composer of German-Polish origin working in the Russian Empire, published in 1879. The sheet-music historian James Fuld reports that a Soviet musicologist told him the song was not a Russian traditional song but a cabaret song, published in 1884 and reprinted in 1897 in a songbook by A. Gutheil, where it was called a "Gypsy romance based on the melody of Florian Hermann's Valse Hommage".

The singer Feodor Chaliapin made his own version, with changed words and further verses, and dedicated it to his future wife. He sang it with Rachmaninoff at the piano, and Rachmaninoff especially valued one of Chaliapin's recordings of it. Chaliapin's version became a typical example of the gypsy-style Russian romance.

A second widely played version was written by the Italian-born British composer Adalgiso Ferraris, who spent many years in Russia before 1915. He published it there in 1910, with the German editor Otto Kuhl, as "Schwarze Augen", and again on 9 October 1931, with Paris Editions Salabert and Jacques Liber, as "Tes yeux noirs (impression russe)". It was one of his main successes of the 1920s and 1930s. Albert Sandler played it in 1932, a British Pathé film of 1934 shows Ferraris in the orchestra of Alfredo and his Gypsy band playing it, and Al Bowlly sang it in 1939 as "Black Eyes", with English words by Albert Mellor.

The melody has also been claimed elsewhere. In Rebeca Chávez's 2010 documentary, the Cuban musician Sindo Garay (born 1867) said that he had composed it in the 1890s as the bolero "Ojos negros que fascinan", for a Russian choir-girl of a visiting opera company, and that the musicians took it back to Russia. The Sundanese folk song "Panon Hideung" is derived from "Dark Eyes".
== meaning
A romance addressed to the dark eyes of a beloved, in the sentimental, gypsy-style manner of the Russian romance.
