== story
"Plaisir d'amour" ("The Pleasure of Love") is a French love song written in 1784 by the composer Jean-Paul-Égide Martini (1741–1816). Its words come from a poem by Jean-Pierre Claris de Florian (1755–1794), which appears in his novel Célestine.

Martini's song was a great success. In 1816 a young woman, Julie Charles, sang it to the poet Alphonse de Lamartine while he was taking a cure at Aix-les-Bains, and Lamartine recalled it thirty years later. Hector Berlioz arranged it for orchestra in 1859, and in the 1880s Louis van Waefelghem arranged the tune for viola d'amore or viola and piano.

In the 20th century it was recorded by singers of many kinds, among them Yvonne Printemps (1931), Paul Robeson (1940), Joan Baez (1961) and Mary Hopkin, in Welsh, in 1971. It was a theme of the films Love Affair (1939) and The Heiress (1949). Its melody was the basis of Elvis Presley's "Can't Help Falling in Love" (1961) and of the 20th-century Christian hymn "My God Loves Me". In his short story "Chagrin d'amour" (1908), Hermann Hesse invented a medieval origin for it, giving it to an obscure troubadour who sings it to a queen he loves without hope.
== meaning
A song of lost love, tender and sorrowful.
