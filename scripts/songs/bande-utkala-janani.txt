== story
"Bande Utkala Janani" ("Glory to the Mother Utkala!") is a patriotic poem in Odia written by Laxmikanta Mohapatra in 1912. Over the centuries outsiders, the Mughals, the Marathas and the British, had ruled Odisha, and its boundaries had changed with each of them. In the early 20th century a movement for a separate province gathered strength, led by the Utkal Sammilani, a federation of Odias. It influenced the young Mohapatra, who wrote this and other fiery songs with a patriotic aim. The song was first sung at the Utkal Sammilani's conference at Balasore in 1912, and the Sammilani used it as a weapon in its campaign. The poet Banchhanidhi Mohanty, who was also a good singer, sang Mohapatra's patriotic songs throughout Odisha, and at a meeting at Bhadrak where Mohapatra was honoured, the young leader Harekrushna Mahtab said that the poet's songs had inspired him a great deal. On 1 April 1936 Odisha became the first state in British India to be formed on a linguistic basis.

The music was first recorded by Gokul Mohanty in 1932, and the Odissi musician Balakrushna Dash arranged the modern notation, which was later adopted for the anthem. In the early 1990s the Chief Minister of Odisha, Biju Patnaik, asked the Speaker of the Odisha Legislative Assembly, Yudhistir Das, to have the song played at the end of Assembly sessions, and in 1994 an all-party meeting chaired by Das adopted it to be played and sung at the valedictory session of the Assembly.

In 2020 the Chief Minister, Naveen Patnaik, encouraged people to recite "Bande Utkala Janani" to raise the morale of the health workers fighting the coronavirus outbreak. On 7 June 2020 a meeting of the state cabinet that he chaired gave the poem the status of the anthem of Odisha.
== meaning
A hymn of praise to Utkala, the Odia homeland, addressed as a mother. It pictures a land that keeps its self-respect and dignity from confidence and strength rather than from fear, and calls on its people to honour her. The mood is devoted and proud.
