== story
"Tian Mi Mi" ("sweet honey") began as an Indonesian song. Its melody comes from "Dayung Sampan", a gambang kromong folk song of the 1940s about sampan boats by the pasindhèn (singer) Dasimah, popular in Indonesia and Singapore; according to Huang Yuyuan of the National Taiwan Museum, the original was about fishers gossiping and calling to one another. The Taiwanese singer Teresa Teng recorded an Indonesian-language cover of "Dayung Sampan" while touring Southeast Asia in 1978.

On 14 February 1979 Teng was arrested in Japan and deported to Hong Kong for travelling on an Indonesian passport that Japan found had been obtained from the Indonesian embassy in Hong Kong under suspicious circumstances. Fearing punishment in Taiwan, then under an autocratic government, and unable to stay in Hong Kong or to re-enter Japan for a year, she went to the United States and enrolled at the University of Southern California. To meet her contract she made a Mandarin version of "Dayung Sampan" while studying. The lyricist Zhuang Nu, who would write many of her later songs, gave it new words in a playful, sweet tone, reportedly in about five minutes after hearing they were for Teresa Teng.

The song first appeared on Teng's album An Unforgettable Day, released in Taiwan by Kolin Records on 20 September 1979, and then on an album named after it, issued by PolyGram Records on 15 November 1979 and certified Platinum in Hong Kong. On 18 December 1980 she released a Cantonese version, "The Day I Met You", with words by Lo Kwak-chan, on her first Cantonese album, Irreconcilable.

"Tian Mi Mi" became widely popular across the Sinophone world and remains one of Teng's best-known songs. Young couples have used it to tell each other their feelings, and it has been covered by Faye Wong, Stephen Chow, Leon Lai, Yu Quan, Jane Zhang and others. In December 2008, marking thirty years of China's reform and opening up, the newspaper Southern Weekly named it one of the ten classic Chinese pop songs of those years.

Teng died in 1995. The next year the Hong Kong film Comrades: Almost a Love Story, starring Maggie Cheung and Leon Lai, was released as a tribute to her, with "Tian Mi Mi" as its Chinese title and her songs running through the story. Because of political tension between Hong Kong and mainland China, the film was approved for release on the mainland only in 2015, nineteen years later; for that release the singer Luhan recorded a new version of the song, arranged under Dou Feng at the director Peter Chan's request and issued on 3 February 2015.
== meaning
A gentle love song about tenderness and familiarity, whose title means "sweet as honey". Its mood is warm and light rather than sorrowful.
