== story
The "Lied der Partei" (Song of the Party), also known as "Die Partei hat immer recht" (The Party Is Always Right), was the anthem of the Socialist Unity Party of Germany (SED), the ruling party of East Germany. Its words and music were written in 1949 by the German-Bohemian Louis Fürnberg (1909–1957), who had joined the Communist Party of Czechoslovakia in 1928, and it was first performed in 1950.

Though the song spread as a hymn of praise, above all in East Germany, the occasion for it was the opposite. Fürnberg, then living in Prague, was not invited to the Ninth Congress of the Communist Party of Czechoslovakia in 1949 because he was regarded as a German, which deeply hurt him. His widow, Lotte Fürnberg, explained in 2001 that he wrote the song to call himself back to order and to justify the slight to himself. The German article notes that, read closely, the lines show not only demonstrative obedience to the party in the climate of denunciation of the time but also a desperate defiance; years later Fürnberg told his wife that the song would one day do him great harm.

The cult of the party that the song expresses is regarded as typical of Leninism, which declared the Communist party the vanguard of the working class. The song also carried the personality cult of Joseph Stalin, who was named in its chorus. After the 20th Congress of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union in February 1956, with a more critical view of Stalin's rule, the text was altered. After the 22nd Congress, from November 1961 the Stalin cult was no longer part of East Germany's state ideology, and Stalin's name was removed from the song altogether (by 1964 according to the English article, from 1965 according to the German one).

By the Honecker era at the latest, "The Party is always right" had become a common saying in the GDR. In 1961 the writer Ralph Giordano, who had followed Stalinism until about 1955, used the phrase as the title of a book settling accounts with it. Since its founding in 2004 the German satirical party Die PARTEI has used a version with altered words as its "final party anthem".
== meaning
A hymn of praise to the Communist party, presenting the party as infallible and, in its early form, honouring Stalin; earnest and devotional.
