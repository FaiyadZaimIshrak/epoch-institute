== story
"La Adelita" is one of the most famous corridos, or narrative ballads, of the Mexican Revolution, and it has been adapted many times. It was inspired by Adela Velarde Pérez, a woman who joined the Maderista movement in the early stages of the revolution. She became a popular icon and a symbol of the role of women in the revolution, and "adelita" came to mean the same as soldadera: a woman who supported, and sometimes fought beside, the revolutionary armies against the federal government's forces.

The song's heroine stands for thousands of real women. Soldaderas and camp followers, from young girls to middle-aged women and often of mestiza or indigenous origin, came from Oaxaca, Morelos, Tehuantepec, Central Mexico, Sonora and elsewhere. Some joined because poverty left them no other work; some were forced, when President Victoriano Huerta raised the quota of his standing army and sent women to the front as cooks; some followed husbands who had been drafted or had enlisted. Angela Jimenez, whose sister died after federal soldiers searched their home, dressed as a boy and fought beside her father under the name "Angel".

Soldaderas made and cooked tortillas, nursed the wounded, carried messages, spied in enemy camps, stole documents and smuggled arms from the United States into Mexico. Female soldiers, usually of higher social standing, fought alongside the men. After the war many women went home; some, like Amelia Robles, went on living as men; some received veterans' pensions while many were refused; and many who were not welcomed back moved to California, where Angela Jimenez founded an organisation of veterans of the revolution of 1910–1920.

The song and its heroine have been read in different, often conflicting ways. Corridos tended to make Adelita a lover or a caretaker, and the song helped fix the idea of soldaderas as romantic figures, while the real women showed courage and endurance. Some read it as the voice of male soldiers facing death; the feminist scholar María Herrara-Sobek argues that Adelita's own bravery is lost in the soldiers' desires and fears. In dance she appears as brave and heroic: the folk dance "las Adelitas", performed only by women, is very popular at Chicano festivals.
== meaning
A soldier's love song about Adelita, a woman of the revolutionary camps who is admired for her beauty and respected for her courage. Its mood mixes devotion and longing with the uncertainty of men going into battle.
