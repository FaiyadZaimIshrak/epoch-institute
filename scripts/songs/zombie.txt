== story
"Zombie" is an Afrobeat protest song by the Nigerian musician and bandleader Fela Kuti, recorded with his band Africa 70. Kuti's politics owed much to his mother, Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti, an anti-colonial activist who led the Abeokuta Market Women's Movement in the 1940s, and to a visit to the United States in 1969, where he met the Black power movement and Pan-Africanist writing. By the mid-1970s Nigeria had lived through a decade of military rule after a series of coups and the Nigerian Civil War, and under General Olusegun Obasanjo soldiers were a constant presence in public life. Kuti had already attacked corruption and military abuses in songs such as "Alagbon Close", "Kalakuta Show" and "Ikoyi Blindness".

The song was recorded in 1976 at the Kalakuta Republic, Kuti's compound in Lagos. Kuti composed, arranged and produced it, sang lead and played saxophone and keyboards; the drummer Tony Allen gave it its polyrhythmic drumming. Coconut Records released it in Nigeria in late 1976 as the title track of the album Zombie, beside "Mister Follow Follow", another song about obedience to authority, and international editions followed in 1977, among them Creole Records in the United Kingdom. The track runs 12 minutes and 26 seconds and draws on Yoruba percussion, highlife, jazz harmony and funk. The voices enter only about five minutes in, singing in Nigerian Pidgin English in call and response; a later section takes the shape of a military drill, so that the performance itself imitates the system of command and obedience it mocks. Scholars have read this use of musical form as central to the song's political meaning.

"Zombie" became widely popular in Nigeria and the neighbouring countries and was taken up by civilians as an anthem, which undermined deference to the armed forces. The authorities banned it from the airwaves, and people who played it in public could face arrest or violence, but it went on being played in homes and public places. The song enraged the military government of General Obasanjo and sharpened a confrontation that had begun before it.

On 18 February 1977 an estimated 1,000 armed soldiers cordoned off and stormed the Kalakuta Republic. Kuti played "Zombie" on the saxophone from the roof before the attack. The soldiers set fire to the compound, assaulted its residents and destroyed instruments, sound equipment and master recordings. Kuti was severely beaten, and his 77-year-old mother was thrown from a second-storey window; her injuries led to her death in April 1978. The raid cannot be put down to the song alone, since Kuti's conflict with the authorities predated it. Kuti carried his mother's coffin to Dodan Barracks in Lagos, the head of state's residence, and answered the government's blame of "unknown soldiers" with the songs "Unknown Soldier" and "Coffin for Head of State".

The song is counted among the defining works of Afrobeat and of 1970s political music. Kuti's sons Femi Kuti and Seun Kuti have performed it, and it has been covered and sampled around the world. In 2026 it was still recognised in Nigeria as a symbol of protest.
== meaning
A protest song against blind military obedience under Nigeria's military government. Its mood is mocking and defiant, carried by a long, driving dance groove.
