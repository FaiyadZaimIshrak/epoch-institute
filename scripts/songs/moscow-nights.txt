== story
"Moscow Nights" is a Soviet song with words by Mikhail Matusovsky and music by Vasily Solovyov-Sedoy, who wrote it in 1955 under the title "Leningrad Nights". At the request of the Soviet Ministry of Culture it was renamed "Moscow Nights", and the words were changed to match.

In 1956 Vladimir Troshin, a young actor of the Moscow Art Theatre, recorded it for a documentary about the Spartakiad, the athletic competition of the Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic, in a scene where the athletes rest in Podmoskovye, the suburbs of Moscow. The film did little for the song, but radio broadcasts made it popular.

The song soon travelled abroad. In Finland it was sung as "Unohtumaton ilta" (Unforgettable Evening) by many artists, among the earliest Georg Ost in 1958. On 4 January 1961 the Dutch jazz group New Orleans Syncopators recorded an arrangement by their leader Jan Burgers as "Midnight in Moscow"; Kenny Ball and his Jazzmen used the same arrangement in November 1961, and their record reached number two on the UK Singles Chart in January 1962 and on the US Billboard Hot 100 in March 1962, and spent three weeks at number one on the American Easy Listening chart. In 1962 the Chad Mitchell Trio sang it with its Russian words on their live album At the Bitter End, bringing it to American listeners at a time of strained relations between the United States and the Soviet Union, and Ketty Lester recorded it for her album Love Letters.

In 1964 the Ukrainian tenor Anatoliy Solovianenko took third place with it on the Italian television show Napoli contro tutti, and Italian choirs recorded it as "Mezzanotte a Mosca". Jan Johansson recorded a jazz version for his 1967 album Jazz på ryska, James Last recorded it, and in 2003 the Chinese composer Gao Ping made it the basis of one of his Soviet Love Songs for Vocalising Pianist.

From 1978, when Radio Moscow began its 24-hour English service, the station played an instrumental big-band version between its frequency announcements and the hourly news. In 2015 the Massed Bands of the Moscow Garrison, under Lieutenant General Valery Khalilov, played a march arrangement of it as the contingents of Azerbaijan, Armenia, Belarus and Kazakhstan marched past at the Moscow Victory Day Parade.
== meaning
A quiet evening song set in the countryside around Moscow; gentle and wistful.
