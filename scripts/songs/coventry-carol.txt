== story
The "Coventry Carol" is the second of three songs in the Pageant of the Shearmen and Tailors, a nativity play that was one of the Coventry Mystery Plays, performed by the city's guilds. The pageant tells the Christmas story from the second chapter of the Gospel of Matthew. The carol is sung by three women of Bethlehem, who come on stage with their children just after an angel has warned Joseph to take his family to Egypt; it is a lullaby of the mothers whose children are about to die in the Massacre of the Innocents, when Herod ordered all boys under two in Bethlehem to be killed. Its author is unknown. Coventry's guild pageants are recorded from 1392 onwards, but the date of the text is not known.

The only text of the pageant and its songs was edited by Robert Croo (or Crowe), who dated his manuscript 14 March 1534. Croo worked for some years as a manager of the city pageants: over twenty years he was paid for playing God in the Drapers' Pageant, for making and mending costumes and props, for new dialogue and for copying out the Shearmen and Tailors' Pageant "newly correcte". He seems to have adapted older material and added verse of his own. The music was added to his manuscript later by Thomas Mawdyke, whose additions are dated 13 May 1591. Mawdyke wrote it in three parts (alto, tenor and baritone), which shows that the "mothers" were played by men, as was usual in the mystery plays; whether he composed the music is debated, and its style may point to an earlier date. The setting contains a well-known Picardy third and a striking false relation. Mawdyke, perhaps a tailor of St Michael's parish in Coventry, probably wrote it down for an attempt to revive the plays in the summer of 1591, which the city authorities in the end did not support.

Religious changes led to the suppression of the plays in the later 16th century, but Croo's prompt book survived. The Coventry antiquarian Thomas Sharp published a transcription in 1817 in his study of the city's mystery plays, and a second edition with the songs' music in 1825. Both were meant as facsimiles, copying Croo's spelling and layout. This proved fortunate: the manuscript, by then in the Birmingham Free Library, was destroyed in a fire there in 1879, so Sharp's transcriptions are the only source.

Although the plays were performed in summer, the lullaby is now sung as a Christmas carol. It reached a wide audience at Christmas 1940, shortly after the Bombing of Coventry in the Second World War, when the BBC's Empire Broadcast ended with the carol sung in the bombed-out ruins of the cathedral. The surviving pageants have been revived in the cathedral since 1951. Modern settings have been written by Kenneth Leighton, Philip Stopford and Michael McGlynn, and a four-part setting of the tune by Walford Davies.

A different tune, the "Appalachian" variant, was collected by the folklorist John Jacob Niles in Gatlinburg, Tennessee, in June 1934, by his account from an old woman who wished to stay anonymous. It has a largely similar text with an added verse. It has not been found elsewhere, and there is reason to think that Niles wrote it himself; Joel Cohen, however, found an 18th-century shape-note song with some of the carol's words and a tune somewhat like Niles's, and argued that the variant is likely to be authentic.
== meaning
A lullaby sung by mothers in Bethlehem to their infant sons, who are about to be killed on Herod's orders. It is a quiet, sorrowful lament in which the cradle song and the grief of the mothers are one.
