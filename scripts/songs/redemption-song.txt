== story
"Redemption Song" is the last track of Uprising, the twelfth album of Bob Marley and the Wailers, produced by Chris Blackwell and released by Island Records in 1980. It is reported to have been written around 1979 and first appears on a demo tape known as the "Dada Demos". The German journalist Teja Schwaner later claimed to have heard Marley play it in a Hamburg hotel on tour in 1976, though he may have confused it with another song. Marley had by then been diagnosed with the cancer, first found in his toe, that killed him in 1981; Rita Marley said that he was already in great pain in secret and was facing his own mortality, and that this can be heard on the album and in this song above all.

Some of its key lines are drawn from a speech that the Pan-Africanist Marcus Garvey gave at Menelik Hall in Sydney, Nova Scotia, Canada, in October 1937 and published in his magazine Black Man. Marley was reciting Garvey's words in public at least as early as July 1979, at the Amandla Festival.

Unlike nearly all of Marley's work, the album recording is a solo acoustic performance: his voice and an acoustic guitar (an Ovation Adamas), in G major, with no reggae rhythm. The biographer Timothy White called it an "acoustic spiritual" and Stephen Davis a "total departure"; in message and sound it recalled Bob Dylan. Marley first played it in public on 30 May 1980 in Zürich, at the opening concert of the Uprising Tour, and it was in every known setlist of that tour; a concert recording from Dortmund on 13 June 1980 was released in 2014 on Uprising Live. A full-band version was issued as a single in the United Kingdom and France in October 1980, but the solo recording is the one listeners know.

The song came to be counted among Marley's greatest works. In 2009 the Jamaican poet and broadcaster Mutabaruka named it the most influential recording in the history of Jamaican music. Rolling Stone placed it 66th among "The 500 Greatest Songs of All Time" in 2004 and 42nd in the list's 2024 revision, and in 2010 the New Statesman listed it among the twenty best political songs. In 2017 BBC Radio 4's Soul Music devoted an episode to it, with Marley's art director Neville Garrick, the Jamaican Poet Laureate Lorna Goodison, John Legend and the Wailers guitarist Don Kinsey. On 5 February 2020, the eve of what would have been his 75th birthday and forty years after its release, Marley's estate released an animated video for it.
== meaning
A song about freedom and the dignity of people descended from the enslaved, in the spirit of Marcus Garvey. Its mood is spare, personal and hopeful.
