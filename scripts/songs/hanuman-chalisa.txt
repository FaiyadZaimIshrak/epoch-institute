== story
The Hanuman Chalisa, "forty chaupais on Hanuman", is a Hindu devotional hymn (stotra) in praise of Hanuman, written in Awadhi by Tulsidas, a poet-saint of the 16th century. Tulsidas, devoted to Rama, is best known for the Ramcharitmanas, his retelling of the Ramayana in the Awadhi vernacular. He lived in Varanasi until his death, founded the Sankat Mochan Hanuman Temple there, believed to stand where he saw Hanuman, and began the Ramlila plays, folk theatre of the Ramayana. Hanuman, a general of the vanaras and Rama's devotee and warrior in the war against Ravana, is worshipped as a deity in his own right, and in the Shaiva tradition as an incarnation of Shiva.

The name comes from chālīs, "forty", the number of its chaupais (quatrains). With two introductory dohas and one closing doha the hymn has forty-three verses. The first doha honours the guru, understood as Shiva, Hanuman's teacher. The first ten chaupais describe Hanuman's form, knowledge, virtues and bravery; the next ten his service to Rama, among them his part in reviving Lakshmana; then the poet turns to Hanuman's grace and ends by asking him to dwell in the hearts of his devotees, with Rama, Lakshmana and Sita. The hymn holds that whoever recites it gains protection, merit and even liberation.

Millions of Hindus recite it every day, and many know it by heart; Tuesdays and Saturdays are traditionally Hanuman's days. Yet no commentary was written on it before the 1980s, which Rambhadracharya ascribes to its absence from printed editions of Tulsidas's collected works. Indubhushan Ramayani wrote the first brief commentary, and Rambhadracharya's Mahaviri commentary in Hindi followed in 1983.

It is one of the best-selling Hindu religious books and has been sung by many devotional, classical and folk singers. Hari Om Sharan's recording, released in 1974 by the Gramophone Company of India and again in 1995, set to traditional melodies in the raga Mishra Khamaj, is played in temples and homes across northern India. A 1992 recording on the same melodies, sung by Hariharan with Gulshan Kumar, became in November 2025 the first devotional song and the first video on YouTube to pass five billion views. M. S. Subbulakshmi, Pandit Jasraj and Bhimsen Joshi sang it, Amitabh Bachchan recorded it with twenty other singers in 2011, and the American singer Krishna Das performs it in the West. Hindi films such as 1920 and Bajrangi Bhaijaan use it in key scenes.

== meaning
A hymn of praise and petition addressed to Hanuman. It celebrates his strength, courage, wisdom and devotion, recalls his deeds in Rama's service, and asks him for protection and to live in the devotee's heart together with Rama, Lakshmana and Sita. Its mood is confident, energetic and devout.
