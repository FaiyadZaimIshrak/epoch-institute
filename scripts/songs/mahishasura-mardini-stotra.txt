== story
The Mahishasura Mardini Stotra is a Hindu stotra, a hymn of praise, of 21 verses. Its name comes from an epithet of the goddess Durga, "the slayer of the demon Mahisha". It praises Durga, a principal aspect of the supreme goddess Mahadevi, and her killing of the asura Mahishasura.

Its authorship is attributed to the theologian Adi Shankara. The hymn is mentioned in the 53rd chapter of the first part of the Shivarahasya Purana.

It draws on the Devi Mahatmya and refers to several of the goddess's legends: the slaying of Mahishasura, of Raktabija, and of Chanda and Munda. In the Devi Mahatmya's account of Mahishasura's slaying, the asuras under Mahishasura drove the devas out of Svarga, and in anger the gods Brahma, Vishnu and Shiva, the Trimurti, joined their energies, which took the form of the goddess Durga. Armed with the gods' weapons, she killed the shape-shifting Mahishasura, who took the forms of a lion, an elephant, a buffalo and finally a man. The gods glorified her as the first being and the origin of the Vedas, and she promised to save them whenever they were in danger.
== meaning
A hymn of praise to Durga. Verse after verse it hails her titles and attributes and celebrates her victories over the demons, returning each time to a cry of victory to the slayer of Mahishasura. The mood is joyful and exultant.
