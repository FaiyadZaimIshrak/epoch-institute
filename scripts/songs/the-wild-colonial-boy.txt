== story
"The Wild Colonial Boy" (Roud 677, Laws L20) is a traditional Irish-Australian folk ballad by an unknown author. It tells of a bushranger in early colonial Australia who dies in a gunfight with the local police, and it romanticises his fight against colonial authority. It is sung to an earlier Scottish melody, "The Black Douglas".

Versions of the ballad name the bushranger differently: always "Jack", with surnames such as Dolan, Doolan, Duggan and Donahue. It is unclear whether the song first referred to a real person. One possible origin is Jack Donahue (also spelled Donohoe), an Irish convict of the 1820s who was sent to Australia, became a bushranger and was killed by police. Another is John Doolan, a juvenile convict of the 1860s born in Castlemaine, Victoria, who also turned to bushranging; but the real Doolan was not shot by police: he was captured and sentenced to a further convict term. The two histories may have blended over time, and the name may also have been changed to hide the song's origins.

By 1880 the ballad was well known. According to a report in The Argus in November 1880, Ann Jones, the innkeeper of the Glenrowan Hotel, asked her son to sing it when the Kelly Gang were at her hotel in June of that year.

In the 20th century it travelled far beyond Australia. It is sung in John Ford's film The Quiet Man (1952), in the scene where John Wayne's character, Sean Thornton, first enters the bar in the Irish village of Innisfree. In early 1981 the American band Dr. Hook sang it on the Australian television programme The Don Lane Show; their studio recording, issued on Australian pressings of their album Rising and as a single, reached No. 4 on the Australian charts in April 1981. In 1990 The Pogues and The Dubliners used its tune for "Jack's Heroes", a single celebrating the Republic of Ireland national football team.
== meaning
A ballad of a young bushranger who defies the colonial authorities and dies fighting the police. It tells his story with sympathy and admiration, making the outlaw a hero of resistance.
