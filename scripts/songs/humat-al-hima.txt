== story
"Defenders of the Homeland" (Arabic: Ḥumāt al-Ḥimā) is the national anthem of Tunisia. Its words come from a poem written in the 1930s by the Egyptian poet Mostafa Saadeq Al-Rafe'ie, and its last verses are by the Tunisian poet Aboul-Qacem Echebbi; according to the Tunisian musicologist Salah El Mahdi, those verses were added in June 1955 by the nationalist Mongi Slim. Who wrote the march's melody is disputed: some say Mohammed Abdel Wahab, while El Mahdi holds that it was the poet Ahmed Kheireddine, and that the poem's original music was by Zakariyya Ahmad.

Known as the "Hymn of the Revolution", it was sung at the meetings of the ruling party, the Neo Destour, which later changed its name to the Socialist Destourian Party. When the monarchy ended on 25 July 1957 it replaced the "Salam al-Bey" and served as a provisional national anthem until 20 March 1958, when "Ala Khallidi" took its place. It was brought back after the coup d'état of 7 November 1987 that brought Zine El Abidine Ben Ali to power, and it has been the anthem since. On occasions that call for brevity a short version is sung: the chorus, the third verse once, and the chorus again.
== meaning
A patriotic march addressed, as its title says, to the defenders of the homeland; a stirring, martial song, sung as the "Hymn of the Revolution" of the Neo Destour party before it became the anthem.
