== story
"The Bonnie Blue Flag", also known as "We Are a Band of Brothers", is a marching song of 1861 associated with the Confederate States of America. The entertainer Harry McCarthy wrote the words and set them to the melody of "The Irish Jaunting Car". The title refers to the Bonnie Blue Flag, the unofficial first flag of the Confederacy. McCarthy first sang it at a concert in Jackson, Mississippi, in the spring of 1861, and again that September at the New Orleans Academy of Music for the First Texas Volunteer Infantry regiment. The New Orleans publisher A. E. Blackmar issued six editions between 1861 and 1864, with three further arrangements.

The song names the seceding states one by one and so serves as a list of them, though for the sake of the metre it changes the order in which they left the Union, from South Carolina on 20 December 1860 to Tennessee on 8 June 1861. Its editions differ: the 1861 Blackmar edition held by the Library of Congress gives the Confederates' cause as liberty, while another 1861 edition, held by Duke University's library, gives it as property gained by honest toil; eleven other editions were published with different words. When Union Major General Benjamin Butler captured New Orleans he is said to have arrested Blackmar, fined him $500, destroyed the copies of the music and fined anyone caught singing or whistling the song $25. Annie Chambers Ketchum, a Confederate widow, published new verses as "The Gathering Song".

The Union answered with its own versions. J. L. Geddes, a British-born colonel, wrote "The Bonnie Flag With the Stripes and Stars" in 1863, singing of the Union and of equality, and a Mrs C. Sterett published another in New York; the 69th New York regiment of the Irish Brigade sang its "Song of the Irish Volunteers" to the same tune. After the war the song lived on in recordings, such as Polk Miller and his Old South Quartet's cylinder of 1913, and in films of the Civil War and the West, from Gone with the Wind (1939) and The Searchers (1956) to Gettysburg (1993) and Gods and Generals (2003).
== meaning
A rallying song of the Confederacy that presents the seceding states as a band of brothers joining one by one under a single flag to fight for their cause. Its mood is proud and martial.
