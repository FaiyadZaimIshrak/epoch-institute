== story
"White Christmas" was written by Irving Berlin for the 1942 musical film Holiday Inn. Accounts differ as to when and where he wrote it. One story is that he wrote it in 1940 in warm La Quinta, California, while staying at the La Quinta Hotel, a retreat favoured by Hollywood; the Arizona Biltmore also claims the song was written there. Berlin, who often stayed up all night writing, is said to have told his secretary that it was the best song he had ever written, and indeed the best song anybody had ever written.

Bing Crosby gave the first public performance on his NBC radio show The Kraft Music Hall on Christmas Day 1941, a few weeks after the attack on Pearl Harbor. He recorded it for Decca Records on 29 May 1942, in 18 minutes, with the John Scott Trotter Orchestra and the Ken Darby Singers, and it was released on 30 July in an album of six 78-rpm discs from Holiday Inn. Crosby at first saw nothing special in it. In the film he sings it as a duet with the actress Marjorie Reynolds, whose voice was dubbed by Martha Mears; the original script had Reynolds, not Crosby, singing it. The song won the Academy Award for Best Original Song at the 15th Academy Awards.

It began slowly, overshadowed by another song from the film, but by the end of October 1942 it topped the Your Hit Parade chart, and Crosby's record spent eleven weeks at the top of the Billboard charts that year. It also reached number one on the Harlem Hit Parade for three weeks, Crosby's first appearance on that black-oriented chart. Its mixture of melancholy and comforting images of home has often been said to have spoken especially strongly to listeners during the Second World War, and the Armed Forces Network was flooded with requests for it. Crosby's nephew Howard Crosby recalled his uncle saying that the hardest thing he did in his career was to sing it at an outdoor USO show in northern France in December 1944, with Bob Hope and the Andrews Sisters, before 100,000 soldiers in tears, many of whom were killed in the Battle of the Bulge a few days later.

The record returned to number one in later holiday seasons and reappeared on the pop chart year after year. Because the 1942 master was worn out by use, Crosby recorded the song again on 19 March 1947 with the same orchestra and singers, and with every effort to reproduce the first session; this is the version most often heard on the radio today. The song was used again in the 1954 musical film White Christmas, the highest-grossing film of that year. Crosby's single is credited with sales of at least 50 million copies, which Guinness World Records recognises as the best-selling single of all time; with other versions the song is counted at more than 100 million. More than 500 versions have been recorded, in several languages, among them those of Frank Sinatra in 1944 and of the Drifters, with the lead singer Clyde McPhatter, in 1954.

Writers on popular music see it as a turning point. The song, written by a Jewish immigrant to the United States, showed that secular Christmas songs could succeed commercially; Dave Marsh and Steve Propes wrote that it changed Christmas music by revealing the market for such songs and by establishing the themes of home and nostalgia that run through them. During the Vietnam War a recording was broadcast on Armed Forces Radio on 30 April 1975 as a secret, pre-arranged signal precipitating the U.S. evacuation from Saigon. The 1942 recording entered the Grammy Hall of Fame in 1974 and was added to the Library of Congress's National Recording Registry in 2002.
== meaning
A song of nostalgia for the Christmases of the past and of longing for home. Its mood is wistful and gentle rather than festive.
