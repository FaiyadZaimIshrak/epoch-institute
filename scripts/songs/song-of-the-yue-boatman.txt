== story
The Song of the Yue Boatman is a short song in an ancient language of southern China, from the Yangtze River valley, said to have been recorded around 528 BC. It survives in the Garden of Stories (Shuoyuan), compiled by Liu Xiang five centuries later, which preserves both a transcription of the original sounds in Chinese characters and a Chinese version.

In the Garden of Stories the song sits inside a story within a story. A minister of the state of Chu, infatuated with a handsome nobleman, the Lord of Xiangcheng, tells him of the 6th-century BC prince Zixi, Lord of È. On an excursion on his state barge, Zixi was struck by the singing of his Yue boatman and asked for an interpreter to translate it. The song praised rural life and told of the boatman's secret pleasure at knowing the prince. On hearing it, the prince embraced the boatman and covered him with his embroidered coverlet. The minister points out that Zixi was the Chu king's brother and prime minister, yet a Yue boatman could become close to him; persuaded, the Lord of Xiangcheng lets the minister hold his hand, which he had refused before. The story became an emblem of love between men in imperial China, and Feng Menglong placed it in the chapter on love between men in his anthology History of Love (Qing Shi, c. 1628–1630).

The original words were written down in 32 Chinese characters, each standing for the sound of a non-Chinese syllable. Because the sounds of Old Chinese that those characters stood for are uncertain, the song is hard to read. In 1981 the linguist Wei Qingwen proposed an interpretation by comparing it with Tai languages, especially the Zhuang varieties spoken today in Guangxi. Building on his work, Zhengzhang Shangfang produced a version in written Thai of the late 13th century, as the closest available approximation to the original language. Both readings correspond loosely to the 54-character Chinese version but have nothing matching its third and ninth lines, which Zhengzhang suggests were added to fit the Chu Ci poetic style. His interpretation remains controversial, because nearly two thousand years separate the song from written Thai and because Thai belongs to the more distant Southwestern Tai languages. Qin Xiaohang has argued that this kind of transcription, though not a true writing system, formed the basis of the later Sawndip script for Zhuang.
== meaning
A boatman's song in praise of country life, in which he tells of his secret joy at being in the company of a prince. Its mood is warm, shy and admiring.
