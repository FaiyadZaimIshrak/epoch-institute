== story
The "Marcha Real" (Royal March), officially the "Marcha Real Española" and also called the "Marcha Granadera" (Grenadier March), is the national anthem of Spain, one of the oldest in the world. It was first printed in a book of military fife and drum calls for the Spanish infantry by Manuel de Espinosa, dated 1761, under the title "La Marcha Granadera"; the document names Manuel de Espinosa de los Monteros as its composer. A story first published in 1861 claimed it had been composed by Frederick II of Prussia and given to a Spanish officer sent to learn Prussian tactics; it was repeated in the press, with the recipient changing, until it entered the Enciclopedia Espasa in 1908, but it is spurious.

In 1770 Charles III declared the Grenadier March the official Honour March, formalising the custom of playing it in public on solemn occasions, and it became the official Spanish anthem in the reign of Isabella II. After the Revolution of 1868, General Juan Prim held a national contest for a new anthem. More than 400 compositions were sent in, among them works by the young Federico Chueca, Ruperto Chapí and Tomás Bretón, but the jury advised that the Grenadier March was already regarded as the anthem and the contest was suspended. Under Alfonso XIII a Royal Circular Order of 27 August 1908 made the orchestration by Bartolomé Pérez Casas, musician of the Royal Corps of Halberdier Guards, the official version. The Second Spanish Republic used the Himno de Riego instead.

The anthem has never had official words. Eduardo Marquina wrote words for Alfonso XIII's silver wedding anniversary, and José María Pemán wrote others in 1928, under Primo de Rivera; Pemán later changed his to carry the symbols of Francisco Franco's regime, the raised-arm salute and the Falange's yoke and arrows. None of these was made official, and school textbooks of the Franco era taught still other words. The Carlists, a rival monarchist faction, had a version of their own. Since 1978 the anthem has been played without words, though the Francoist words have occasionally been performed by mistake.

The current version, arranged by Francisco Grau, has been official since a Royal Decree of 10 October 1997, which fixes its key, tempo, form and length of 52 seconds, with arrangements for orchestra, military band and organ; the long version is the honours music for the King. Because Pérez Casas's harmonisation was still under copyright, the state bought the rights from his heirs in 1997 for 130 million pesetas; collecting societies still charge fees for it, which has drawn criticism. In 2007, ahead of Madrid's bid for the 2016 Olympic Games, the Spanish Olympic Committee's president Alejandro Blanco sought words for the anthem after hearing a crowd sing at Anfield. A television contest chose words by Enrique Hernández-Luike, and a second competition chose words by Paulino Cubero, but these were criticised and withdrawn five days later, and the idea was set aside.
== meaning
A ceremonial military march without official words, played in honour of the King and of Spain at state, military, sporting and church occasions. Its mood is stately and festive; the many attempts to give it words, from the monarchy, from the Franco regime and from the Olympic committee, show how hard it has been to agree on what the anthem should say.
