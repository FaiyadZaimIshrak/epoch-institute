== story
"Funiculì, Funiculà" is a Neapolitan song composed in 1880 by Luigi Denza in his home town of Castellammare di Stabia, with words by the journalist Peppino Turco. It celebrated the opening that year of the first funicular railway on Mount Vesuvius, and it was Turco who prompted Denza to write it, perhaps as a joke. It was first sung at the Quisisana Hotel in Castellammare di Stabia and then presented by Turco and Denza at the Piedigrotta festival the same year. It became immensely popular in Italy and abroad: Casa Ricordi published the sheet music, which sold more than a million copies within a year.

The song was soon taken for a folk song. In 1884 the New York publishers Spear & Dehnhoff printed it with English words as "Tra-la-la-lee", "adapted and arranged by W. T. Harris", without naming Denza. Richard Strauss heard it on a tour of Italy six years after it was written, thought it a traditional Neapolitan tune and used it in his tone poem Aus Italien; Denza sued him and won, and Strauss had to pay him a royalty. Nikolai Rimsky-Korsakov made the same mistake in his "Neapolitan Song" of 1907. Herman Bellstedt wrote a theme and variations on it, Napoli, and Arnold Schoenberg arranged it for ensemble in 1921.

In English-speaking countries it is often sung with words by Edward Oxenford, a lyricist and translator of librettos, whose version, often titled "A Merry Life", has little to do with the original. Singers from Beniamino Gigli and Mario Lanza to Luciano Pavarotti and Andrea Bocelli have recorded it, and it has been sung in German and Japanese; in 1960 Robert B. and Richard M. Sherman wrote new English words to the tune as "Dream Boy", which Annette Funicello released as a single. The melody has lived on in parodies, films, cartoons and video games, and since a children's parody of 1991 it has been linked with pizza and is often played in pizzerias. Denza died in 1922, and the song has been in the public domain since 1 January 1993.
== meaning
A young man compares his sweetheart to a volcano and invites her to ride up with him to the top of Vesuvius on the new railway. The mood is high-spirited and teasing, a song of a day out and of courtship.
