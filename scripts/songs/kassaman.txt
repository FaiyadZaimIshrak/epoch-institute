== story
"Kassaman", or "Qassaman" (Arabic for "we pledge", "the oath" or "we swear"), is the national anthem of Algeria. France invaded Ottoman Algeria in 1830 and made it an integral part of Metropolitan France within its colonial empire; for the next century the native population was given very few political rights. A nationalist movement began in the 1920s and gained strength after the Second World War, when a government commitment to grant French Algeria autonomy came to nothing. One of its prominent members was Moufdi Zakaria, a Mozabite Berber poet of the Algerian People's Party (PPA), who was jailed and tortured several times between the 1920s and 1962.

It was during one of these imprisonments, in April 1955, that he wrote the words of "Kassaman". Having no paper or pen in Barberousse Prison, he is said to have written them in his own blood on the walls of his cell. The music was then composed by the Egyptian composer Mohamed Fawzi, who was asked to do so after two earlier settings by other composers, one of them by Mohamed Triki, had been rejected. Words and music were officially adopted in 1962, the year in which the Évian Accords were signed and a referendum followed in which Algerians voted overwhelmingly for independence, which was duly granted. Meant only as a provisional anthem, "Kassaman" has remained in use ever since.

The words read like a war song: they promote nationalist ideals on the front line, glorify the actions of the National Liberation Front (FLN), present armed uprising as the only road to independence, and name France, the former colonial ruler, foretelling a day of reckoning for it. For brevity, at sporting events and the like, usually only the first verse is sung. In November 2008 an amendment to Article 5 of the Constitution of Algeria declared the anthem "immutable", for its association with the revolution, and confirmed that it comprises all its verses, ending the debate over whether the verse that refers to France was still appropriate. By decree of President Abdelmadjid Tebboune, all the stanzas, the third one that refers to France included, must be performed at any event the President of the Republic attends, among them welcome ceremonies for visiting heads of state, presidential inaugurations and FLN congresses.
== meaning
A solemn oath to fight for Algeria's freedom, honouring the National Liberation Front and the armed struggle against French rule; martial, defiant and grave.
