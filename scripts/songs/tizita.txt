== story
Tizita (also Tezeta; Amharic ትዝታ) means memory, nostalgia or longing. The word names one of the qenet, the pentatonic scales or modes of the Amhara people of Ethiopia, and the songs sung in it. The term belongs to Amharic and Tigrinya and has no equivalent in Ge'ez. Tizita folk songs developed in the countryside among the Amhara peasantry and the village musicians called azmaris.

Tizita songs became a popular genre in both Ethiopia and Eritrea, named after the mode in which they are sung, and are known for moving listeners deeply, among the Amhara and among a great many other Ethiopians. Western writers often compare tizita to the blues; other comparisons are the Portuguese saudade, the assouf of the Tuareg, the Romanian dor and the German Sehnsucht.

In the 1960s the tizita mode became one of the foundations of Ethiopian jazz, or Ethio-jazz, which joined it with elements of Ethiopian Orthodox Christian music and with Western instruments; Mulatu Astatke was its pioneer. Modern Ethiopian musicians who have performed tizita songs include Aster Aweke, Hailu Mergia, Bezawork Asfaw, Teddy Afro, Mulatu Astatke, Meklit Hadero, Seyfu Yohannes and Mahmoud Ahmed.
== meaning
Tizita is less one song than a mood and a musical mode: songs of remembrance and longing for what has been lost, whether a person, a place or a past time. The mode itself carries the feeling, so that listeners recognise the ache of memory before the words.
