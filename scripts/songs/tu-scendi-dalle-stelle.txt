== story
"Tu scendi dalle stelle" ("You Come Down from the Stars") is a Christmas carol from Italy, written in 1744 by Alphonsus Liguori in the musical style of a pastorale. Liguori was a prominent Neapolitan priest and scholastic philosopher, later canonised, who founded the Redemptorist missionary order. He wrote both the melody and the original words. By one account he wrote the song while staying at the Convent of the Consolation, one of his order's houses in the small city of Deliceto, in the province of Foggia in south-eastern Italy, and called it "Little song to Child Jesus".

The Italian song came after an earlier one that Liguori had written in Neapolitan, "Quanno nascette Ninno" ("When the child was born"), entitled "For Jesus' birth" and sometimes called the "Carol of the Bagpipers" (Canzone d'i zampognari). The Italian "Little song" became a widely popular Christmas carol in Italy. It is found in many arrangements and is commonly sung, but it is traditionally associated with the zampogna, the large Italian bagpipe.

The earliest known sound recording was made by the Italian baritone Rodolfo Fornari, a member of the Boston Opera Company, on 21 September 1915 in New York City for Columbia Records, under the title "Novena Di Natale". A year later an unknown ensemble and singer recorded it for Columbia, though the record was not released until 1917, and on 24 September 1917 the Italian-American tenor Pasquale Feis recorded it for Victor as "Pastorale Di Natale". In later decades recordings began to use the original title, crediting various composers and arrangers, among them Liguori and Tommaso Capocci, or calling the song traditional. An edition published in 1932 by A. Paolilli's Music Co. of Providence, Rhode Island, credits the music to Capocci and the words to Pope Pius IX and does not mention the earlier work.

The tune has been used by other composers. Domenico Scarlatti used it in his Sonata in C major, Kk513, and Ottorino Respighi took the carol for "Adoration of the Magi", the second movement of his Trittico botticelliano (1927). Luigi Tutela composed a "Novena Di Natal" that closely resembles Liguori's tune and words; the Reverend Francis Auriemma recorded it for Victor in New York City on 19 September 1916. Anthony Velona and Remo Capra set English words to the music as "O Bambino", also known as "One Cold and Blessed Winter", which several singers recorded in the late 1960s, before the history of the song was widely known. The Italian-American tenor Sergio Franchi recorded the traditional Italian version for Durium and again, in a slightly updated arrangement, on his RCA Victor album The Heart of Christmas (Cuor' Di Natale) of 1965. The Italian composer Piero Niro wrote Three Variations on "Tu scendi dalle stelle" for large orchestra (2000), and Ralph Woodward has arranged the carol for unaccompanied choir. There are several English translations in the style of the King James Bible, and at least one in modern English.
== meaning
A song addressed to the infant Jesus, who has come down from heaven to be born in the cold of a winter night. The singer wonders that the maker of the world should lie poor and without warmth, and sees in it how much the child's love has cost him. The mood is tender and full of pity.
