== story
"İstiklal Marşı" ("Independence March") is the national anthem of the Republic of Turkey and of the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus. It was made during the Turkish War of Independence, when the Turkish National Movement led by Mustafa Kemal Atatürk was fighting both invading foreign powers and the Ottoman court, which it held to be complicit in the partition of the Turkish homeland after the Treaty of Sèvres of 1920.

In 1921 the movement organised a nationwide competition for a National March, meant to encourage the forces fighting for independence and to give pride to the homeland that would be founded after victory. 724 poems were sent in. Mehmet Âkif Ersoy, a well-known poet of the time, at first refused to take part because the competition offered a money prize, but the National Parliament persuaded him to submit a poem and set the reward aside; the prize later went to a society of veterans. His ten-stanza poem was read to the National Assembly by the deputy Hamdullah Suphi on 1 March 1921 and, after a parliamentary committee had examined it, unanimously adopted. The Grand National Assembly officially adopted it as the anthem on 12 March 1921, two and a half years before the Republic was proclaimed on 29 October 1923. Ersoy's manuscript is dedicated "To Our Heroic Army", the national forces that went on to win the war.

The music took longer to settle. The first setting, by a composer named Çağatay, lasted only six years. In 1930 Parliament adopted a new composition by Osman Zeki Üngör, a virtuoso and the first conductor of the Presidential Symphony Orchestra, and in 1932 the government commissioned the musician Edgar Manas to harmonise and orchestrate Üngör's melody, which gave the anthem its final official form.

The anthem has 41 lines in ten stanzas, but only the first two are sung, at state and military events, national festivals and bayrams, sporting events and school ceremonies. In Turkish classrooms a framed copy usually hangs above the blackboard, beside the Turkish flag, a photograph of Atatürk and his address to the nation's youth from his speech to Parliament of 20 October 1927. The first two stanzas appeared on the back of the Turkish 100 lira banknotes of 1983–1989. In 1983 the Turkish Republic of Northern Cyprus adopted it as its own anthem under Article II of its constitution.
== meaning
A poem of love for the Turkish homeland, its flag and its soil, and of faith and freedom. It praises hope, devotion and sacrifice in the struggle for independence and honours the soldiers who fought for it; the mood is solemn and defiant.
