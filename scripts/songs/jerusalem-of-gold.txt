== story
Naomi Shemer wrote "Yerushalayim Shel Zahav" for the Israeli Song Festival held in West Jerusalem on 15 May 1967. The city's mayor, Teddy Kollek, commissioned it as a piece outside the competition, and Shemer chose Shuli Natan, then an unknown singer, to perform it. Its melody is largely based on the Basque lullaby "Pello Joxepe", which Shemer first heard when the singer Paco Ibáñez performed it in Israel in 1962 to a group that included her and Nehama Hendel; she later acknowledged that she had drawn on it and regretted the likeness. Ibáñez said he bore no ill will, was glad the melody had contributed, and did not see it as plagiarism.

Since the 1948 Palestine War Jerusalem had been divided, and the Old City and East Jerusalem were under Jordanian control. Jews had been expelled from them and were not allowed to return or to enter, and many Jewish holy sites there had been damaged. The song, drawing on old Jewish poetry of exile and longing for Jerusalem, expressed the wish of many Jews to return to those parts of the city. Three weeks after its first performance the Six-Day War broke out, and the song quickly became a morale song for the Israel Defense Forces; Shemer had sung it for soldiers even before the festival.

On 7 June 1967 Israeli forces captured East Jerusalem and the Old City from Jordan. Shemer was about to sing for a unit of paratroopers when she heard that the Western Wall and the Temple Mount had been taken. She quickly wrote a new verse to replace the one that mourned that the Temple Mount could not be visited; it marked the return to the Old City and paid tribute to Rabbi Shlomo Goren, who had sounded the shofar at the Western Wall when it was captured. Shemer later said the new verse was not meant to replace the old one and that the original three verses remained the official song.

"Yerushalayim Shel Zahav" was named Israel's Song of the Year in 1967 and the Song of the Jubilee at the country's 50th Independence Day in 1998, when Ofra Haza sang it at the celebrations. It is often contrasted with the national anthem, "Hatikvah". The Israeli collecting society for authors and composers counts more than 300 versions, in Spanish, Yiddish, Portuguese, French, Finnish and English among other languages; Chaim Topol sang an English translation on The Ed Sullivan Show days after the war, probably the first time it was heard in the United States. Its use near the end of the 1993 film Schindler's List caused controversy in Israel, where it is widely seen as an informal anthem of the 1967 victory with no link to the Holocaust; Israeli prints replaced it with "A Walk to Caesarea", written by the resistance fighter Hannah Szenes in 1942.
== meaning
A song about Jerusalem, written as a lament for the parts of the city Jews could not reach and as an expression of longing for them. It is woven from older Jewish texts of exile and mourning for Jerusalem, from the Bible to medieval Hebrew poetry. The verse Shemer added in June 1967 turns that sorrow into joy at the return.
