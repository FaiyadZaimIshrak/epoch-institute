== story
Liliʻuokalani (1838–1917), the last queen of the Hawaiian Islands, was one of Hawaiʻi's most accomplished composers and musicians; she wrote more than 165 songs and chants. "The Queen's Prayer", in Hawaiian "Ke Aloha O Ka Haku", is a mele she composed on 22 March 1895 while she was held under house arrest at ʻIolani Palace, and dedicated to her niece and heir apparent, Victoria Kaʻiulani. At the foot of the manuscript she wrote: "Composed during my imprisonment at ʻIolani Palace by the Missionary party who overthrew my government." She meant the overthrow of the Hawaiian monarchy by United States business interests, backed by the United States government. The hymn was published as "Liliʻuokalani's Prayer", with its Hawaiian title and an English translation, "The Lord's Mercy"; it is now commonly called "The Queen's Prayer".

It was one of several songs of her imprisonment. "Kuʻu Pua I Paoakalani" was written for John Wilson, a young haole supporter whose mother, Evelyn T. Wilson, went into voluntary imprisonment with the Queen: he regularly brought her flowers from her garden at Paoakalani wrapped in newspaper, and through the newspapers she could read the news that was kept from her. She set Psalms 121 and 126 in hymns dated June 1895, and in "Ka Wai ʻApo Lani" expressed the hope that she would be returned to the throne.
== meaning
A prayer of a captive queen. She praises the Lord's love as high as the heavens and his truth as perfect, speaks of her own sorrow and imprisonment, and calls him her light and support; she asks him not to look in anger on the sins of men but to forgive and cleanse them, and to keep her people in peace beneath his wings for ever. The mood is quiet, grieving and forgiving.
