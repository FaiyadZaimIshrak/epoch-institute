== story
The Llibre Vermell de Montserrat, the "Red Book of Montserrat", is a manuscript collection of devotional texts that includes a small group of late medieval songs. It was prepared in approximately 1399 at the monastery of Montserrat outside Barcelona, in Catalonia, where it is still kept. The monastery held the shrine of the Virgin of Montserrat, a major site of pilgrimage at the time. The manuscript originally had 172 double pages, of which 32 have been lost, and six folios contain music. Its name comes from the red binding in which it was placed in the 19th century. No composer is named for any of its songs.

The anonymous compiler explained in a Latin note why the songs were written down. Pilgrims keeping their night watch in the church of the Blessed Mary of Montserrat, and in the square by day, wished to sing and dance; since only chaste and pious songs were fit for the church, these were set down for them, to be used modestly so as not to disturb those at prayer. The songs were thus meant for singing and for circle dances.

Ten songs survive, in Catalan, Occitan and Latin, most of them, by their titles, addressed to the Virgin Mary; the last, Ad Mortem Festinamus ("We hasten towards death"), is a virelai on death. They include virelais, a ballad-dance, songs and a motet. Some are monophonic and can be sung as two- or threefold canons; others are set in two to four parts, usually in non-imitative polyphony. They combine features of folk song and hymn.

The book was written near the end of the 14th century, but much of its music appears from its style to be older. Among them is the motet Imperayritz de la ciutat joyosa / Verges ses par misericordiosa ("Empress of the happy city" / "Virgin, out of mercy"), whose two texts are sung at the same time, a manner that would already have been old-fashioned when the manuscript was compiled.

Their simple forms, dance rhythms and strong melodies have made these songs some of the most frequently recorded pieces of early music. Recordings began with Atrium Musicae in 1971 and include Jordi Savall with Hespèrion XX (1979) and with La Capella Reial de Catalunya and Hespèrion XXI (2016), as well as Micrologus, Studio der Frühen Musik and Ensemble Unicorn.
== meaning
A pilgrims' songbook: judging by their titles, most of the songs praise the Virgin Mary, calling her a star, a queen of heaven and a virgin mother, and invite the singers to join together in her praise. One song, Ad Mortem Festinamus, turns to death and the shortness of life. The book's own note says they were meant to keep night vigils and daytime dancing devout.
