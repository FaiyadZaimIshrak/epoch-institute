== story
The Stabat Mater is a 13th-century Christian hymn to the Virgin Mary. It portrays her suffering as a mother at the crucifixion of her son, Jesus. Its title comes from its first line. The hymn has long been ascribed to the Franciscan friar Jacopone da Todi (c. 1230–1306), and also to Pope Innocent III. The attribution to Jacopone has been strongly challenged by the discovery of the earliest notated copy, in a 13th-century gradual that belonged to the Dominican nuns of Bologna.

By the end of the 14th century the hymn was well known. Georgius Stella wrote of its use in 1388, and around 1399 it was sung in Provence during processions lasting nine days. The Council of Trent later suppressed it as a sequence of the Mass, together with hundreds of other sequences. Pope Benedict XIII restored it to the missal in 1727, for the Feast of the Seven Dolours of the Blessed Virgin Mary. It is sung today in the liturgy on the memorial of Our Lady of Sorrows. An indulgence of 100 days was attached to each recitation.

The poem survives in several versions; one commonly printed is the text of an 1853 Roman Breviary. Edward Caswall made an English translation that is not literal but keeps the original's trochaic metre and rhymes.

Few medieval texts have been set to music so often. Composers from Josquin des Prez and Palestrina in the Renaissance, through Vivaldi, Pergolesi (1736), Haydn (1767) and Rossini (1831–1841), to Dvořák (1876–1877), Verdi (1897), Poulenc (1950) and Arvo Pärt (1985) have written settings, and a website devoted to them counts some 250. Most are in Latin. Karol Szymanowski's setting of 1925–1926 is in Polish, and George Oldroyd's of 1922 is in Latin with an English translation for Anglican and Episcopalian use.
== meaning
A meditation on the grief of Mary as she stands at the foot of the cross where her son is dying. The mood is sorrowful and devotional, dwelling on a mother's suffering.
