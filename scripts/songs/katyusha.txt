== story
"Katyusha" is a Soviet-era song composed by Matvey Blanter in 1938, with Russian words by the Soviet poet Mikhail Isakovsky. It tells of a young woman, Katyusha (a tender form of the name Ekaterina), and a soldier serving far away. Its first official performance was given by Valentina Batishcheva in the autumn of 1938, at a concert of the State Jazz Orchestra in the Column Hall of Moscow's House of the Unions.

The song's popularity grew after Germany launched Operation Barbarossa against the Soviet Union. Female students of a Soviet industrial school in Moscow sang it as they said goodbye to soldiers leaving for the front against Nazi Germany. As many Soviet men left wives and girlfriends to serve in the Soviet Army during the Second World War, known in Russia as the Great Patriotic War, its theme matched what many were living through, and it became a patriotic song that encouraged people to serve and defend their land. The Red Army's BM-8, BM-13 and BM-31 rocket launchers took their nickname, "Katyusha", from it.

The melody travelled with new words. In 1943 the Kingdom of Italy signed the Armistice of Cassibile and, after the German Operation Achse, joined the Allies; in the next two years Felice Cascione wrote Italian lyrics to the tune, and his "Fischia il vento" (The Wind Blows) became one of the best-known songs of the Italian partisans who fought German forces and the remaining Italian fascists, alongside "Bella Ciao". On the other side, the Spanish Blue Division used the melody in the last battles on the Eastern Front for "Primavera" (Spring), a chant praising Francoist fighters. In Greece, partisans who had fought the German invasion of 1941 made their own version, "The Hymn of EAM", with words by Vassilis Rotas, in the years of the Greek Civil War (1946–1949). It was recorded much later by Thanos Mikroutsikos and sung by Maria Dimitriadi.

Soviet influence in the second half of the 20th century carried the song to the People's Republic of China, where it is still widely sung. During a night rehearsal for the 2015 Moscow Victory Day Parade, the Chinese honour guard led by Li Bentao sang it as they marched back to their quarters, and at the parade the Massed Bands of the Moscow Garrison played it as the contingents of India, Mongolia, Serbia and China marched past. In Iceland, where schoolchildren in particular know it as "Vertu til er vorið kallar á þig" (Be Ready When Spring Calls You), Tryggvi Þorsteinsson's words urge hard work in the hayfields in spring.

Singers who have performed it include Lidia Ruslanova, Tamara Sinyavskaya, Georgi Vinogradov, Eduard Khil, Anna German, Ivan Rebroff, Dmitri Hvorostovsky and Joseph Kobzon, and it is part of the repertoire of the Alexandrov Ensemble. It is heard briefly in the 1961 Japanese film A Soldier's Prayer, the last part of The Human Condition trilogy.

A wartime love song of parting and loyalty, tender and hopeful rather than martial.
