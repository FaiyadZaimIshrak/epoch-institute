name: Norfolk four-course system
type: work (other)
dates: early 16th century

The Norfolk four-course system is a method of crop rotation in which four crops follow one another over a four-year cycle: wheat, turnips, barley, and clover or ryegrass. Unlike the earlier three-field system, it has no fallow year. It was developed in the early 16th century in Waasland, in present-day northern Belgium, and popularised in the 18th century by the British agriculturist Charles Townshend. Its fodder crop, turnips, and its grazing crop, clover, allowed livestock to be kept year-round, and it was a key development in the British Agricultural Revolution.
