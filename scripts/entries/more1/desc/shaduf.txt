name: Shadoof
type: work (artifact)
dates: c. 2200 BCE

The shadoof is a tool for lifting water from a well, river or pond onto land or into a channel: a long pole pivoted on an upright frame, with a bucket at one end and a counterweight of clay or stone at the other. The earliest evidence of it is a cylinder seal from Mesopotamia of about 2200 BCE; it appeared in Upper Egypt sometime after 2000 BCE and reached China around the same time. One man can lift 39 to 130 litres a minute with it, and it is still used for irrigation in Africa, Asia and parts of Europe.
