Batch a: 12 entries written, none left out. All "dates" are "" (no article gives a plain founding date for the observance).

saturnalia: the article writes BC; given as BCE. Type idea/concept (a festival). "may have influenced" later midwinter customs: the article's hedge, kept.
yule: dates left "": the earliest record (a Gothic calendar in the Codex Ambrosianus, c. 350 CE) is in the desc; the editor may prefer it as a date. The Hákon the Good law is given "according to Hákonar saga góða", as the article does. aka Jól and Ġēol are the Old Norse and Old English forms the article gives.
dongzhi: the article is both the solar term and the festival; the desc says both. "First celebrated during the Zhou dynasty (1045 BCE–256 BCE)" and the Han dates are the article's own, unsourced there. "around 21 to 23 December": the article says "usually around December 21 to 23".
yalda-night: left out the article's claim that the night was in the Achaemenid official calendar "from at least 502 BCE under Darius I" (stated flatly, looked doubtful) and the claim that Omar Khayyam "founded" the Iranian calendar. Name written "Yalda Night" as the article title; the lead spells it "Yaldā Night" (in aka). "Shab-e Yalda"/"Shab-e Chilla" are in the article.
christmas: the article's dates are month-first ("December 25"); written day-first in the desc. "Puritans prohibited … 1647 to 1660": the article says "prohibited by Puritans during their ascendency in the Commonwealth of England (1647–1660)". The article also says "in the early fourth century, the church fixed the date as December 25" and that Pope Julius I formally set it; I kept only the Chronograph of 354 / 336 statement. aka "Noel" and "Xmas" are from the etymology section.
advent: "existed from about 480" is the article's "certainly in existence from about 480". The article also reports Gregory of Tours' account (Bishop Perpetuus, fifth century) of the St Martin's fast; I gave only that the practice "remained limited to the diocese of Tours until the sixth century".
epiphany-holiday: name "Epiphany" (bracket dropped), slug kept. "361, by Ammianus Marcellinus" is the article's "AD 361". Only the lead and early history were read of this long article (79 KB).
koliada: thin on history; the article's sections for Belarus, Poland and Russia are empty. Type idea/concept; it is as much a term (the Slavic name of the season). aka "Kolęda" is the Old Church Slavonic/Polish form, "Kolenda" the Croatian/Old Polish.
sol-invictus: type idea/deity. The article gives both views (Syrian god v. one continuous Roman cult) and hedges the founding of the Dies Natalis festival by Aurelian ("according to some scholars"; Hijmans doubts it): both hedges kept. "25 December 274" (the temple's dedication) stands in the desc, not in "dates".
saint-lucys-day: "early-fourth-century virgin martyr" is the article's; it also gives her death as 13 December 304, not used. The candle wreath is "according to legend", as the article says. aka "St. Lucy's Day" is the article's shortened form.
hogmanay: the article's Origins section is short and speculative ("It is speculated that …"): hedge kept. No founding date; 1443 (Latin, West Riding of Yorkshire) and 1604 (Scots, Elgin) are first records of the word, not of the custom.
twelfth-night-holiday: name "Twelfth Night", slug kept. The Council of Tours statement is given in the article twice, once as a quotation of Bruce Forbes. The bean-and-pea custom is described for medieval and Tudor England; written in the past tense.
